This lesson takes it apart: where side goes on the white, how it mixes with follow and screw, <strong>squirt</strong> and <strong>swerve</strong> one at a time and together, how pace changes them, <strong>throw</strong> on the object ball, what side does at the cushion, two ways to aim with side, and when to leave it alone.</p>

<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Put side on the white, alone or with follow or screw, inside the safe zone.</li>
<li>See squirt and swerve separately, and know when they cancel.</li>
<li>Know how pace and a raised cue change where the white arrives.</li>
<li>Know that side throws the object ball the other way.</li>
<li>See that side does its main work <strong>at the cushion</strong>: running side and check side.</li>
<li>Aim with side two ways, and use it only when you need it.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-1-—-What-side-is"><a href="#Chapter-1-—-What-side-is" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 1 — What side is"></a>Chapter 1 — What side is</h1>
<h2 id="1-Side-the-tip-left-or-right-of-centre"><a href="#1-Side-the-tip-left-or-right-of-centre" class="headerlink" title="1 · Side: the tip left or right of centre"></a>1 · Side: the tip left or right of centre</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s05_tip.webp" alt="Two 3D close-ups from behind the white. Left: the cue tip touches the white left of centre. Right: the tip touches right of centre. A small face of the white in the corner shows the blue tip spot"></p>
<p>Move the <strong>whole cue</strong> sideways, parallel to your line, and strike left or right of centre. Keep the tip within about <strong>half-way to the edge</strong> (the safe zone, post 03). The pictures in this post use half-way side.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> don't just turn the butt to reach the side. That points the cue off the line (see section 9 for when this is done on purpose).</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="2-Side-mixes-with-follow-and-screw"><a href="#2-Side-mixes-with-follow-and-screw" class="headerlink" title="2 · Side mixes with follow and screw"></a>2 · Side mixes with follow and screw</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s05_combo.svg" alt="The white's face with nine tip spots: centre, top, bottom, left, right, and four corners. Each corner combines spin: top right is follow with right side, bottom left is screw with left side"></p>
<p>The tip can go anywhere in the safe zone. The corners mix two effects:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Top right / top left:</strong> follow, then side off the cushion.</li>
<li><strong>Bottom right / bottom left:</strong> screw, then side off the cushion.</li>
</ul>
<p>Everything below about squirt, swerve and throw applies to the corner spots too.</p>

<p>Real shots have both: squirt first (left), then swerve (right). Aimed straight at a red 2 m away with right side:</p>

<th>What wins</th>

</tr>

<td>too far left ✗</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>power 2, cue raised</td>

<td>far right ✗</td>

<h2 id="6-Pace-changes-it"><a href="#6-Pace-changes-it" class="headerlink" title="6 · Pace changes it"></a>6 · Pace changes it</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s05_pace.svg" alt="A line chart: how far left (down) or right (up) of the aiming line the white is, over 2.5 metres, with right side and a nearly level cue. Power 2 stays close to the line; power 3 ends 1.5 cm left; power 4 keeps drifting left until about 1.5 metres and ends almost 4 cm left"></p>
<p>The same right side, the same nearly level cue (5°), three paces (model):</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>

<th>Off line after 2.5 m</th>

</tr>

<p>A soft shot slides only a short way, so swerve has time to undo the squirt early. A firm shot slides further; squirt wins, and the gap stays once the white rolls. So: <strong>the harder you hit with side, the more you must allow for squirt.</strong></p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-What-side-does-to-the-ball-and-the-cushion"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-What-side-does-to-the-ball-and-the-cushion" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — What side does to the ball and the cushion"></a>Chapter 3 — What side does to the ball and the cushion</h1>
<h2 id="7-Throw-side-pushes-the-object-ball-the-other-way"><a href="#7-Throw-side-pushes-the-object-ball-the-other-way" class="headerlink" title="7 · Throw: side pushes the object ball the other way"></a>7 · Throw: side pushes the object ball the other way</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b05_throw_side.webp" alt="Two top views of a full-ball hit on a red. Right side: the red goes a little to the left of the straight line, about 3 cm after a metre. Left side: the red goes a little to the right"></p>
<p>At contact, the spinning white rubs the red. <strong>Right side throws the red left; left side throws it right</strong>, here about 3 cm over a metre on a slow full-ball hit.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> this is how good players fix a slow cut that keeps going in fat (post 02): a little side that throws the ball towards the pocket.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="8-Side-waits-for-the-cushion"><a href="#8-Side-waits-for-the-cushion" class="headerlink" title="8 · Side waits for the cushion"></a>8 · Side waits for the cushion</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s05_cushion.webp" alt="Top view: a white rolls up to the top cushion along one line. Three tracks leave the cushion: right side comes off flatter at 34 degrees to the cushion, no side at 57 degrees, left side steeper at 68 degrees"></p>
<p>Before the cushion, side changes little (once you allow for squirt; in this picture squirt is switched off so all three take the same path in). <strong>At the cushion</strong>, the spin grips and changes the angle. Coming in at 63° to the cushion, power 2 (model):</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Tip</th>
<th>Comes off at (to the cushion)</th>
<th>Name here</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>no side</td>
<td>57°</td>
<td></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>right side</td>
<td>34°: flatter, runs on along the cushion</td>
<td><strong>running side</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>left side</td>
<td>68°: steeper, &quot;held&quot; back</td>
<td><strong>check side</strong></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p><strong>Running side</strong> is the side that keeps the white going the way it was travelling along the cushion. <strong>Check side</strong> is the other one. Which is left or right depends on the shot. Post 07 uses this a lot.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-4-—-Aiming-with-side"><a href="#Chapter-4-—-Aiming-with-side" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 4 — Aiming with side"></a>Chapter 4 — Aiming with side</h1>
<h2 id="9-Two-ways-to-aim-with-side"><a href="#9-Two-ways-to-aim-with-side" class="headerlink" title="9 · Two ways to aim with side"></a>9 · Two ways to aim with side</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b05_aim.webp" alt="Two top views of the white with right side. 1: the cue moved sideways, parallel to the aiming line; the white goes off the line to the left, cross. 2: the cue pivoted about a point behind the white; the white goes along the line, tick"></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Aim, then adjust.</strong> Line up as normal, move the cue sideways for side, then turn your aim a touch <strong>towards the side</strong> you use. How much depends on your cue and the pace; learn it by practice.</li>
<li><strong>Pivot the cue.</strong> Line up centre-ball, then turn only the back of the cue about a point on the cue behind the white. The cue now points slightly towards the side, and squirt brings the white back onto the line. It cancels squirt exactly when that point is at your cue's <strong>pivot length</strong> behind the tip (about 30 cm for the model cue here).</li>
</ol>
<p>Neither is &quot;right&quot;. Method 1 is easier to learn; method 2 is easier to repeat once you know your cue.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="10-Watch-it-a-long-pot-with-side"><a href="#10-Watch-it-a-long-pot-with-side" class="headerlink" title="10 · Watch it: a long pot with side"></a>10 · Watch it: a long pot with side</h2>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b05_clip_side_miss.webp" alt="Clip: a long straight red into the far corner, played with right side and aimed as if there were no side. The white arrives slightly off and the red misses the pocket"></div>
<p>Right side, power 3, aimed as if there were no side: the white arrives a little off, and over a long pot that is enough to miss ✗.</p>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b05_clip_side_fix.webp" alt="Clip: the same shot with the aim turned a touch to the right. The red goes into the pocket"></div>
<p>The same shot with the aim turned a touch to the right (less than a degree): it goes in ✓.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Common pitfall:</strong> &quot;I put side on to get position and missed the pot.&quot; Most misses with side are long pots. Use it on long pots only when you must.</p>
</blockquote>
