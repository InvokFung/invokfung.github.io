<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Find the <strong>square line</strong> on any cut, and send the white along it with a stun.</li>
<li>Send the white a little forward (stun run-through) or about 30° forward (rolling).</li>
<li>Use power and the cut angle to predict how follow and screw bend the white.</li>
<li>Know how much speed the white keeps on full, half and thin cuts.</li>
<li>Check the white's path for a pocket (in-off) and for other balls (kisses).</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="3-Rolling-white-about-30"><a href="#3-Rolling-white-about-30" class="headerlink" title="3 · Rolling white: about 30°"></a>3 · Rolling white: about 30°</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b03_rule.webp" alt="Three top views: three-quarter ball, half ball and quarter ball cuts with a rolling white. In each, the white's track bends and then runs straight. A yellow wedge between the white's old line and its new line is about the same size in all three"></p>
<p>When the white is <strong>rolling</strong> (hit above centre, or slow from far away), it ends up turned about <strong>30°</strong>, a third of a right angle, from the line you sent it on. That holds for most cuts from ¾ ball to ¼ ball. In the model: ¾ ball 28°, ½ ball 34°, ¼ ball 27°.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> lay your cue on the table along the white's line, then turn it a third of the way to a right angle. That is where a rolling white goes.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>Low tip: the white leaves along the square line too, then the backspin pulls it <strong>back</strong> behind the line.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="6-Power-decides-where-the-bend-starts"><a href="#6-Power-decides-where-the-bend-starts" class="headerlink" title="6 · Power decides where the bend starts"></a>6 · Power decides where the bend starts</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s04_power_bend.webp" alt="Two top views of the same half-ball cut. Left, follow: at power 2 the white bends forwards almost at once; at power 4 it first runs a long way along the square line, then curves. Right, screw: at power 2 it bends back almost at once over a short distance; at power 4 it runs along the square line first and swings back in a wide curve"></p>
<p>Every white leaves along the square line. The spin then takes time to grip. More pace = further along the square line before the bend (model, distance until the white has turned 15°):</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th></th>
<th>Power 2</th>
<th>Power 4</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>follow</td>
<td>4 cm</td>
<td>39 cm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>screw</td>
<td>1 cm</td>
<td>36 cm</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>So the same tip spot gives very different paths. A <strong>soft</strong> shot turns at once; a <strong>firm</strong> shot keeps the square line for a while, then swings in a wide curve.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> a firm screw on a cut does not come straight back. It runs along the square line first. Check that part of the path too.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="7-The-cut-decides-how-much-screw-comes-back"><a href="#7-The-cut-decides-how-much-screw-comes-back" class="headerlink" title="7 · The cut decides how much screw comes back"></a>7 · The cut decides how much screw comes back</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s04_screw_cut.webp" alt="Three top views with the same low tip and power 3: three-quarter, half and quarter ball cuts. On the three-quarter ball the white comes back well behind the square line, 62 degrees; on half ball 40 degrees; on quarter ball only 23 degrees"></p>
<p>The same screw (tip 0.45 R below centre, power 3) on three cuts:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Cut</th>
<th>The white ends behind the square line by</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>¾ ball</td>
<td>62°</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>½ ball</td>
<td>40°</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>¼ ball</td>
<td>23°</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The fuller the hit, the more the screw shows. On thin cuts the white keeps most of its forward speed (section 9), so screw only nudges it.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="8-Nearly-full-cuts"><a href="#8-Nearly-full-cuts" class="headerlink" title="8 · Nearly full cuts"></a>8 · Nearly full cuts</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s04_full.webp" alt="Top view of a seven-eighths ball cut. The yellow follow track runs on almost straight past the red, 17 degrees off the cue line; the blue screw track comes back almost straight towards the start, 26 degrees off"></p>
<p>On a nearly straight pot (here ⅞ ball, a 7° cut), the white goes almost <strong>straight on</strong> with follow or almost <strong>straight back</strong> with screw. In the model, follow ended 17° off the cue line and screw 26° off it.</p>
<p>This is why nearly straight pots are hard for position: the white can only go forward or back along one line. Leave yourself <strong>a bit of angle</strong> when you can (post 12).</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="9-How-much-speed-the-white-keeps"><a href="#9-How-much-speed-the-white-keeps" class="headerlink" title="9 · How much speed the white keeps"></a>9 · How much speed the white keeps</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s04_speed_cut.webp" alt="Three top views with a stun at power 3: three-quarter, half and one-eighth ball cuts. Labels: the white keeps 25 percent of its speed on three-quarter ball, 50 percent on half ball, 87 percent on one-eighth ball"></p>
<p>On a stun, the speed is split between the two balls:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Cut</th>
<th>The white keeps</th>
<th>The red gets</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>¾ ball</td>
<td>25%</td>
<td>97%</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>½ ball</td>
<td>50%</td>
<td>87%</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>⅛ ball</td>
<td>87%</td>
<td>48%</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>(The two shares are the sine and cosine of the cut angle; they add up in speed squared, not in plain numbers.)</p>
<p>So on a <strong>thin cut</strong>, the white travels a long way and the red only a little: play thin cuts <strong>firmer</strong> to reach the pocket, and expect the white to run on. On a <strong>full</strong> hit, the white goes only a short way.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-Check-the-whites-path"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-Check-the-whites-path" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — Check the white's path"></a>Chapter 3 — Check the white's path</h1>
<h2 id="10-Avoid-the-in-off"><a href="#10-Avoid-the-in-off" class="headerlink" title="10 · Avoid the in-off"></a>10 · Avoid the in-off</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s04_inoff.webp" alt="Three top views of the same half-ball pot into the top-right corner. Stun: the white runs along the square line straight into the bottom-right corner pocket, cross. Follow: the white bends forwards, hits a cushion and stays on the table, tick. Screw: the white comes back and stays out, tick"></p>
<p>Here the square line points <strong>straight at a pocket</strong>. A stun sends the white in after the red: an in-off, a foul ✗. Follow or screw move the white off the line ✓.</p>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/s04_clip_inoff.webp" alt="Clip: the half-ball pot into the top-right corner with a stun. The square line is drawn dashed from the contact point to the bottom-right corner pocket. The red goes in, and the white runs along the square line into the bottom-right corner"></div>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> before every cut, look along the square line. If it hits a pocket, choose follow or screw, or change the pace so the white stops short.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="11-Check-for-kisses"><a href="#11-Check-for-kisses" class="headerlink" title="11 · Check for kisses"></a>11 · Check for kisses</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s04_kiss.webp" alt="Two top views of the half-ball pot with a blue ball half a metre along the square line. Stun: the white runs along the square line and hits the blue, cross. Screw: the white bends back and misses the blue, tick"></p>
<p>A ball on the white's path changes everything: the white stops, the other ball moves, and your next shot is gone. Here a blue sits 52 cm along the square line. The stun <strong>hits</strong> it ✗; screw takes the white <strong>behind</strong> it ✓.</p>
<p>The check is the same as for in-offs: picture the path for your tip and pace, and look along all of it.</p>
