Billiards & Snooker — 04 · Where the White Goes

Created2026-07-25|Updated2026-07-25|Sports > Billiards
|Post View:

Post 03 controlled the white on a straight pot. Most pots are cuts, and on a cut the white goes off to the side. This lesson shows where: the square line, the 30° rule, follow and screw on a cut, how power and the cut angle change the path, how much speed the white keeps, and two checks before every shot: in-offs and kisses.

Learning Outcome

  • Find the square line on any cut, and send the white along it with a stun.
  • Send the white a little forward (stun run-through) or about 30° forward (rolling).
  • Use power and the cut angle to predict how follow and screw bend the white.
  • Know how much speed the white keeps on full, half and thin cuts.
  • Check the white's path for a pocket (in-off) and for other balls (kisses).

Chapter 1 — The square line

1 · Three tips, three paths

The same pot, the same aim, three tips:

Tip The white goes…
▲ above centre (follow) forwards, bending away from the square line
■ a little below (stun) along the square line, at a right angle to the red's path
▼ low (screw) back, behind the square line

The square line (the small white square) is your anchor. Find it first, then decide: forwards of it, on it, or behind it.

2 · Stun run-through on a cut

Between stun and follow there is a whole range. In the model, half ball, power 3, white 40 cm away:

Tip The white ends…
0.19 R below centre (stun) on the square line
0.04 R below centre (stun run-through) 15° forward of it
0.35 R above centre (rolling) 26° forward of it

Trick: need the white just a little forward of the square line, for a red a few inches from it? Stun run-through: hit just below centre, a little higher than your stun spot.

3 · Rolling white: about 30°

When the white is rolling (hit above centre, or slow from far away), it ends up turned about 30°, a third of a right angle, from the line you sent it on. That holds for most cuts from ¾ ball to ¼ ball. In the model: ¾ ball 28°, ½ ball 34°, ¼ ball 27°.

Trick: lay your cue on the table along the white's line, then turn it a third of the way to a right angle. That is where a rolling white goes.

Chapter 2 — Follow and screw on a cut

4 · Watch it: follow on a cut

The white starts along the square line, then the topspin grips and bends it forwards.

5 · Watch it: screw on a cut

Low tip: the white leaves along the square line too, then the backspin pulls it back behind the line.

6 · Power decides where the bend starts

Every white leaves along the square line. The spin then takes time to grip. More pace = further along the square line before the bend (model, distance until the white has turned 15°):

Power 2 Power 4
follow 4 cm 39 cm
screw 1 cm 36 cm

So the same tip spot gives very different paths. A soft shot turns at once; a firm shot keeps the square line for a while, then swings in a wide curve.

Watch out: a firm screw on a cut does not come straight back. It runs along the square line first. Check that part of the path too.

7 · The cut decides how much screw comes back

The same screw (tip 0.45 R below centre, power 3) on three cuts:

Cut The white ends behind the square line by
¾ ball 62°
½ ball 40°
¼ ball 23°

The fuller the hit, the more the screw shows. On thin cuts the white keeps most of its forward speed (section 9), so screw only nudges it.

8 · Nearly full cuts

On a nearly straight pot (here ⅞ ball, a 7° cut), the white goes almost straight on with follow or almost straight back with screw. In the model, follow ended 17° off the cue line and screw 26° off it.

This is why nearly straight pots are hard for position: the white can only go forward or back along one line. Leave yourself a bit of angle when you can (post 12).

9 · How much speed the white keeps

On a stun, the speed is split between the two balls:

Cut The white keeps The red gets
¾ ball 25% 97%
½ ball 50% 87%
⅛ ball 87% 48%

(The two shares are the sine and cosine of the cut angle; they add up in speed squared, not in plain numbers.)

So on a thin cut, the white travels a long way and the red only a little: play thin cuts firmer to reach the pocket, and expect the white to run on. On a full hit, the white goes only a short way.

Chapter 3 — Check the white's path

10 · Avoid the in-off

Here the square line points straight at a pocket. A stun sends the white in after the red: an in-off, a foul ✗. Follow or screw move the white off the line ✓.

Trick: before every cut, look along the square line. If it hits a pocket, choose follow or screw, or change the pace so the white stops short.

11 · Check for kisses

A ball on the white's path changes everything: the white stops, the other ball moves, and your next shot is gone. Here a blue sits 52 cm along the square line. The stun hits it ✗; screw takes the white behind it ✓.

The check is the same as for in-offs: picture the path for your tip and pace, and look along all of it.

Try it at the table

Drill Set-up Look for
Square line a half-ball pot; lay a cue on the table along the square line stun: the white runs along the cue
Four paths the same pot with screw, stun, stun run-through, follow four different landing spots; mark each with a coin
30° check top spin on ¾, ½ and ¼ ball cuts the white turns about the same each time
Power and the bend follow at power 2, then at power 4 the firm one runs along the square line first
In-off spotter a pot whose square line points at a pocket play it with follow, then with screw, and stay out both times

Cheat-sheet
Stun along the square line, at a right angle to the red's path
Stun run-through just below centre: a little forward of the square line (about 15° on half ball)
Follow forwards; ends about 30° off the line you sent the white on
Screw behind the square line; more on fuller cuts (¾: 62°, ½: 40°, ¼: 23°)
Power firmer = runs along the square line longer before bending
Nearly full only straight on or straight back
Speed kept ¾: 25% · ½: 50% · ⅛: 87%
Before every shot look along the white's path: pockets, balls
