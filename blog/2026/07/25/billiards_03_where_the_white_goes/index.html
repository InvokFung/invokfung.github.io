Billiards & Snooker — 03 · Where the White Goes After a Cut

Created2026-07-25|Updated2026-07-25|Sports > Billiards
|Post View:

On a straight pot the white stops, follows or comes back. On a cut, it also goes sideways. The good news: where it goes follows three simple patterns, one for each tip spot from post 02. Learn these and you can predict the white before you play.

Series map. ★ Basics: 00 Aiming → 01 The hit → 02 Stop, follow, screw → 03 Where the white goes (this post) · ★★ Club: 04 Thin cuts → 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker · ★★★ Advanced: 10 Plants and sets → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → 13 Position · ★★★★ Hard: 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → 15 Swerve and special spots.

Learning Outcome

  • Find the square line (the stun line) on any cut.
  • Know where a rolling white goes (the "30° rule").
  • Use screw to bring the white back behind the square line.

1 · Three tips, three paths

The same pot, the same aim, three tips:

Tip The white goes…
▲ above centre (follow) forwards, bending away from the square line
■ a little below (stun) along the square line, at a right angle to the red's path
▼ low (screw) back, behind the square line

The square line (the small white square in the picture) is your anchor. Find it first, then decide: forwards of it, on it, or behind it.

2 · Rolling white: about 30°

When the white is rolling (hit above centre, or a slow shot from far away), it ends up turned by about 30°, a third of a right angle, from where it was going. That holds for most cuts from ¾ ball to ¼ ball. In the model: ¾ ball 28°, ½ ball 34°, ¼ ball 27°.

Trick: hold your hand flat on the table along the white's line, then turn it a third of the way to a right angle. That is where a rolling white goes.

3 · Watch it: follow on a cut

Watch the white: it starts along the square line, then the topspin grips and bends it forwards. A softer shot bends sooner; a firmer one runs further along the square line first.

4 · Watch it: screw on a cut

Low tip: the backspin pulls the white back behind the square line. More screw, or a thicker cut, brings it back further.

Watch out: on thin cuts, screw does little. The white keeps most of its speed and runs on. Post 04 shows why.

5 · Try it at the table

Drill Set-up Look for
Square line a half-ball pot; lay a cue on the table along the square line stun: the white runs along the cue
Three paths the same pot with top, stun and screw three different landing spots; mark each with a coin
30° check top spin on ¾, ½ and ¼ ball cuts the white turns about the same each time

Cheat-sheet
Stun along the square line, at a right angle to the red's path
Follow forwards of the square line; ends about 30° off the white's first line
Screw behind the square line
Softer the curve starts sooner
Thin cut the white keeps its speed; spin matters less
