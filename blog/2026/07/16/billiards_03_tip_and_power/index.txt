<h2 id="2-Stay-inside-the-safe-zone"><a href="#2-Stay-inside-the-safe-zone" class="headerlink" title="2 · Stay inside the safe zone"></a>2 · Stay inside the safe zone</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b01_safe.svg" alt="The white seen from behind. A green inner circle, half the size of the ball, is the safe zone; blue tip spots inside it are fine, red spots near the edge are miscues"></p>
<p>Keep the tip inside the <strong>green circle</strong>, about half-way from the centre to the edge. Further out, the tip slides off the ball (a <strong>miscue</strong>) and the white goes anywhere.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> chalk before every shot. Most miscues are a dry tip, not a bad spot.</p>
</blockquote>

<h2 id="3-Low-shots-keep-the-cue-level"><a href="#3-Low-shots-keep-the-cue-level" class="headerlink" title="3 · Low shots: keep the cue level"></a>3 · Low shots: keep the cue level</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s03_level.webp" alt="Two 3D side views of a screw shot, the tip low on the white. Left: the cue almost flat, a thin wedge shows about 3 degrees, tick. Right: the butt is raised, a bigger wedge shows 15 degrees, cross"></p>
<p>To hit low, <strong>lower the whole cue</strong> (post 01, tip height) and keep it nearly flat, about 3° ✓. Raising the butt to reach the bottom of the white ✗ makes the tip strike down into the ball.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> a raised cue plus even a little side makes the white curve (swerve, post 14). On low shots that curve is a common, hidden cause of missed pots.</p>
</blockquote>

<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> before a shot, picture the white's journey and pick the number. Then play that number, not &quot;hard&quot; or &quot;soft&quot;.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="5-Watch-it-a-straight-pot-centre-ball"><a href="#5-Watch-it-a-straight-pot-centre-ball" class="headerlink" title="5 · Watch it: a straight pot, centre ball"></a>5 · Watch it: a straight pot, centre ball</h2>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b01_clip_straight.webp" alt="Clip: a straight red into the corner pocket. Tip in the centre, power 2. The cue moves back and forth, goes straight through, the red goes in and the white rolls a little after it"></div>
<p>Centre, power 2, cue straight through. The red drops ✓ and the white follows it a little way. Why it follows is the next picture.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="6-Where-the-white-starts-to-roll"><a href="#6-Where-the-white-starts-to-roll" class="headerlink" title="6 · Where the white starts to roll"></a>6 · Where the white starts to roll</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s03_roll.svg" alt="Five rows, one per tip height, power 3. A bar shows the white's first 120 cm: blue while it still has backspin, amber while it slides, green once it rolls. High: rolls at once. Centre: slides 58 cm. Low: backspin to 88 cm, rolls from 103 cm"></p>
<p>A struck white first <strong>slides</strong>. The cloth then rubs it into a natural <strong>roll</strong>. Where that happens depends on the tip height (model, power 3):</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Tip</th>
<th>Backspin lasts to</th>
<th>Rolling from</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>high, 7/10 of the ball's height</td>
<td>none</td>
<td>at once</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>a little above centre</td>
<td>none</td>
<td>31 cm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>centre</td>
<td>none</td>
<td>58 cm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>a little below centre</td>
<td>42 cm</td>
<td>79 cm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>low</td>
<td>88 cm</td>
<td>103 cm</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>This one picture explains the next four sections:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Stop</strong> = the white reaches the red while it has <strong>no spin</strong>: the moment the blue part ends.</li>
<li><strong>Screw</strong> = it reaches the red while still <strong>blue</strong> (backspin).</li>
<li><strong>Follow</strong> = it reaches the red while <strong>green</strong> (rolling).</li>
<li>A <strong>centre</strong> hit on a 2 m pot is rolling long before it arrives, so it follows.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-Stop-follow-screw-and-the-in-betweens"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-Stop-follow-screw-and-the-in-betweens" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — Stop, follow, screw and the in-betweens"></a>Chapter 3 — Stop, follow, screw and the in-betweens</h1>
<h2 id="7-Stop-the-white-stays-where-it-hits"><a href="#7-Stop-the-white-stays-where-it-hits" class="headerlink" title="7 · Stop: the white stays where it hits"></a>7 · Stop: the white stays where it hits</h2>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b02_clip_stop.webp" alt="Clip: straight red into the middle pocket. Tip a little below centre, power 3. The red goes in and the white stops exactly where it touched the red, marked with a yellow ring"></div>
<p>A little <strong>below centre</strong>, power 3, the white 50 cm from the red. It arrives with no spin left, hands all its speed to the red and stops ✓.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="8-Follow-the-white-runs-on"><a href="#8-Follow-the-white-runs-on" class="headerlink" title="8 · Follow: the white runs on"></a>8 · Follow: the white runs on</h2>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b02_clip_follow.webp" alt="Clip: the same pot. Tip above centre, power 2. The red goes in and the white rolls on after it, about a third of a metre"></div>
<p><strong>Above centre</strong>, power 2. The white is rolling when it hits, so it keeps going after the red, here about a third of a metre.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> too much follow on a straight pot and the white follows the red into the pocket (an <strong>in-off</strong>, a foul).</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="9-Screw-the-white-comes-back"><a href="#9-Screw-the-white-comes-back" class="headerlink" title="9 · Screw: the white comes back"></a>9 · Screw: the white comes back</h2>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b02_clip_screw.webp" alt="Clip: the same pot. Tip low, power 3. The red goes in and the white spins back towards the player, about 60 centimetres"></div>
<p><strong>Low</strong>, power 3, and let the cue go <strong>through</strong> the white. The backspin is still on when it hits, so the white comes back, here about 60 cm.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="10-Stun-run-through-a-few-centimetres-on"><a href="#10-Stun-run-through-a-few-centimetres-on" class="headerlink" title="10 · Stun run-through: a few centimetres on"></a>10 · Stun run-through: a few centimetres on</h2>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/s03_clip_stun_run.webp" alt="Clip: the same straight pot into the middle pocket. Tip just below centre, power 3. The red goes in and the white runs on 16 centimetres past where it touched, marked with a yellow ring; a faint ghost ball marks the contact"></div>
<p>Between stop and follow. The tip goes <strong>just below centre</strong>, a little higher than for the stop. The white arrives with a touch of forward roll and creeps on, here <strong>16 cm</strong> (model: stop at 0.21 R below centre, run-through at 0.09 R below).</p>
<p>Use it when you need the white <strong>a short way forward</strong> with a firm, straight-feeling hit. A soft follow over the same distance would be more affected by the cloth.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="11-Further-away-Hit-lower-or-firmer"><a href="#11-Further-away-Hit-lower-or-firmer" class="headerlink" title="11 · Further away? Hit lower, or firmer"></a>11 · Further away? Hit lower, or firmer</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b02_distance.webp" alt="Three top views with the same red. The white is 30 cm, 80 cm and 145 cm away. Next to each is the white's face: to stop the white the tip is just below centre at 30 cm, clearly lower at 80 cm, and at 145 cm power 4 with the tip moderately low"></p>
<p>Backspin wears off on the way (section 6). So to <strong>stop</strong> the white:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Distance</th>
<th>Tip</th>
<th>Power</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>short (30 cm)</td>
<td>just below centre</td>
<td>3</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>medium (80 cm)</td>
<td>clearly lower</td>
<td>3</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>long (1.5 m)</td>
<td>low</td>
<td>4 (more power keeps the spin on)</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<hr>
<h2 id="12-More-power-more-screw"><a href="#12-More-power-more-screw" class="headerlink" title="12 · More power, more screw"></a>12 · More power, more screw</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b02_screw_power.webp" alt="Two top views of the screw shot with the same low tip. Power 3: the white comes back about 60 cm and stops, tick. Power 4: the white comes back much further and drops into the middle pocket behind, cross"></p>
<p>The same low tip: power 3 brings the white back about 60 cm ✓. Power 4 brings it back more than a metre, here into the pocket behind ✗.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Common pitfall:</strong> &quot;My screw doesn't work.&quot; Usually the tip is not low enough, or the cue stops at the ball. Let it go through.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="13-Screw-from-long-range"><a href="#13-Screw-from-long-range" class="headerlink" title="13 · Screw from long range"></a>13 · Screw from long range</h2>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/s03_clip_long_screw.webp" alt="Clip: a straight red near the top middle pocket, the white 1.25 metres behind it. Tip low, power 4. The red goes in and the white screws back about 38 centimetres"></div>
<p>The white is 1.25 m from the red. In the model:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Power</th>
<th>To bring the white back 40 cm</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>3</td>
<td>not possible: even the lowest safe tip (0.5 R) runs on about 20 cm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>4</td>
<td>tip 0.34 R below centre: back about 38 cm ✓</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>So for long screw, <strong>power comes first</strong>: the white must still be blue (backspin) when it arrives. Then set the distance with the tip height. Small changes matter: at power 4 the full 0.5 R low sent the white back almost 1.5 m, into the far pocket.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="14-The-drag-shot-hit-firmer-arrive-slowly"><a href="#14-The-drag-shot-hit-firmer-arrive-slowly" class="headerlink" title="14 · The drag shot: hit firmer, arrive slowly"></a>14 · The drag shot: hit firmer, arrive slowly</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s03_drag.svg" alt="A line chart of the white's speed over its first 2 metres. Grey: centre ball, starts at 1.2 metres a second and slows evenly. Blue: low, starts much faster, slows quickly while the backspin wears off, then rolls. Both reach the red at the same slow speed"></p>
<p>You want the white to reach a ball 2 m away <strong>slowly</strong>, at 0.55 m/s. Two ways (model):</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th></th>
<th>Starts at</th>
<th>Time to arrive</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>centre ball, soft</td>
<td>1.20 m/s</td>
<td>2.8 s</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>drag</strong>: low, firmer</td>
<td>2.07 m/s</td>
<td>2.3 s</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The drag shot loses its extra speed while the backspin wears off. From 96 cm on, the two shots are the same: both roll at the same speed and reach the red at 0.55 m/s. The drag shot just gets there sooner.</p>
<p>Why play it (coaching advice): a firm, normal-feeling stroke is easier to keep straight than a very soft push, and a fast-moving white is less pushed off line by the cloth than a slow one.</p>
