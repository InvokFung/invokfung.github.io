Billiards & Snooker — 02 · Stop, Follow and Screw

Created2026-07-21|Updated2026-07-21|Sports > Billiards
|Post View:

On a straight pot you can make the white stop, follow the ball, or come back towards you. It is the same aim each time; only the tip spot and the power change. This is the first real control of the white, and the start of position play.

Series map. ★ Basics: 00 Aiming → 01 The hit → 02 Stop, follow, screw (this post) → 03 Where the white goes · ★★ Club: 04 Thin cuts → 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker · ★★★ Advanced: 10 Plants and sets → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → 13 Position · ★★★★ Hard: 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → 15 Swerve and special spots.

Learning Outcome

  • Stop the white dead on a straight pot.
  • Make it follow through, or screw it back.
  • Know why a longer shot needs a lower tip or more power.

1 · Stop: the white stays where it hits

A little below centre, power 3. The white arrives still sliding, with no spin left, so it hands all its speed to the red and stops ✓.

2 · Follow: the white runs on

Above centre, power 2. The white is rolling forwards when it hits, so it keeps going after the red, here about a third of a metre.

Watch out: too much follow on a straight pot and the white follows the red into the pocket (an in-off, a foul).

3 · Screw: the white comes back

Low, power 3, and let the cue go through the white. The backspin is still on when it hits, so the white comes back, here about 60 cm.

Trick: keep the cue level and low. A raised cue on screw shots gives curve and miscues.

4 · Further away? Hit lower, or firmer

Backspin wears off while the white slides on the cloth. So to stop the white:

Distance Tip Power
short (30 cm) just below centre 3
medium (80 cm) clearly lower 3
long (1.5 m) low 4 (more power keeps the spin on)

The same idea works for screw: a longer shot needs a lower tip, more power, or both.

5 · More power, more screw

The same low tip: power 3 brings the white back about 60 cm ✓. Power 4 brings it back more than a metre. Here that is too much: it runs into the pocket behind ✗.

Common pitfall: "My screw doesn't work." Usually the tip is not low enough, or the cue stops at the ball. Let it go through.

6 · Try it at the table

Drill Set-up Look for
Stop ladder straight blue into a middle pocket, white 30 cm, then 60, then 90 cm away the white stops dead every time
Follow and screw the same shot follow 30 cm, then screw 30 cm, then screw 60 cm
Mark the spot put a coin where the white should stop land on the coin

Cheat-sheet

Want Tip Power
Stop (straight) a little below centre medium (3)
Follow above centre gentle to medium (2–3)
Screw low (inside the safe zone) medium to firm (3–4)
Longer shot lower tip, or more power

What's next

References

  • Dr. Dave Alciatore, "stun, follow and draw" technical proofs (sliding to rolling, effect of distance on draw). https://billiards.colostate.edu/
  • WPBSA, Official Rules (2022): in-off is a foul. https://wpbsa.com/rules/
  • The clips come from a physics model of the balls (sliding, rolling, spin) at snooker sizes. Exact tip heights depend on the cloth and the cue; the pattern (longer → lower or firmer) does not.
