<h2 id="1-Three-steps-from-the-pocket-back"><a href="#1-Three-steps-from-the-pocket-back" class="headerlink" title="1 · Three steps, from the pocket back"></a>1 · Three steps, from the pocket back</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b00_steps.webp" alt="Three top-down pictures. 1: a dashed line from the pocket through the yellow ball. 2: a red dot where the line leaves the back of the yellow. 3: a see-through ghost ball touching the red dot, the white arrow aimed at its centre, and a green wedge showing the cut"></p>
<ol>
<li>Line from the pocket through the middle of the ball.</li>
<li>Where that line comes out of the back: the <strong>red dot</strong>.</li>
<li>A ball touching the red dot: the <strong>ghost ball</strong>. Send the white's centre to the ghost's centre.</li>

<p>The red dot never moves when the white moves. Only the cut changes.</p>

<h2 id="2-What-you-see-down-the-cue"><a href="#2-What-you-see-down-the-cue" class="headerlink" title="2 · What you see down the cue"></a>2 · What you see down the cue</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b00_aim_eye.webp" alt="Player's eye view along the cue: the white ball in front, the yellow ahead, the see-through ghost ball beside it with a white cross on its centre"></p>
<p>Aim at the <strong>+</strong>, the ghost's centre. It sits on empty cloth, half a ball beyond the red dot. Pick a speck on the cloth there and aim at it.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> walk in behind the ball first, looking at the pocket. Find the red dot from there, then walk round to the white.</p>
</blockquote>

<h2 id="4-The-classic-miss-aiming-at-the-red-dot"><a href="#4-The-classic-miss-aiming-at-the-red-dot" class="headerlink" title="4 · The classic miss: aiming at the red dot"></a>4 · The classic miss: aiming at the red dot</h2>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b00_clip_miss.webp" alt="Clip: the white is sent straight at the red dot; it touches the yellow early, the hit is too full, and the yellow misses the pocket and hits the cushion"></div>
<p>The red dot is easy to see, so it is tempting to send the white straight at it. But the white is a ball, not a point: it touches <strong>early</strong>, the hit is too <strong>full</strong>, the yellow does not turn enough, and it misses ✗.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Common pitfall:</strong> &quot;I keep missing thick.&quot; Move the aim out to the ghost's centre: one ball-radius beyond the red dot.</p>

Top: the view down the cue, the see-through white covering all, three quarters, half, a quarter or an eighth of the yellow. Bottom: from above, the yellow leaves at 0, 14, 30, 49 and 61 degrees from the white's line"></p>
<p>Down the cue, look at how much of the object ball the white <strong>covers</strong>. That fraction sets the angle the ball leaves at:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Cover</th>
<th>Where the white's centre points</th>
<th>The ball leaves at</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>full</td>
<td>the ball's centre</td>
<td>0° (straight on)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>¾</td>
<td>halfway between centre and edge</td>
<td>about 14°</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>½</td>
<td>the ball's <strong>edge</strong></td>
<td><strong>30°</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>¼</td>
<td>half a radius outside the edge</td>
<td>about 49°</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>⅛</td>
<td>three-quarters of a radius outside the edge</td>
<td>about 61°</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The <strong>half ball</strong> is the one to learn first: the white's centre points at the ball's edge, and the ball goes off at 30°. The others are &quot;a bit fuller&quot; or &quot;a bit thinner&quot; than that picture.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> the angles are not evenly spaced. Moving the aim half a radius, from full to ¾, turns the ball 14°. Moving it only a quarter of a radius, from ¼ to ⅛, turns it another 12°. That is why thin cuts feel fussy.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="6-How-thin-is-very-thin"><a href="#6-How-thin-is-very-thin" class="headerlink" title="6 · How thin is very thin?"></a>6 · How thin is very thin?</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b04_overlap.webp" alt="Top row: the view down the cue for three thin cuts, with the see-through ghost ball touching the red from the side; labels 7 mm, 3 mm and under 1 mm show how much the white overlaps the red. Bottom row: from above, the red goes off at a wider and wider angle, shown by a yellow wedge"></p>
<p>Past ⅛, the overlap is measured in millimetres:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>The ball turns…</th>
<th>The white overlaps it by…</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>60°</td>
<td>about 7 mm (≈ ⅛ ball)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>70°</td>
<td>about 3 mm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>80°</td>
<td><strong>less than 1 mm</strong></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>So an 80° cut is a &quot;nearly miss&quot;. Leave yourself fuller pots when you can.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="7-Two-ways-to-aim-a-thin-cut"><a href="#7-Two-ways-to-aim-a-thin-cut" class="headerlink" title="7 · Two ways to aim a thin cut"></a>7 · Two ways to aim a thin cut</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b04_two_ways.webp" alt="Two close-up top views of the same thin cut. 1: the ghost ball touches the red from the side, and the white arrow points at the ghost's centre, marked with a plus. 2: a yellow dashed line runs from the white's edge to just inside the red's edge"></p>
<p>Use whichever your eye likes; many players check one with the other.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Ghost ball.</strong> Put the ghost against the far side of the red and aim the white's <strong>centre</strong> at the ghost's <strong>centre</strong>. On thin cuts the ghost sits almost a whole ball out to the side.</li>
<li><strong>Edge to edge.</strong> Send the white's <strong>edge</strong> to pass <strong>just inside</strong> the red's edge. For a near right angle, &quot;just inside&quot; means less than 1 mm.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> on very thin cuts, stand up and look from the side first. Decide how far out the ghost is, then get down and keep that picture.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="8-Watch-it-almost-sideways"><a href="#8-Watch-it-almost-sideways" class="headerlink" title="8 · Watch it: almost sideways"></a>8 · Watch it: almost sideways</h2>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b04_clip_thin.webp" alt="Clip: the red is near a side cushion, not far from a corner pocket; the white comes from below. The white barely touches the red's left side, the red runs to the right along the cushion into the corner pocket, and the white carries on almost straight to the side cushion and comes back"></div>
<p>The white only grazes the red. The red goes off about 76° from the white's line and runs into the corner ✓. The white hardly notices and carries on.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> the red gets only a small part of the white's speed (here about a quarter). This pot needs <strong>power 4</strong>. At power 2 or 3 the red stops short.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-What-makes-a-pot-hard"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-What-makes-a-pot-hard" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — What makes a pot hard"></a>Chapter 3 — What makes a pot hard</h1>
<h2 id="9-Thin-cuts-spread-your-error"><a href="#9-Thin-cuts-spread-your-error" class="headerlink" title="9 · Thin cuts spread your error"></a>9 · Thin cuts spread your error</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b00_fan.webp" alt="Two top-down pictures with the same tiny aiming error. Left, a straight shot: the three possible yellow paths stay close together. Right, a thin cut: the paths spread wide apart"></p>
<p>The same tiny mistake on both (the white 2 mm to one side). Straight pot: the ball's paths stay together. Thin cut: they fan out wide. <strong>The thinner the cut, the more an error grows.</strong></p>
<hr>
<h2 id="10-Distance-shrinks-the-room"><a href="#10-Distance-shrinks-the-room" class="headerlink" title="10 · Distance shrinks the room"></a>10 · Distance shrinks the room</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s02_distance.svg" alt="Three rows. A straight red 50 cm from a corner pocket, the white 30, 80 or 150 cm behind it. A green wedge from the white shows how far the aim may turn and still pot: 2.2, 0.72 and 0.36 degrees. Wedges drawn ten times wider"></p>
<p>The same straight red, 50 cm from the corner. Move the white back and the room to pot shrinks (model, medium pace):</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>White behind the red</th>
<th>Aim may turn by (total)</th>
<th>Room at the red</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>30 cm</td>
<td>2.2°</td>
<td>about 12 mm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>80 cm</td>
<td>0.72°</td>
<td>about 10 mm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>150 cm</td>
<td>0.36°</td>
<td>about 9 mm</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The room <strong>at the red</strong> stays about 1 cm. But from further back, 1 cm is a smaller turn of the cue. Twice as far back, about half the room.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="11-Watch-it-a-long-pot"><a href="#11-Watch-it-a-long-pot" class="headerlink" title="11 · Watch it: a long pot"></a>11 · Watch it: a long pot</h2>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/s02_clip_long.webp" alt="Clip from behind the white in the baulk area. A red sits near the top cushion, not far from the top-right corner pocket, with a yellow dashed line to the pocket. The white rolls the length of the table, the red drops into the corner and the white runs on"></div>
<p>The white is 2.5 m from the red, and the red is 0.6 m from the pocket. The model gives only <strong>0.12°</strong> of room: about 5 mm at the red. That is why the long pot is the big test of a straight cue.</p>
<p>What coaches usually advise for long pots (coaching advice, not a rule):</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Centre ball or a touch below, no side.</strong> Side bends the white's path (post 05).</li>
<li><strong>Medium pace.</strong> Firm enough to reach the pocket at a good speed, not a slam.</li>
<li><strong>Hold still</strong> until the red is in. Moving early moves the cue.</li>
</ul>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> practise long pots with the white and red in the same places each time. The same picture, ten times, trains the eye faster than ten different pots.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-4-—-How-much-room-the-pocket-gives"><a href="#Chapter-4-—-How-much-room-the-pocket-gives" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 4 — How much room the pocket gives"></a>Chapter 4 — How much room the pocket gives</h1>
<h2 id="12-The-corner-pocket-into-the-jaws-vs-along-the-cushion"><a href="#12-The-corner-pocket-into-the-jaws-vs-along-the-cushion" class="headerlink" title="12 · The corner pocket: into the jaws vs along the cushion"></a>12 · The corner pocket: into the jaws vs along the cushion</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s02_corner.webp" alt="Two close-up top views of the top-right corner pocket. 1: a red rolls in on the 45-degree line, straight into the jaws; a wide green band shows 98 mm of room. 2: a red rolls along the top cushion into the pocket; a thin green band shows only 16 mm"></p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>The ball rolls in…</th>
<th>Room for the ball's centre (model)</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>straight into the jaws (45°)</td>
<td>about 10 cm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>along the cushion</td>
<td>about 1.6 cm: the ball must stay within about 1.6 cm of the cushion</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>A ball sent <strong>into the jaws</strong> has lots of room. A ball <strong>along the cushion</strong> has to stay tight to it; drift 2 cm off it and the ball misses. Post 07 covers balls on the cushion.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="13-The-middle-pocket-closes-as-the-angle-gets-flatter"><a href="#13-The-middle-pocket-closes-as-the-angle-gets-flatter" class="headerlink" title="13 · The middle pocket closes as the angle gets flatter"></a>13 · The middle pocket closes as the angle gets flatter</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b06_window.webp" alt="Three close-up top views of the top middle pocket. A green band shows the room the ball has. Straight in: the band is wide, 98 mm. Coming in at 40 degrees: 66 mm. At 60 degrees: only 40 mm"></p>
<p>The middle pocket is cut into a straight cushion, so a ball coming in at a slant meets the far knuckle:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Coming in…</th>
<th>Room for the ball's centre (model)</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>straight</td>
<td>about 10 cm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>40° off straight</td>
<td>about 6–7 cm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>60° off straight</td>
<td>about 4 cm</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> on a slant, aim the ball at the <strong>middle of the hole itself</strong> (the yellow dot, behind the cushion line), not at the middle of the gap between the knuckles. That keeps it away from the far knuckle.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-5-—-A-small-correction"><a href="#Chapter-5-—-A-small-correction" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 5 — A small correction"></a>Chapter 5 — A small correction</h1>
<h2 id="14-Slow-cuts-get-dragged-a-little-throw"><a href="#14-Slow-cuts-get-dragged-a-little-throw" class="headerlink" title="14 · Slow cuts get dragged a little (throw)"></a>14 · Slow cuts get dragged a little (throw)</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b04_throw.webp" alt="Two top views of the same 60° cut with the white aimed at the ghost ball. A white dashed line shows where the red should go; a red line shows where it really goes, a few degrees fuller. At power 2 the gap is about 6 cm after one metre; at power 4 about 1.5 cm"></p>
<p>At contact the white rubs on the red and drags it a little the way the white is moving. This is <strong>throw</strong>. The red goes a touch <strong>fuller</strong> (less cut) than the ghost-ball line, more on slow shots.</p>
<p>If your slow cuts keep missing on the thick side:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Fix</th>
<th>How</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Overcut a hair</td>
<td>aim a fraction thinner than the ghost ball</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Add pace</td>
<td>a firmer hit drags less (power 4: about 1.5 cm off at 1 m instead of 6 cm)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Side</td>
<td>a touch of side the right way cancels it (post 05)</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<hr>
<h1 id="Try-it-at-the-table"><a href="#Try-it-at-the-table" class="headerlink" title="Try it at the table"></a>Try it at the table</h1>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Drill</th>
<th>Set-up</th>
<th>Look for</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Straight pots</td>
<td>white, red and pocket in a line, 30 cm then 80 cm apart</td>

</tr>
<tr>
<td>Fraction ladder</td>

<tr>
<td>Ghost check</td>

<td>ten in a row from the same spot</td>
</tr>

</tr>
