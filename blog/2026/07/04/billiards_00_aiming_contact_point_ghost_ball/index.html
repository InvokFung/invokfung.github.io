Billiards & Snooker — 00 · Aiming: Contact Point, Ghost Ball and Cut Angle

Created2026-07-04|Updated2026-07-04|Sports > Billiards
|Post View:

How do you send the yellow into the pocket when the white is somewhere else? You plan it backwards, from the pocket. This first lesson is all pictures: follow the numbers and the colours.

Series map. ★ Basics: 00 Aiming (this post) → 01 The hit → 02 Stop, follow, screw → 03 Where the white goes · ★★ Club: 04 Thin cuts → 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker · ★★★ Advanced: 10 Plants and sets → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → 13 Position · ★★★★ Hard: 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → 15 Swerve and special spots.

Learning Outcome

  • Find the red dot on any ball, for any pocket.
  • Aim the white at the ghost ball, not at the red dot.
  • See the cut as a shape, and know why thin cuts miss more.

Colour key, used in every picture:

Mark Means
yellow dashed line the way to the pocket
red dot where the two balls must touch
see-through ball the ghost ball: where the white must be at the moment it touches
white arrow where you send the white
green wedge how much the yellow turns (the cut)
✓ / ✗ pot / miss

1 · The shot

White, yellow, pocket. Don't start from the white. Start from the pocket.

2 · Three steps, from the pocket back

  1. Line from the pocket through the middle of the yellow.
  2. Where that line comes out of the back: the red dot.
  3. A ball touching the red dot: the ghost ball. Send the white's centre to the ghost's centre.

The red dot never moves when the white moves. Only the wedge (the cut) changes.

3 · What you see down the cue

Aim at the +, the ghost's centre. It sits on empty cloth, half a ball beyond the red dot. Pick a speck on the cloth there and aim at it.

Trick: walk in behind the yellow first, looking at the pocket. Find the red dot from there, then walk round to the white.

4 · Where the tip hits the white

A little below the middle, firm. The white then slides into the yellow without spinning forward or back (a stun). Post 01 shows every other spot on the white.

5 · Watch it

The yellow follows the dashed line into the pocket ✓. The white leaves at a right angle (the square). That is what a stun does, every time.

6 · The classic miss: aiming at the red dot

The red dot is easy to see, so it is tempting to aim the white straight at it. But the white is a ball, not a point: it touches early, the hit is too full, the yellow does not turn enough, and it misses ✗.

Common pitfall: "I keep missing thick." Move the aim out to the ghost's centre: one ball-radius further than the red dot.

7 · Full, half, thin

Top: what you see down the cue. Bottom: where the yellow goes.

  • Full (1): white covers all of the yellow. Straight on, no wedge.
  • Half (½): the white's centre points at the yellow's edge. The easiest one to see; learn it first.
  • Quarter (¼): only a sliver overlaps. A big turn.

Less overlap, bigger turn. Post 04 takes this all the way to very thin cuts.

8 · Thin cuts need more care

Same tiny mistake on both (the white 2 mm to one side). Straight shot: the yellow's paths stay together. Thin cut: they fan out wide. Thin and long shots forgive nothing, so leave yourself short, not-too-thin pots when you can.

9 · Try it at the table

Drill Set-up Look for
Straight pots white, yellow and pocket in a line the white stops dead (stun, full ball)
Half-ball pots white's centre at the yellow's edge the white leaves at a right angle
Ghost check put a coin where the ghost ball sits, look from behind the white, take the coin away, play your eye learns where the ghost is

Trick: misses mostly on the same "too full" side? You are aiming partly at the red dot. Go back to picture 6.

Cheat-sheet
Plan pocket → line → red dot → ghost → aim
Aim white's centre to ghost's centre, never at the red dot
Stun a little below the middle, firm: the white leaves at a right angle
Half ball white's centre on the yellow's edge
Thin / long small mistakes grow; choose easier shots when you can
