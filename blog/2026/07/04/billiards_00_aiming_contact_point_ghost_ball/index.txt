<p>White, yellow, pocket. Don't start from the white. <strong>Start from the pocket.</strong></p>

<h1 id="2-Three-steps-from-the-pocket-back"><a href="#2-Three-steps-from-the-pocket-back" class="headerlink" title="2 · Three steps, from the pocket back"></a>2 · Three steps, from the pocket back</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b00_steps.webp" alt="Three top-down pictures. 1: a dashed line from the pocket through the yellow ball. 2: a red dot where the line leaves the back of the yellow. 3: a see-through ghost ball touching the red dot, the white arrow aimed at its centre, and a green wedge showing the cut"></p>
<ol>
<li>Line from the pocket through the middle of the yellow.</li>
<li>Where that line comes out of the back: the <strong>red dot</strong>.</li>
<li>A ball touching the red dot: the <strong>ghost ball</strong>. Send the white's centre to the ghost's centre.</li>
</ol>
<p>The red dot never moves when the white moves. Only the wedge (the cut) changes.</p>

<h1 id="3-What-you-see-down-the-cue"><a href="#3-What-you-see-down-the-cue" class="headerlink" title="3 · What you see down the cue"></a>3 · What you see down the cue</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b00_aim_eye.webp" alt="Player's eye view along the cue: the white ball in front, the yellow ahead, the see-through ghost ball beside it with a white cross on its centre"></p>
<p>Aim at the <strong>+</strong>, the ghost's centre. It sits on empty cloth, half a ball beyond the red dot. Pick a speck on the cloth there and aim at it.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> walk in behind the yellow first, looking at the pocket. Find the red dot from there, then walk round to the white.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="4-Where-the-tip-hits-the-white"><a href="#4-Where-the-tip-hits-the-white" class="headerlink" title="4 · Where the tip hits the white"></a>4 · Where the tip hits the white</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b00_tip.webp" alt="Close-up of the cue tip touching the white ball a little below its centre, with a small face view of the white showing the tip spot just below the middle"></p>
<p>A little <strong>below the middle</strong>, firm. The white then slides into the yellow without spinning forward or back (a <strong>stun</strong>). Post 01 shows every other spot on the white.</p>

<h1 id="5-Watch-it"><a href="#5-Watch-it" class="headerlink" title="5 · Watch it"></a>5 · Watch it</h1>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b00_clip_pot.webp" alt="Clip: 1 line to the pocket, 2 red dot, 3 ghost ball, 4 aim and green wedge, then the stroke: the yellow runs down the line into the pocket and the white leaves at a right angle, marked with a square"></div>
<p>The yellow follows the dashed line into the pocket ✓. The white leaves at a <strong>right angle</strong> (the square). That is what a stun does, every time.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="6-The-classic-miss-aiming-at-the-red-dot"><a href="#6-The-classic-miss-aiming-at-the-red-dot" class="headerlink" title="6 · The classic miss: aiming at the red dot"></a>6 · The classic miss: aiming at the red dot</h1>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b00_clip_miss.webp" alt="Clip: the white is sent straight at the red dot; it touches the yellow early, the hit is too full, and the yellow misses the pocket and hits the cushion"></div>
<p>The red dot is easy to see, so it is tempting to aim the white straight at it. But the white is a ball, not a point: it touches <strong>early</strong>, the hit is too <strong>full</strong>, the yellow does not turn enough, and it misses ✗.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Common pitfall:</strong> &quot;I keep missing thick.&quot; Move the aim out to the ghost's centre: one ball-radius further than the red dot.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="7-Full-half-thin"><a href="#7-Full-half-thin" class="headerlink" title="7 · Full, half, thin"></a>7 · Full, half, thin</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b00_fullness.webp" alt="Three columns: full ball, half ball, quarter ball. Top: the view down the cue, with the ghost ball covering all, half, or a quarter of the yellow. Bottom: from above, the yellow goes straight on, turns a little, or turns a lot, shown by a green wedge"></p>
<p>Top: what you see down the cue. Bottom: where the yellow goes.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Full (1):</strong> white covers all of the yellow. Straight on, no wedge.</li>
<li><strong>Half (½):</strong> the white's centre points at the yellow's <strong>edge</strong>. The easiest one to see; learn it first.</li>
<li><strong>Quarter (¼):</strong> only a sliver overlaps. A big turn.</li>
</ul>
<p>Less overlap, bigger turn. Post 04 takes this all the way to very thin cuts.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="8-Thin-cuts-need-more-care"><a href="#8-Thin-cuts-need-more-care" class="headerlink" title="8 · Thin cuts need more care"></a>8 · Thin cuts need more care</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b00_fan.webp" alt="Two top-down pictures with the same tiny aiming error. Left, a straight shot: the three possible yellow paths stay close together. Right, a thin cut: the paths spread wide apart"></p>
<p>Same tiny mistake on both (the white 2 mm to one side). Straight shot: the yellow's paths stay together. Thin cut: they fan out wide. <strong>Thin and long shots forgive nothing</strong>, so leave yourself short, not-too-thin pots when you can.</p>
