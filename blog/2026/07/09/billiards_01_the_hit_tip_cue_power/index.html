Billiards & Snooker — 01 · The Hit: Tip, Cue and Power

Created2026-07-09|Updated2026-07-09|Sports > Billiards
|Post View:

Before spin and position, let's get the hit itself right: where the tip touches the white, how the cue moves, and how hard to play. Every later post uses the same two small signs from this lesson, so it is worth a few minutes.

Series map. ★ Basics: 00 Aiming → 01 The hit (this post) → 02 Stop, follow, screw → 03 Where the white goes · ★★ Club: 04 Thin cuts → 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker · ★★★ Advanced: 10 Plants and sets → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → 13 Position · ★★★★ Hard: 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → 15 Swerve and special spots.

Learning Outcome

  • Know the five basic tip spots on the white.
  • Keep the tip inside the safe zone, so you never miscue.
  • Keep the cue level and on the line.
  • Choose a power from 1 to 5, by how far the white would roll.

Two signs used in every clip from now on:

Sign Means
white circle with a blue dot the white seen from behind; the blue dot is where the tip hits
five bars power: 1 gentle … 5 very hard

1 · Five spots on the white

Spot What it does Post
centre plain ball, the white just rolls on here
▲ top follow: the white runs on after the hit 02
▼ bottom screw (backspin): the white comes back 02
◀ left / right ▶ side: changes the angle off a cushion 05

Please start with the centre. Most pots need nothing else.

2 · Stay inside the safe zone

Keep the tip inside the green circle, about half-way from the centre to the edge. Further out, the tip slides off the ball (a miscue) and the white goes anywhere.

Trick: chalk before every shot. Most miscues are a dry tip, not a bad spot.

3 · Keep the cue level

Keep the cue as flat as the cushion allows ✓. A raised butt ✗ pushes the white down into the cloth and makes it curve. That curve is a special shot for post 15, not for every day.

4 · Stay on the line

The white goes where the cue is pointing at the moment of the hit. Please keep the cue on the line from start to finish, and let it follow through, past where the white was.

Watch out: a small turn of the cue (about 7° here) misses a red only half a table away.

5 · Power 1 to 5

We describe power by how far the white would roll on its own, hit in the centre from the baulk end:

Power The white alone rolls…
1 to the blue
2 to the top cushion
3 up, and back past the blue
4 up, and back to the baulk line
5 up, back, and up again

Most pots are power 2–3. Power 5 is for breaking up a pack or a long escape. A softer hit is easier to control.

Trick: before a shot, picture the white's journey and pick the number. Then play that number, not "hard" or "soft".

6 · Watch it: a straight pot

Centre, power 2, cue straight through. The red drops ✓ and the white follows it a little way, because a centre hit makes the white roll forwards.

7 · Try it at the table

Drill Set-up Look for
Cue on the line white on the brown spot, hit it up the table over the blue and pink spots, centre ball, power 2 it comes straight back to the tip
Power ladder white on the baulk line, play power 1, 2, 3, 4 where it stops matches the picture
Safe zone chalk, then hit top, bottom, left and right, each just inside the zone no miscues

Cheat-sheet
Tip spot centre first; top = follow, bottom = screw, sides = side
Safe zone about half-way to the edge; chalk every shot
Cue level, on the line, follow through
Power 1 blue · 2 top cushion · 3 back past the blue · 4 back to baulk · 5 up and back twice

What's next

References

  • WPBSA, Official Rules of the Games of Snooker and English Billiards (2022): table and ball sizes, spot positions. https://wpbsa.com/wp-content/uploads/Rulebook-Website-Updated-May-2022.pdf
  • Dr. Dave Alciatore, technical proofs and FAQ: tip offset, miscue limit (about half the ball radius), cue elevation. https://billiards.colostate.edu/
  • The pictures and clips come from a physics model of the balls at snooker sizes. Rolling distances depend on the cloth, so treat the power table as a guide and check it on your table.
