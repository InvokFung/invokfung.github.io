<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Know what a good safety leaves: the white <strong>far away</strong>, <strong>on a cushion</strong>, or <strong>behind a colour</strong>.</li>
<li>Play a break-off, and miss on the thin side.</li>
<li>Play a thin safety back to baulk, and judge its pace.</li>
<li>Use distance and cushions as defence.</li>
<li>Pick between safety options by what your opponent has next.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-1-—-The-idea"><a href="#Chapter-1-—-The-idea" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 1 — The idea"></a>Chapter 1 — The idea</h1>
<h2 id="1-What-a-good-safety-leaves"><a href="#1-What-a-good-safety-leaves" class="headerlink" title="1 · What a good safety leaves"></a>1 · What a good safety leaves</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s11_leave.svg" alt="Three small tables. 1: the white far from the reds. 2: the white close to a cushion. 3: the white hidden behind a colour, so no red can be hit directly"></p>
<p>After a good safety your opponent has <strong>no easy pot</strong>, and the white is:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>far</strong> from the reds (a long shot has little room), or</li>
<li><strong>on a cushion</strong> (they must raise the cue and hit the top of the white), or</li>
<li><strong>behind a colour</strong> (a snooker: they cannot hit a red directly).</li>
</ol>
<p>One of these is good. Two or three together is very good. You do not need a perfect snooker every time.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="2-Distance-is-defence"><a href="#2-Distance-is-defence" class="headerlink" title="2 · Distance is defence"></a>2 · Distance is defence</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s02_distance.svg" alt="A diagram of the room for error in the aim at three distances, with the wedges drawn ten times wider so they show: 30 cm 2.20 degrees, 80 cm 0.72 degrees, 1.5 m 0.36 degrees"></p>
<p>From post 02: the same pot from 30 cm has 2.20° of room, from 1.5 m only 0.36°. Every metre you put between the white and the reds makes your opponent's next shot much harder.</p>
<hr>

<h2 id="3-The-break-off"><a href="#3-The-break-off" class="headerlink" title="3 · The break-off"></a>3 · The break-off</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b12_breakoff.webp" alt="Top view of the whole table at the start of a frame. The white starts in the D near the yellow. <ol>
<li>White in the D, between the brown and the yellow.</li>
<li>Aim to just <strong>clip the end red of the back row</strong>, on the outside.</li>
<li>Power 4, a touch above centre.</li>
<li>The white goes top cushion, side cushion, and back to the <strong>baulk end</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b12_clip_break.webp" alt="Clip from behind the baulk end: the white leaves the D, clips the end red of the pack, runs round off the top and side cushions and comes back past the yellow to the baulk end"></div>
<p>Only <strong>3 reds</strong> move more than 4 cm, and the white stops in baulk near the cushion ✓ (model).</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> pick a mark on the top cushion where the white should land, and watch that mark, not the red, as you play.</p>

<h2 id="5-Thin-off-a-red-back-behind-a-colour"><a href="#5-Thin-off-a-red-back-behind-a-colour" class="headerlink" title="5 · Thin off a red, back behind a colour"></a>5 · Thin off a red, back behind a colour</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s11_behind.webp" alt="Top view of the whole table. Six reds and the pink near the top end; one red on the bottom side cushion near the middle pocket. The white, near the middle of the table, clips that red thinly, comes off the cushion and runs back to baulk, past the left end, and stops behind the brown, in a green ring"></p>
<p>The same idea as the break-off, later in the frame. The reds are up the table; one red sits on the side cushion.</p>
<ol>
<li>Clip that red <strong>thin</strong>: it moves only 1 cm and stays on the cushion.</li>
<li>The white comes off the cushion, runs to the baulk cushion and back.</li>
<li>It stops about <strong>35 cm behind the brown</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>From there no red can be hit at both edges (post 09): the colours are in the way. A full <strong>snooker</strong>.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="6-Pace-makes-the-snooker"><a href="#6-Pace-makes-the-snooker" class="headerlink" title="6 · Pace makes the snooker"></a>6 · Pace makes the snooker</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s11_pace.webp" alt="Three close top views of the baulk end after the same thin safety at three paces. 1: a touch soft: the white stops nearer the baulk cushion; a red dashed line shows a red it can still hit, cross. 2: right: the white stops behind the brown, tick. 3: a touch firm: the white stops above the brown, two red dashed lines show reds it can hit, cross"></p>
<p>The same aim at different paces (model):</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Pace</th>
<th>The white ends</th>
<th>Reds it can hit</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>a touch soft</td>
<td>nearer the baulk cushion, off the line behind the brown</td>
<td>1 ✗</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>right</td>
<td>behind the brown</td>
<td><strong>none</strong> ✓</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>a touch firm</td>
<td>closer to the brown, but off to the side</td>
<td>2 ✗</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The snooker works over about <strong>6%</strong> of the pace, somewhere between power 2 and power 3. Even when it misses by a little, the white is still in baulk, far from the reds: a good safety, just not a snooker.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> pick the stopping spot first (&quot;a ball behind the brown&quot;), then play the pace for that spot. The aim is the easy part.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="7-Leave-the-white-on-a-cushion"><a href="#7-Leave-the-white-on-a-cushion" class="headerlink" title="7 · Leave the white on a cushion"></a>7 · Leave the white on a cushion</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s07_back.webp" alt="Top view of four whites, each sent straight into the top cushion from 60 cm at power 2 with a different tip, shown by small faces. The yellow tracks show how far each comes back: top 117 cm, centre 56 cm, a little low 23 cm, low 8 cm"></p>
<p>A white that dies <strong>on a cushion</strong> is hard to play from. Straight in with a low tip and soft pace, it comes back only 8 cm (post 07).</p>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s07_tight.webp" alt="Two 3D views of the white touching the top cushion, cueing away from it. 1: from the side, the cue rising over the cushion and the rail, a wedge shows 12 degrees. 2: closer, the tip meets the white well above its centre; a face shows the tip spot near the top"></p>
<p>Your opponent then has to raise the cue about <strong>12°</strong>, and hit the top of the white: no screw, no stun (post 07).</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="8-A-soft-touch-is-a-shot-too"><a href="#8-A-soft-touch-is-a-shot-too" class="headerlink" title="8 · A soft touch is a shot too"></a>8 · A soft touch is a shot too</h2>
<p>Snooker has <strong>no cushion rule</strong>: after the white hits a ball on, nothing has to reach a cushion (unlike pool). So a soft safety is legal:</p>
<ul>
<li>roll the white up to a red so it stops just behind it, or behind a colour next to it;</li>
<li>nudge a red a few centimetres off a pocket;</li>
<li>just touch a red in the pack and stay there.</li>
</ul>
<p>A soft touch moves the fewest balls, so you know exactly what you leave.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> stopping <strong>touching</strong> a red is a special case (post 14). Your opponent may play away from it without hitting anything, which can be easy for them.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="9-The-shot-to-nothing"><a href="#9-The-shot-to-nothing" class="headerlink" title="9 · The shot to nothing"></a>9 · The shot to nothing</h2>
<p>A <strong>shot to nothing</strong> is a pot where the white goes somewhere safe <strong>whether it goes in or not</strong> (coaching advice):</p>
<ul>
<li>you play the pot at a pace that also sends the white back to baulk;</li>
<li>if it goes in, you have a chance to build a break;</li>
<li>if it misses, your opponent still faces a long shot.</li>
</ul>
<p>Before a long pot, ask: &quot;if this misses, where are the red and the white?&quot; In the model, a long red into a top corner missed by 0.6° ran all the way back to baulk, so check where the <strong>red</strong> ends, not just the white.</p>
<hr>

<p>For each option ask: <strong>&quot;what does my opponent have next?&quot;</strong> Choose the one that leaves the least, and the one you can play.</p>
