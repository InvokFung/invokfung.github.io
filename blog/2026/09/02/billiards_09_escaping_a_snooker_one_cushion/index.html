Billiards & Snooker — 09 · Escaping a Snooker off One Cushion

Created2026-09-02|Updated2026-09-02|Sports > Billiards
|Post View:

You are snookered: a colour sits between the white and the red, and you must hit a red. The answer is a cushion, and the aiming method (對射角, the mirror angle) is the one from posts 07 and 08, now for the white. This lesson shows how to find the line, why one route can be easier than another, and how pace changes the aim.

Series map. ★ Basics: 00 Aiming → 01 The hit → 02 Stop, follow, screw → 03 Where the white goes · ★★ Club: 04 Thin cuts → 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker (this post) · ★★★ Advanced: 10 Plants and sets → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → 13 Position · ★★★★ Hard: 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → 15 Swerve and special spots.

Learning Outcome

  • Aim an escape at the mirror ball.
  • Compare routes by how much room (the window) each one gives.
  • Know when the mirror aim works as it is and when to move it.
  • Remember the white must go somewhere safe afterwards.

1 · Snookered

The red is straight behind the blue. Hitting the blue first is a foul: your opponent gets at least 4 points, and the referee may also call a miss and let them put the balls back and make you play again. So you must find a way round.

2 · Aim at the mirror ball

  1. Picture the red reflected behind the cushion you will use (the mirror red).
  2. Aim the white's centre straight at the mirror red's centre.
  3. The yellow point where that line meets the cushion is your target. Look at it, not at the red.

Trick: measure the reflection from where the white's centre touches the cushion, half a ball out from the rubber (post 07). From a distance, pick a mark on the rail level with that point.

3 · Two routes, two windows

You do not need to hit the red in the middle; any contact escapes. So each route has a window: the range of aims that touch the red first.

Route (power 4) White to the cushion Room at the cushion Mirror aim as it is
1 · top side cushion 1.4 m about 5 cm misses ✗; move the cushion point about 8 cm towards you
2 · bottom side cushion 1.0 m about 7 cm hits ✓, right in the middle

Route 2 is the better choice here: more room, and the plain mirror works.

Watch out: check the whole path for other balls first. A route that clips a third ball is no route at all.

4 · Pace moves the aim

The mirror works as it is when the white is still sliding when it reaches the cushion. A white that has started rolling comes off a little flatter and runs on (post 07).

Route 2 Where to aim
power 4 (still sliding at the cushion) at the mirror red ✓
power 3 (rolling by the cushion) about 13 cm shorter along the cushion; the room shrinks to about 4 cm

So there are two good ways to play it. Play it firmer and trust the mirror, or play it softer and aim shorter. Firmer is easier to aim; softer is easier to control afterwards.

5 · Watch it: the escape

Power 4, centre ball, aimed at the mirror red ✓.

6 · Where does the white go?

A good escape also leaves the white safe. Before you play, think through:

  • After hitting the red, where does the white run at this pace?
  • Will it leave a red near a pocket?
  • Could a softer route or a thinner contact keep the white near a cushion?

If no route leaves the white safe, take the one with the biggest window. A miss costs more than a loose position.

7 · Try it at the table

Drill Set-up Look for
Mirror ball the white in the D, a red near the pink spot; a colour in between find the cushion point before you get down; hit the red
Window test the same, aim 2 cm left and right of your point which aims still hit: that is your window
Two paces the same route at power 3 and power 4 how much shorter the soft one needs

Cheat-sheet
Line the white's centre at the mirror ball
Mirror from where the white's centre touches the cushion
Choose the route with the biggest window and no other balls in the way
Power 4 the mirror works as it is
Softer aim shorter along the cushion
After think about where the white stops
