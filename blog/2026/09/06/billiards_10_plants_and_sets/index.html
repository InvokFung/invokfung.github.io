Billiards & Snooker — 10 · Plants and Sets

Created2026-09-06|Updated2026-09-06|Sports > Billiards
|Post View:

Sometimes the ball you hit is not the one that goes in. You hit one red, and it pots another. When the two reds touch, it is a set; when there is a gap, it is a plant. This lesson shows when a set is "on", why a set can still miss, how to aim a plant with two ghost balls, and why a plant is so much harder than it looks.

Series map. ★ Basics: 00 Aiming → 01 The hit → 02 Stop, follow, screw → 03 Where the white goes · ★★ Club: 04 Thin cuts → 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker · ★★★ Advanced: 10 Plants and sets (this post) → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → 13 Position · ★★★★ Hard: 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → 15 Swerve and special spots.

Learning Outcome

  • Spot a set: the line of centres points at the pocket.
  • Allow for throw on a long set.
  • Aim a plant with two ghost balls, from the pocket back to the white.
  • Judge when a plant is worth playing.

1 · A set: two touching reds

When two balls touch, the second one leaves along the line through both centres, almost wherever you hit the first. So a set is "on" when that line points at the pocket.

Almost, because the first red rubs the second and drags it a little the way the first red is moving (throw, post 04). Over 1.5 m that is enough to miss:

White comes in… Second red dragged by Result
along the line (1) 0° ✓
10° to the side 1.4° ✓
20° to the side (2) 2.9° ✗
30° to the side 3.0° ✗

Trick: the drag can help. A set that points a little wide (2.5° in picture 3) goes in when you play it from the side that drags it back towards the pocket (here anywhere from 10° to 40°). Straight on, it misses.

On a short set, near the pocket, the drag hardly matters. On a long one, either come in close to the line, or use the drag on purpose.

2 · A plant: two reds apart

Work backwards from the pocket:

  1. Ghost ball 2: where red 1 must be when it touches red 2 to send it to the pocket.
  2. Ghost ball 1: where the white must be when it touches red 1 to send red 1 to ghost ball 2.
  3. Aim the white at ghost ball 1, like any pot.

Watch out: throw happens twice. Here the plain ghost-ball aim needed a turn of 0.08° to go in. Small, but this plant has no room to spare.

3 · Small errors grow

From the same white, compare a direct pot of red 1 with the plant:

Room in the aim
direct pot of red 1 into the corner 0.24°
the plant 0.08°, 3 times less

Aim 0.1° off (1.4 mm sideways at red 1, about 78 cm away): the direct pot still drops; the plant misses.

Every ball in the chain passes the error on and makes it bigger. Plants are easiest when:

  • the two reds are close together, and
  • the second red is close to the pocket, and
  • the first red is hit nearly full.

4 · Watch it: the plant

Power 4, a little below centre. Pocket, ghost 2, ghost 1, then aim ✓.

5 · Plant or not?

Choose the plant when it is short and close to straight, or when nothing else is on. When it is long and angled, look for something else first:

  • another red you can pot directly;
  • a safety (post 12) that leaves the reds where they are.

A missed plant often leaves two reds near a pocket for your opponent.

6 · Try it at the table

Drill Set-up Look for
Set check two touching reds 1 m from a corner, pointing at it play from straight behind, then 10°, 20° and 30° to the side
Use the drag the same set turned a little wide find the side and angle that pot it
Short plant two reds 10 cm apart, 30 cm from a pocket pot it five times in a row
Long plant the same reds moved 1 m away how much harder it feels

Cheat-sheet
Set the line of centres points at the pocket
Long set the second ball is dragged the way the first one moves; come in close to the line
Plant ghost 2, then ghost 1, then aim
Errors grow through every ball in the chain
Play it when short, close together, near the pocket
