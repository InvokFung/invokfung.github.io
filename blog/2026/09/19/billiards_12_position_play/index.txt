11:T3927,<p>A break is a chain: each pot must leave the white where the next pot is easy. That is <strong>position play</strong>. This lesson covers aiming the white at a zone, choosing the route that forgives pace errors, keeping an angle, the right side of the ball, the natural path, planning three balls ahead, and a checklist for every shot.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Series map. <em>★ Basics:</em> 01 Cue action and elevation → 02 Aiming and potting → 03 Tip and power → 04 Where the white goes · <em>★★ Club:</em> 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles · <em>★★★ Advanced:</em> 09 Escaping snookers → 10 Multi-ball shots → 11 Safety → <strong>12 Position play</strong> (this post) · <em>★★★★ Hard:</em> 13 Break-building → 14 Special shots · <em>Reference:</em> 15 Rules, tactics and practice · <em>★★★★★ Pro:</em> 16 Cue-ball control.</p>

<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Aim the white at a <strong>zone</strong>, not a spot.</li>
<li>Pick the route whose path runs <strong>along</strong> the zone.</li>
<li>Keep a small <strong>angle</strong> on the next ball, on the <strong>right side</strong>.</li>
<li>Use the <strong>natural path</strong> and control only the pace.</li>
<li>Plan <strong>three balls</strong> ahead.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="1-A-zone-not-a-point"><a href="#1-A-zone-not-a-point" class="headerlink" title="1 · A zone, not a point"></a>1 · A zone, not a point</h2>

<p>Above centre, power 3: the red drops, the white comes off the end cushion and lands in the green ✓.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="5-The-right-side-of-the-ball"><a href="#5-The-right-side-of-the-ball" class="headerlink" title="5 · The right side of the ball"></a>5 · The right side of the ball</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s12_side.webp" alt="Two top views of the black end. The same 25 degree cut on the black into the top right corner, from either side of the line, power 3. 1: the right side: the white goes off the end cushion and runs up the table, ending 30 cm from the next red. 2: the wrong side: the white comes back off the side cushion and stops near the black's spot, 106 cm from the red"></p>
<p>The same 25° cut on the black, from the two sides of the black's line to the pocket (model, power 3, a touch below centre):</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>White on…</th>
<th>After the black it…</th>
<th>Next red</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>the right side (1)</td>
<td>runs off the end cushion up the table</td>
<td><strong>30 cm</strong> away ✓</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>the wrong side (2)</td>
<td>comes back to the black's spot</td>
<td>106 cm away ✗</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The angle is the same; only the <strong>side</strong> of the line changes, and the white goes the opposite way. So the zone for position is half of the green: the half that sends the white towards the next ball.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="6-The-natural-path-change-only-the-pace"><a href="#6-The-natural-path-change-only-the-pace" class="headerlink" title="6 · The natural path: change only the pace"></a>6 · The natural path: change only the pace</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s12_natural.webp" alt="Top view of the whole table. The black is potted into the top right corner from a 25 degree angle with a centre-ball hit. The white goes off the end cushion and up along the top side cushion. Three rings show where it stops: a bit softer, power 3 and a bit firmer, spread along the same path"></p>
<p>The white's <strong>natural path</strong> is where it goes with a plain centre-ball hit. Here, three paces of the same shot (model):</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Pace</th>
<th>Where the white stops</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>a bit softer (85%)</td>
<td>by the top side cushion, between the pink and the blue</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>power 3</td>
<td>about 30 cm further on</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>a bit firmer (120%)</td>
<td>about 60 cm further again</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>If the next ball is on the natural path, <strong>don't add spin</strong>: just choose the pace. It is the most accurate way to get position.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-2-—-Planning"><a href="#Chapter-2-—-Planning" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 2 — Planning"></a>Chapter 2 — Planning</h1>
<h2 id="7-Plan-three-balls-ahead"><a href="#7-Plan-three-balls-ahead" class="headerlink" title="7 · Plan three balls ahead"></a>7 · Plan three balls ahead</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s12_plan.svg" alt="A table with three pots in a row. 1: the white pots a red into the top right corner and rolls to a landing spot below the blue. 2: from there it pots the blue into the top middle pocket and rolls left to a spot near the next red. 3: it pots that red into the bottom left corner"></p>
<p>Work <strong>backwards</strong>:</p>
<ol>
<li>Where must the white be for pot 3 (the next red)?</li>
<li>So which angle on pot 2 (the colour) sends it there?</li>
<li>So where must pot 1 (this red) leave the white?</li>
</ol>
<p>In snooker you choose the colour after each red. Choose the one whose angle sends the white towards the next red.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="8-Stay-below-the-ball-you-need"><a href="#8-Stay-below-the-ball-you-need" class="headerlink" title="8 · Stay below the ball you need"></a>8 · Stay below the ball you need</h2>
<p>Coaching advice: when you want to come <strong>back</strong> towards the next ball, land a little <strong>below</strong> it (on the side away from where you will go). Then the white comes back towards it with a natural angle. If you land level with it or past it, you have to screw back or go round the table.</p>
<p>This is the same idea as section 5: choose the side of the ball before you choose the spot.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="9-Choose-the-simplest-shot"><a href="#9-Choose-the-simplest-shot" class="headerlink" title="9 · Choose the simplest shot"></a>9 · Choose the simplest shot</h2>
<p>When two routes reach the zone, prefer:</p>
<ol>
<li>the <strong>natural path</strong> with pace only (section 6);</li>
<li>then plain follow, stun or screw;</li>
<li>side only when it opens a clearly better route (post 06).</li>
</ol>
<p>Each extra thing (side, a very low tip, a long route round the table) adds a chance of missing the pot itself. <strong>The pot comes first</strong>: position is no use if this ball stays out.</p>
<hr>
