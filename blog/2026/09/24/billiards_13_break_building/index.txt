<h2 id="1-What-each-ball-is-worth"><a href="#1-What-each-ball-is-worth" class="headerlink" title="1 · What each ball is worth"></a>1 · What each ball is worth</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s13_values.svg" alt="Seven balls with their values: red 1, yellow 2, green 3, brown 4, blue 5, pink 6, black 7. Below, the order of a break: red, colour, red, colour while reds are left; then the colours from yellow to black"></p>
<ul>
<li>While reds are on the table: <strong>a red, then any colour</strong>. The colour comes back on its spot.</li>
<li>After the last red and its colour: the colours <strong>in order</strong>, yellow to black, and they stay down.</li>
</ul>
<p>The maximum is 15 reds each with the black, then all the colours: 15 × 8 + 27 = <strong>147</strong>.</p>

<h2 id="4-Stay-close"><a href="#4-Stay-close" class="headerlink" title="4 · Stay close"></a>4 · Stay close</h2>
<p>In those three shots the white moved only <strong>65, 76 and 68 cm</strong> (start to finish in a straight line). Short moves mean:</p>
<ul>

<li>short next pots, with lots of room (post 02);</li>
<li>fewer balls in the way.</li>

<h2 id="5-An-angle-on-every-ball"><a href="#5-An-angle-on-every-ball" class="headerlink" title="5 · An angle on every ball"></a>5 · An angle on every ball</h2>
<p>Every next pot in the sequence had a cut between 9° and 16°: never dead straight, never thin. That is the angle from post 12, kept shot after shot. If one shot leaves you straight, the next is often a stun or screw just to keep going, with no choice of where the white goes.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="6-Use-the-pink-and-the-blue-too"><a href="#6-Use-the-pink-and-the-blue-too" class="headerlink" title="6 · Use the pink and the blue too"></a>6 · Use the pink and the blue too</h2>
<p>The black is worth most, but it is not always the best colour:</p>
<ul>
<li>the black's pot may be blocked, or its angle may send the white the wrong way;</li>
<li>the <strong>pink</strong> and <strong>blue</strong> sit nearer the middle of the table, close to reds that have spread;</li>
<li>a safe <strong>blue</strong> that keeps the break going beats a risky black that ends it.</li>
</ul>
<p>Coaching advice: choose the colour by <strong>where it sends the white</strong>, not by its value alone.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="7-Open-the-reds-early"><a href="#7-Open-the-reds-early" class="headerlink" title="7 · Open the reds early"></a>7 · Open the reds early</h2>
<p>Reds in a tight pack cannot be potted. Open them while you are still in, and while the colours around them are on their spots:</p>
<ul>
<li>pot a red or a colour and let the white run into the <strong>side</strong> of the pack (post 10);</li>
<li>choose the pace: one step softer leaves a few reds out, one step firmer spreads them (post 10);</li>
<li>do it from a shot where the pot itself is easy.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2 id="8-Problem-reds-first"><a href="#8-Problem-reds-first" class="headerlink" title="8 · Problem reds first"></a>8 · Problem reds first</h2>
<p>Some reds can only be potted one way: on a cushion, near the baulk end, or touching another ball. Coaching advice: take them <strong>early</strong>, while there are other reds for the white to go to afterwards. Leave them to the end and they finish the break.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-The-colours"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-The-colours" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — The colours"></a>Chapter 3 — The colours</h1>
<h2 id="9-Clearing-the-colours"><a href="#9-Clearing-the-colours" class="headerlink" title="9 · Clearing the colours"></a>9 · Clearing the colours</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s13_colours.svg" alt="A table with the six colours on their spots and arrows showing the order: yellow, green, brown in the D, then the blue in the middle, the pink and the black at the top end. Short notes give a usual pocket for each"></p>
<p>After the last red, the colours go in order: <strong>yellow, green, brown, blue, pink, black</strong>, together worth 27. Plan each one for the next, exactly as in post 12. The pockets in the picture are common choices (coaching advice), not rules.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="10-Where-a-colour-is-spotted"><a href="#10-Where-a-colour-is-spotted" class="headerlink" title="10 · Where a colour is spotted"></a>10 · Where a colour is spotted</h2>
<p>While reds remain, a potted colour goes back on <strong>its own spot</strong>. If that spot is taken by another ball (WPBSA rules, simplified):</p>
<ol>
<li>it goes on the <strong>highest-value spot</strong> that is free;</li>
<li>if several colours need spotting, the highest-value one goes first;</li>
<li>if every spot is taken, it goes as near its own spot as possible, between the spot and the top cushion (the short cushion at the black end).</li>
</ol>
<p>So a red sitting on the black spot can send the black somewhere else. Check before you plan the next shot.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="11-When-to-stop-and-play-safe"><a href="#11-When-to-stop-and-play-safe" class="headerlink" title="11 · When to stop and play safe"></a>11 · When to stop and play safe</h2>
<p>Keep going while the next pot is one you make most of the time. Stop when:</p>
<ul>
<li>the only pot left is long, thin, or a plant, and a miss leaves your opponent in (post 11);</li>
<li>the white has run out of position and the next pot is a gamble;</li>
<li>you are well ahead and a safety keeps it that way.</li>
</ul>
<p>A break of 30 and a good safety usually beats a break of 30 and a missed long pot.</p>
