<h1 id="Chapter-2-—-Literals-and-escaping"><a href="#Chapter-2-—-Literals-and-escaping" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 2 — Literals and escaping"></a>Chapter 2 — Literals and escaping</h1>
<p><code>cat</code> matches the three letters <code>c</code>, <code>a</code>, <code>t</code> — anywhere, including inside <code>concatenate</code>. To match a metacharacter literally, put a backslash before it: <code>\.</code> is a real dot, <code>\$</code> a real dollar sign, <code>\(</code> a real parenthesis.</p>
<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line">re.findall(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;\$\d+\.\d{2}&quot;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&quot;Coffee $4.50, cake $12.00&quot;</span>)   <span class="hljs-comment"># [&#x27;$4.50&#x27;, &#x27;$12.00&#x27;]</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>When the text to match comes from a user or a file, <strong>never paste it into a pattern raw</strong>. Escape it: <code>re.escape(text)</code> in Python, <code>[regex]::Escape($text)</code> in PowerShell. Both turn <code>price (USD) $5.00?</code> into <code>price\ \(USD\)\ \$5\.00\?</code>. JavaScript added <code>RegExp.escape()</code> in ECMAScript 2025; older runtimes such as Node.js 20 do not have it yet.</p>

<td><code>[a-z]</code>, <code>[A-Za-z0-9]</code></td>
<td>one character from a range</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>[^0-9]</code></td>
<td>one character that is <strong>not</strong> a digit (<code>^</code> first inside <code>[]</code> means &quot;not&quot;)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>.</code></td>
<td>any character except newline (unless the dot-all flag is on)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>\d</code> / <code>\D</code></td>
<td>a digit / a non-digit</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>\w</code> / <code>\W</code></td>
<td>a &quot;word&quot; character (letter, digit, underscore) / anything else</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>\s</code> / <code>\S</code></td>
<td>whitespace (space, tab, newline…) / anything else</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>[[:digit:]]</code>, <code>[[:space:]]</code>, <code>[[:alpha:]]</code></td>
<td>POSIX classes, for <code>grep</code> and <code>sed</code></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Inside <code>[]</code>, most metacharacters lose their power: <code>[.$]</code> matches a dot or a dollar. Only <code>]</code>, <code>\</code>, <code>^</code> (at the start) and <code>-</code> (between two characters) are special there.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> &quot;digit&quot; is not the same everywhere. In Python (for <code>str</code> patterns) and .NET, <code>\d</code> matches <em>any</em> Unicode decimal digit, including Arabic-Indic <code>٣٤</code>. In JavaScript, <code>\d</code> is only <code>0-9</code>. If you mean ASCII digits, write <code>[0-9]</code> or pass <code>re.ASCII</code> in Python.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Chapter-4-—-Quantifiers-greedy-and-lazy"><a href="#Chapter-4-—-Quantifiers-greedy-and-lazy" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 4 — Quantifiers, greedy and lazy"></a>Chapter 4 — Quantifiers, greedy and lazy</h1>
<p><code>*</code> means 0 or more, <code>+</code> 1 or more, <code>?</code> 0 or 1 (optional), <code>{3}</code> exactly 3, <code>{2,}</code> 2 or more, <code>{2,5}</code> 2 to 5. A quantifier applies to the thing right before it: <code>ab+</code> is <code>a</code> then one or more <code>b</code>; <code>(ab)+</code> repeats <code>ab</code>.</p>
<p><strong>Greedy vs lazy.</strong> By default, quantifiers are <strong>greedy</strong>: they take as much as they can, then give back only if the rest of the pattern fails. Add <code>?</code> to make them <strong>lazy</strong>: take as little as possible.</p>
<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line">html = <span class="hljs-string">&quot;&lt;b&gt;bold&lt;/b&gt;&quot;</span></span><br><span class="line">re.findall(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;&lt;.+&gt;&quot;</span>, html)      <span class="hljs-comment"># [&#x27;&lt;b&gt;bold&lt;/b&gt;&#x27;]   greedy: one huge match</span></span><br><span class="line">re.findall(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;&lt;.+?&gt;&quot;</span>, html)     <span class="hljs-comment"># [&#x27;&lt;b&gt;&#x27;, &#x27;&lt;/b&gt;&#x27;]   lazy: shortest matches</span></span><br><span class="line">re.findall(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;&lt;[^&gt;]+&gt;&quot;</span>, html)   <span class="hljs-comment"># [&#x27;&lt;b&gt;&#x27;, &#x27;&lt;/b&gt;&#x27;]   negated class: often clearest and fastest</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> &quot;anything up to the next <code>X</code>&quot; is best written as <code>[^X]*X</code>. It cannot run past the first <code>X</code>, so it never needs to backtrack.</p>
</blockquote>

<p><code>\bcat\b</code> matches <code>cat</code> in &quot;the cat sat&quot; but not in &quot;concatenate&quot;.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> in Python and .NET, <code>$</code> also matches <em>just before a final newline</em>. <code>re.match(r&quot;^\d+$&quot;, &quot;123\n&quot;)</code> succeeds. In JavaScript, <code>$</code> (without the <code>m</code> flag) means the true end.</p>
</blockquote>
<p><strong>Alternation</strong> <code>|</code> means &quot;or&quot;, and it has the <strong>lowest</strong> priority of all operators. <code>^cat|dog$</code> means &quot;<code>cat</code> at the start&quot; <strong>or</strong> &quot;<code>dog</code> at the end&quot;:</p>
<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">for</span> s <span class="hljs-keyword">in</span> [<span class="hljs-string">&quot;cat food&quot;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&quot;hotdog&quot;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&quot;cat&quot;</span>]:</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-built_in">print</span>(s, <span class="hljs-built_in">bool</span>(re.search(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;^cat|dog$&quot;</span>, s)), <span class="hljs-built_in">bool</span>(re.search(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;^(?:cat|dog)$&quot;</span>, s)))</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment"># cat food True False</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment"># hotdog   True False</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment"># cat      True True</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<h1 id="Chapter-6-—-Groups-backreferences-and-named-groups"><a href="#Chapter-6-—-Groups-backreferences-and-named-groups" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 6 — Groups, backreferences and named groups"></a>Chapter 6 — Groups, backreferences and named groups</h1>
<p>Parentheses do two jobs: they <strong>group</strong> (so a quantifier or <code>|</code> applies to the whole part) and they <strong>capture</strong> (so you can read the matched text later).</p>
<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line">m = re.search(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;(\d{4})-(\d{2})-(\d{2})&quot;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&quot;Due 2026-09-24.&quot;</span>)</span><br><span class="line">m.group(<span class="hljs-number">0</span>), m.group(<span class="hljs-number">1</span>), m.groups()     <span class="hljs-comment"># (&#x27;2026-09-24&#x27;, &#x27;2026&#x27;, (&#x27;2026&#x27;, &#x27;09&#x27;, &#x27;24&#x27;))</span></span><br><span class="line">re.sub(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;(\d{4})-(\d{2})-(\d{2})&quot;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">r&quot;\3/\2/\1&quot;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&quot;Due 2026-09-24.&quot;</span>)   <span class="hljs-comment"># &#x27;Due 24/09/2026.&#x27;</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

</ul>

<pre><code class="language-bash"><figure class="highlight bash"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="bash"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-built_in">echo</span> <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;abc123&#x27;</span> | grep -E <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;\d&#x27;</span>           <span class="hljs-comment"># no match: \d is not a digit here</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-built_in">echo</span> <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;dad 123&#x27;</span> | grep -oE <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;\d&#x27;</span>         <span class="hljs-comment"># prints &quot;d&quot; twice: GNU grep treats \d as a plain d</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-built_in">echo</span> <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;abc123&#x27;</span> | grep -oE <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;[0-9]+&#x27;</span>      <span class="hljs-comment"># 123</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-built_in">echo</span> <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;abc123&#x27;</span> | grep -oP <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;\d+&#x27;</span>         <span class="hljs-comment"># 123 (PCRE)</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-built_in">echo</span> <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;abbbc&#x27;</span> | grep -o <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;ab\+c&#x27;</span>         <span class="hljs-comment"># BRE (GNU): \+ means &quot;one or more&quot;</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Newer GNU grep (3.8 and later) also prints a warning about the &quot;stray&quot; backslash. In Git Bash, <code>grep -P</code> may need a UTF-8 locale: <code>LC_ALL=en_US.UTF-8 grep -P ...</code> (see Dev Essentials 05 · Command Line).</p>
<p>Python needs <strong>raw strings</strong>. In a normal string, <code>&quot;\n&quot;</code> is a newline and <code>&quot;\b&quot;</code> is a backspace character, so the regex engine never sees the backslash. Write <code>r&quot;\b\d+\b&quot;</code>. The <code>re</code> docs themselves recommend raw string notation for patterns.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-12-—-Dont-fully-validate-email-with-a-regex"><a href="#Chapter-12-—-Dont-fully-validate-email-with-a-regex" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 12 — Don't fully validate email with a regex"></a>Chapter 12 — Don't fully validate email with a regex</h1>
<p>Real email syntax (RFC 5322) allows quoted local parts, comments and other rare forms, so a &quot;complete&quot; regex is huge, hard to read and still does not tell you whether the mailbox <strong>exists</strong>. The HTML standard itself does not follow RFC 5322 for <code>&lt;input type=&quot;email&quot;&gt;</code>: it calls its own simpler rule a &quot;willful violation&quot; of that RFC, because the RFC grammar is too strict in some places and too loose in others to be practical.</p>
<p>A sensible approach:</p>
<ol>
<li>Do a <strong>light sanity check</strong>: one <code>@</code>, no spaces, a dot in the domain part.</li>
<li>Send a <strong>confirmation email</strong>. Only a click on the link proves the address works.</li>
</ol>
<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line">EMAIL_SANITY = re.<span class="hljs-built_in">compile</span>(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;[^@\s]+@[^@\s]+\.[^@\s]+&quot;</span>)</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">for</span> e <span class="hljs-keyword">in</span> [<span class="hljs-string">&quot;ada@example.com&quot;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&quot;a.b+tag@sub.example.co.uk&quot;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&quot;no-at.example.com&quot;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&quot;two@@example.com&quot;</span>]:</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-built_in">print</span>(e, <span class="hljs-built_in">bool</span>(EMAIL_SANITY.fullmatch(e)))</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment"># True, True, False, False</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>This check rejects some legal but odd addresses (such as <code>user@localhost</code>) and accepts some that bounce. That is fine: its only job is to catch typos early.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-13-—-Catastrophic-backtracking-and-ReDoS"><a href="#Chapter-13-—-Catastrophic-backtracking-and-ReDoS" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 13 — Catastrophic backtracking and ReDoS"></a>Chapter 13 — Catastrophic backtracking and ReDoS</h1>
<p>Most engines (Python, JavaScript, .NET, PCRE, Java) are <strong>backtracking</strong> engines. When part of a pattern fails, they go back and try the other ways the earlier parts could have matched. Usually there are only a few ways. But <strong>nested or overlapping quantifiers</strong> create an exponential number of ways, and on text that <em>almost</em> matches, the engine tries them all.</p>
<p>The textbook case is <code>(a+)+$</code> against <code>aaaa…ab</code>. The inner and outer <code>+</code> can split the <code>a</code>s in about 2ⁿ ways, and the final <code>b</code> makes every split fail. A timing on one Windows 11 laptop with Python 3.11.9 (one run each — an informal measurement, not a benchmark; your numbers will differ, the doubling will not):</p>
<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">import</span> re, time</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">for</span> n <span class="hljs-keyword">in</span> (<span class="hljs-number">18</span>, <span class="hljs-number">20</span>, <span class="hljs-number">22</span>, <span class="hljs-number">24</span>):</span><br><span class="line">    s = <span class="hljs-string">&quot;a&quot;</span> * n + <span class="hljs-string">&quot;b&quot;</span></span><br><span class="line">    t = time.perf_counter()</span><br><span class="line">    re.<span class="hljs-keyword">match</span>(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;(a+)+$&quot;</span>, s)</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-built_in">print</span>(n, <span class="hljs-string">f&quot;<span class="hljs-subst">{time.perf_counter() - t:<span class="hljs-number">.3</span>f}</span>s&quot;</span>)</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<p>Every 2 extra characters cost about 4 times more. Extrapolating, 40 characters would take more than ten hours. That is <strong>ReDoS</strong> (regular expression denial of service): one short, crafted input keeps a CPU core busy. OWASP lists typical &quot;evil&quot; shapes: <code>(a+)+</code>, <code>([a-zA-Z]+)*</code>, <code>(a|aa)+</code>, <code>(a|a?)+</code>. Real-life versions look innocent, like <code>^(\w+\s?)*$</code> for &quot;words separated by spaces&quot; — in the same test, 22 letters plus a <code>!</code> took 0.25 s.</p>
<p>This is not theory. On 2 July 2019, one Cloudflare WAF rule with a badly backtracking regex (the part <code>.*(?:.*=.*)</code>) exhausted CPUs across Cloudflare's network and caused about 27 minutes of downtime.</p>
<h2 id="Fixes-from-best-to-last-resort"><a href="#Fixes-from-best-to-last-resort" class="headerlink" title="Fixes, from best to last resort"></a>Fixes, from best to last resort</h2>
<p><strong>1. Rewrite the pattern so each character can be matched only one way.</strong></p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Evil</th>
<th>Safe</th>
<th>Why</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><code>(a+)+$</code></td>
<td><code>a+$</code></td>
<td>the nesting added nothing</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>^(\d+)*$</code></td>
<td><code>^\d*$</code></td>
<td>same language, one way to split</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>^(\w+\s?)*$</code></td>
<td><code>^(?:\w+\s)*\w*$</code></td>
<td>each repeat must end with a space, so splits are fixed</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>On 100,000 characters, the rewritten <code>^(?:\w+\s)*\w*$</code> finished in about 1 ms in the same test. It also gave the same yes/no answer as the original on every test string tried (<code>&quot;hello world&quot;</code>, <code>&quot;hello  world&quot;</code>, <code>&quot;hello world &quot;</code>, <code>&quot;&quot;</code>, <code>&quot; lead&quot;</code>, <code>&quot;x!&quot;</code>).</p>
<p><strong>2. Forbid backtracking into a part</strong> with an atomic group <code>(?&gt;...)</code> or a possessive quantifier (<code>*+</code>, <code>++</code>, <code>?+</code>). Once matched, the engine will not try other splits. Python supports both since 3.11; PCRE and Java support both; .NET supports atomic groups only; JavaScript supports neither.</p>
<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line">re.<span class="hljs-keyword">match</span>(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;(?&gt;a+)+$&quot;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&quot;a&quot;</span> * <span class="hljs-number">100_000</span> + <span class="hljs-string">&quot;b&quot;</span>)    <span class="hljs-comment"># returns None at once (Python 3.11+)</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p><strong>3. Use a linear-time engine for untrusted patterns or input.</strong> Google's RE2, Go's <code>regexp</code> and Rust's <code>regex</code> crate guarantee time linear in the input size (Rust documents O(m × n) for pattern size m and text size n). The price: no backreferences and no lookarounds. .NET 7 and later offer <code>RegexOptions.NonBacktracking</code> with the same kind of guarantee; Windows PowerShell 5.1 runs on .NET Framework and does not have it. After its outage, Cloudflare planned to move to RE2 or the Rust engine for exactly this reason.</p>
<p><strong>4. Limit the damage.</strong> Cap input length before matching. In .NET, pass a timeout — the default is infinite:</p>
<pre><code class="language-powershell"><figure class="highlight powershell"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="powershell"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-variable">$re</span> = [<span class="hljs-type">regex</span>]::new(<span class="hljs-string">&#x27;^(a+)+$&#x27;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;None&#x27;</span>, [<span class="hljs-type">TimeSpan</span>]::FromMilliseconds(<span class="hljs-number">100</span>))</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-variable">$re</span>.IsMatch((<span class="hljs-string">&#x27;a&#x27;</span> * <span class="hljs-number">40</span>) + <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;b&#x27;</span>)     <span class="hljs-comment"># error after ~100 ms: &quot;The RegEx engine has timed out...&quot;</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Python's built-in <code>re</code> has no timeout, so rewrite the pattern or run risky matching in a separate process you can stop.</p>

<h2 id="Exercise-1-—-Real-dates-only"><a href="#Exercise-1-—-Real-dates-only" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 1 — Real dates only"></a>Exercise 1 — Real dates only</h2>
<p>From <code>&quot;Released 2026-03-14, patched 2026-04-02. Ticket 2026-99-99 is a typo. Next review: 2026-10-01.&quot;</code>, extract the dates with valid month and day ranges. Make sure <code>findall</code> returns whole dates, not tuples.</p>
<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line">text = <span class="hljs-string">&quot;Released 2026-03-14, patched 2026-04-02. Next review: 2026-10-01.&quot;</span></span><br><span class="line">re.findall(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;\b\d{4}-(?:0[1-9]|1[0-2])-(?:0[1-9]|[12]\d|3[01])\b&quot;</span>, text)</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment"># [&#x27;2026-03-14&#x27;, &#x27;2026-04-02&#x27;, &#x27;2026-10-01&#x27;]</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

</details>
<h2 id="Exercise-2-—-Client-errors-per-path"><a href="#Exercise-2-—-Client-errors-per-path" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 2 — Client errors per path"></a>Exercise 2 — Client errors per path</h2>

<details>

<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">from</span> collections <span class="hljs-keyword">import</span> Counter</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">from</span> pathlib <span class="hljs-keyword">import</span> Path</span><br><span class="line">log = Path(<span class="hljs-string">&quot;access.log&quot;</span>).read_text(encoding=<span class="hljs-string">&quot;utf-8&quot;</span>)</span><br><span class="line">pat = re.<span class="hljs-built_in">compile</span>(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;^\S+ \S+ (?P&lt;method&gt;[A-Z]+) (?P&lt;path&gt;\S+) (?P&lt;status&gt;4\d\d) \d+$&quot;</span>, re.M)</span><br><span class="line">Counter(m[<span class="hljs-string">&quot;path&quot;</span>] <span class="hljs-keyword">for</span> m <span class="hljs-keyword">in</span> pat.finditer(log))</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment"># Counter({&#x27;/login&#x27;: 1, &#x27;/missing&#x27;: 1, &#x27;/admin&#x27;: 1})</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<h2 id="Exercise-3-—-Last-First-to-First-Last"><a href="#Exercise-3-—-Last-First-to-First-Last" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 3 — &quot;Last, First&quot; to &quot;First Last&quot;"></a>Exercise 3 — &quot;Last, First&quot; to &quot;First Last&quot;</h2>
<p>Turn <code>Lovelace, Ada</code> / <code>Hopper, Grace</code> / <code>Torvalds, Linus</code> (one per line) into <code>Ada Lovelace</code> etc. — in Python, PowerShell and <code>sed</code>.</p>
<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line">names = <span class="hljs-string">&quot;Lovelace, Ada\nHopper, Grace\nTorvalds, Linus&quot;</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-built_in">print</span>(re.sub(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;^(\w+),\s*(\w+)$&quot;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">r&quot;\2 \1&quot;</span>, names, flags=re.M))</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<pre><code class="language-powershell"><figure class="highlight powershell"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="powershell"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-string">&#x27;Lovelace, Ada&#x27;</span> <span class="hljs-operator">-replace</span> <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;^(\w+),\s*(\w+)$&#x27;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;$2 $1&#x27;</span>      <span class="hljs-comment"># Ada Lovelace</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<pre><code class="language-bash"><figure class="highlight bash"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="bash"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-built_in">printf</span> <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;Lovelace, Ada\nHopper, Grace\n&#x27;</span> | sed -E <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;s/^([A-Za-z]+), *([A-Za-z]+)$/\2 \1/&#x27;</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p><code>sed -E</code> has no <code>\w</code> or <code>\s</code> in POSIX terms, so the bracket classes spell it out.</p>
</details>
<h2 id="Exercise-4-—-Frontmatter-title-CRLF-safe"><a href="#Exercise-4-—-Frontmatter-title-CRLF-safe" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 4 — Frontmatter title, CRLF-safe"></a>Exercise 4 — Frontmatter title, CRLF-safe</h2>
<p>Get the title from <code>&quot;---\r\ntitle: Regex from Zero to Useful\r\ndate: 2026-09-24 10:30:00\r\n---\r\nBody text\r\n&quot;</code> without a trailing <code>\r</code>.</p>
<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line">post = <span class="hljs-string">&quot;---\r\ntitle: Regex from Zero to Useful\r\ndate: 2026-09-24 10:30:00\r\n---\r\nBody text\r\n&quot;</span></span><br><span class="line">re.search(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;^title:\s*(.+?)\s*$&quot;</span>, post, re.M).group(<span class="hljs-number">1</span>)   <span class="hljs-comment"># &#x27;Regex from Zero to Useful&#x27;</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The lazy <code>(.+?)</code> stops as early as it can, and <code>\s*$</code> swallows the <code>\r</code> before the line end. The naive <code>^title: (.+)$</code> returns <code>'Regex from Zero to Useful\r'</code>.</p>
</details>
<h2 id="Exercise-5-—-Defuse-a-slow-pattern"><a href="#Exercise-5-—-Defuse-a-slow-pattern" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 5 — Defuse a slow pattern"></a>Exercise 5 — Defuse a slow pattern</h2>
<p><code>^(\d+)*$</code> is used to check &quot;only digits&quot;. Time it on <code>&quot;1&quot; * 24 + &quot;x&quot;</code>, then rewrite it.</p>
<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">import</span> time</span><br><span class="line">s = <span class="hljs-string">&quot;1&quot;</span> * <span class="hljs-number">24</span> + <span class="hljs-string">&quot;x&quot;</span></span><br><span class="line">t = time.perf_counter(); re.<span class="hljs-keyword">match</span>(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;^(\d+)*$&quot;</span>, s); <span class="hljs-built_in">print</span>(time.perf_counter() - t)   <span class="hljs-comment"># ~0.5 s on the test laptop</span></span><br><span class="line">t = time.perf_counter(); re.<span class="hljs-keyword">match</span>(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;^\d*$&quot;</span>, s);    <span class="hljs-built_in">print</span>(time.perf_counter() - t)   <span class="hljs-comment"># microseconds</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p><code>^\d*$</code> accepts exactly the same strings, with one way to match each. Better still for this job: <code>re.fullmatch(r&quot;[0-9]+&quot;, s)</code> — ASCII digits only, at least one, and nothing after (not even a newline).</p>
</details>
<h2 id="Exercise-6-—-CSS-hex-colors"><a href="#Exercise-6-—-CSS-hex-colors" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 6 — CSS hex colors"></a>Exercise 6 — CSS hex colors</h2>
<p>In <code>&quot;color: #fff; bg: #1a2b3c; bad: #ffff; also #ABCDEF; nope #12345678.&quot;</code>, match 3- or 6-digit hex colors only.</p>
<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
<pre><code class="language-python"><figure class="highlight python"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="python"><span class="line">css = <span class="hljs-string">&quot;color: #fff; bg: #1a2b3c; bad: #ffff; also #ABCDEF; nope #12345678.&quot;</span></span><br><span class="line">re.findall(<span class="hljs-string">r&quot;#(?:[0-9a-fA-F]{3}){1,2}\b&quot;</span>, css)     <span class="hljs-comment"># [&#x27;#fff&#x27;, &#x27;#1a2b3c&#x27;, &#x27;#ABCDEF&#x27;]</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The <code>\b</code> at the end stops <code>#ffff</code> and <code>#12345678</code> from matching a shorter prefix.</p>
</details>
