11:T2b0f,<p>Every escape, double and positional shot uses a cushion. The simple rule, &quot;angle in equals angle out&quot;, is a good start, but pace and spin bend it. This lesson shows when the rule works, how far each kind of hit bends it, what running and check side do, and how to pot a ball that sits on the cushion.</p>
<blockquote>

<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Find a cushion point with the <strong>mirror point</strong>.</li>
<li>Know which hits follow the mirror line and which bend off it.</li>
<li>Use <strong>running side</strong> to widen the angle and <strong>check side</strong> to narrow it.</li>
<li>Pot a ball frozen on the cushion by hitting the cushion and the ball together.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="1-The-mirror-point-and-when-it-works"><a href="#1-The-mirror-point-and-when-it-works" class="headerlink" title="1 · The mirror point, and when it works"></a>1 · The mirror point, and when it works</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b07_mirror.webp" alt="Three top views of the white sent off the top side cushion to a green ring. A faint ring behind the cushion is the mirror point. 1: centre ball, power 3, aimed at the mirror point: the white comes off a little flatter and runs past the ring, cross. 2: the same aim, power 4: the white goes through the ring, tick. 3: power 3 with the aim moved shorter along the cushion: through the ring, tick"></p>
<p>To send the white off a cushion to a spot: imagine the spot reflected behind the cushion (the <strong>mirror point</strong>), and aim at it. Where the aiming line crosses the cushion is your cushion point.</p>
<p>That works when the white is <strong>still sliding</strong> when it reaches the cushion. A white that is already <strong>rolling</strong> comes off a little flatter and runs on along the cushion.</p>

<p>Two good answers: play it firmer so the white still slides, or keep the slow pace and aim shorter. Choose by where you want the white to stop.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> the mirror point is not on the cushion rubber but where the white's <strong>centre</strong> would touch it, half a ball out from the cushion. Measure from there.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>So: <strong>a rolling white comes off flatter</strong> (the slower, the flatter), <strong>a stun follows the mirror</strong>, and <strong>backspin makes it steeper and bends it away</strong>. The cushion meets the ball a little above its middle, and that takes most of the spin off during the hit, so the bends are small; the angle off the cushion is what changes.</p>

<h1 id="3-Running-side-and-check-side"><a href="#3-Running-side-and-check-side" class="headerlink" title="3 · Running side and check side"></a>3 · Running side and check side</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b07_side.webp" alt="Top view of one line into the top side cushion at 60 degrees and three tracks off it, each with a face showing the tip spot. Right-hand side: the flattest track, running along the table. Centre: in the middle. Left-hand side: the steepest track"></p>
<p>Side changes the angle off the cushion:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Running side</strong> makes the white run on: it comes off <strong>wider</strong> (flatter to the cushion) and keeps more pace.</li>
<li><strong>Check side</strong> holds it: it comes off <strong>narrower</strong> (squarer) and slower.</li>
</ul>
<p>Which is which? Look at the way the white will turn off the cushion. Here it turns <strong>right</strong>, so <strong>right-hand side</strong> is running side.</p>

<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> side matters most when you come into the cushion <strong>squarer</strong>. On a shallow line in (30°), running side changed the angle by 4° and check side by only 1°.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="4-Watch-it-running-side-opens-the-angle"><a href="#4-Watch-it-running-side-opens-the-angle" class="headerlink" title="4 · Watch it: running side opens the angle"></a>4 · Watch it: running side opens the angle</h1>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b07_clip_side.webp" alt="Clip: the white is played into the top side cushion with right-hand running side at power 3. It comes off wide and runs on down the table towards the far cushion"></div>
<p>Right-hand side, power 3. Off the cushion, the white runs on down the table instead of coming back across.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="5-A-ball-on-the-cushion"><a href="#5-A-ball-on-the-cushion" class="headerlink" title="5 · A ball on the cushion"></a>5 · A ball on the cushion</h1>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b07_clip_rail.webp" alt="Clip: a red sits against the top side cushion 70 cm from the corner pocket. A yellow mark shows the point on the cushion just in front of it. The white hits the cushion and the red at the same moment, and the red runs along the cushion into the corner pocket"></div>
<p>A red frozen on the cushion is hard to pot along it. If the white hits the red first, the red bounces off the cushion and away from the pocket.</p>
<p>Hit the <strong>cushion and the red at the same moment</strong> (the cushion a hair first is fine). Then the cushion pushes the white into the red and the red stays on the rail. In the clip, power 3, the white's centre aims about 4 cm to the cushion side of the red's centre, and the window is tiny.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Common pitfall:</strong> playing it soft &quot;to be safe&quot;. In the model, at power 2 no aim potted this red; at power 3 the &quot;together&quot; aim did. Medium pace helps the red hold the cushion line.</p>
</blockquote>
