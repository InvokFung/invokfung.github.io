Billiards & Snooker — 07 · Cushions: the Angle In and the Angle Out

Created2026-08-23|Updated2026-08-23|Sports > Billiards
|Post View:

Every escape, double and positional shot uses a cushion. The simple rule, "angle in equals angle out", is a good start, but pace and spin bend it. This lesson shows when the rule works, how far each kind of hit bends it, what running and check side do, and how to pot a ball that sits on the cushion.

Learning Outcome

  • Find a cushion point with the mirror point.
  • Know which hits follow the mirror line and which bend off it.
  • Use running side to widen the angle and check side to narrow it.
  • Pot a ball frozen on the cushion by hitting the cushion and the ball together.

1 · The mirror point, and when it works

To send the white off a cushion to a spot: imagine the spot reflected behind the cushion (the mirror point), and aim at it. Where the aiming line crosses the cushion is your cushion point.

That works when the white is still sliding when it reaches the cushion. A white that is already rolling comes off a little flatter and runs on along the cushion.

Hit Where it goes (model, 80 cm to the cushion)
1 · power 3, centre ball (rolling by the cushion) about 12 cm past the target ✗
2 · the same aim, power 4 (still sliding) within 4 cm ✓
3 · power 3, aim about 5 cm shorter along the cushion on target ✓

Two good answers: play it firmer so the white still slides, or keep the slow pace and aim shorter. Choose by where you want the white to stop.

Trick: the mirror point is not on the cushion rubber but where the white's centre would touch it, half a ball out from the cushion. Measure from there.

2 · Pace and spin bend the line

The same line in at 45°, four hits:

Hit Off the cushion at… Then
slow roll (power 2, a little above centre) 35° almost straight
firm roll (power 4, a little above centre) 39° straight
stun (power 3, below centre, sliding at the cushion) 47° straight: angle in = angle out
screw (power 4, below centre, still spinning back) 48° bends away, to 52°

So: a rolling white comes off flatter (the slower, the flatter), a stun follows the mirror, and backspin makes it steeper and bends it away. The cushion meets the ball a little above its middle, and that takes most of the spin off during the hit, so the bends are small; the angle off the cushion is what changes.

3 · Running side and check side

Side changes the angle off the cushion:

  • Running side makes the white run on: it comes off wider (flatter to the cushion) and keeps more pace.
  • Check side holds it: it comes off narrower (squarer) and slower.

Which is which? Look at the way the white will turn off the cushion. Here it turns right, so right-hand side is running side.

60° in, power 3 Off at…
check side (left) 62°
centre 55°
running side (right) 33°

Watch out: side matters most when you come into the cushion squarer. On a shallow line in (30°), running side changed the angle by 4° and check side by only 1°.

4 · Watch it: running side opens the angle

Right-hand side, power 3. Off the cushion, the white runs on down the table instead of coming back across.

5 · A ball on the cushion

A red frozen on the cushion is hard to pot along it. If the white hits the red first, the red bounces off the cushion and away from the pocket.

Hit the cushion and the red at the same moment (the cushion a hair first is fine). Then the cushion pushes the white into the red and the red stays on the rail. In the clip, power 3, the white's centre aims about 4 cm to the cushion side of the red's centre, and the window is tiny.

Common pitfall: playing it soft "to be safe". In the model, at power 2 no aim potted this red; at power 3 the "together" aim did. Medium pace helps the red hold the cushion line.

6 · Try it at the table

Drill Set-up Look for
Mirror point the white in the D; a coin on the table; off one side cushion to the coin first with power 4, then power 3 aimed shorter
Four hits the same line in at 45°, slow roll, firm roll, stun, screw how far each one bends
Side ladder the white into a side cushion at 60°, left, centre, right side three clearly different angles off
Frozen red a red on the side cushion 70 cm from the corner the red stays on the cushion

Cheat-sheet
Mirror point reflect the target half a ball behind the cushion; aim at it
Works best when the white still slides at the cushion (firmer, or below centre)
Rolling white comes off a little flatter; aim a touch shorter
Backspin comes off steeper and bends away
Running side wider, keeps pace (the side towards the way it turns)
Check side narrower, slower
Ball on cushion cushion and ball together, medium pace
