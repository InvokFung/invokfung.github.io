11:T2317,<p>A thin cut is the white just kissing the side of a ball and sending it off almost sideways, sometimes nearly at a right angle. It looks like magic and feels scary. In this lesson you'll see how thin &quot;thin&quot; really is, two ways to aim it, why it needs more pace, and where the white goes afterwards.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Series map. <em>★ Basics:</em> 00 Aiming → 01 The hit → 02 Stop, follow, screw → 03 Where the white goes · <em>★★ Club:</em> <strong>04 Thin cuts</strong> (this post) → 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker · <em>★★★ Advanced:</em> 10 Plants and sets → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → 13 Position · <em>★★★★ Hard:</em> 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → 15 Swerve and special spots.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>See how small the overlap is on a very thin cut.</li>
<li>Aim a thin cut two ways: ghost ball, or edge to edge.</li>
<li>Give the shot enough pace, and allow for a small &quot;drag&quot; (throw).</li>
<li>Know where the white runs afterwards.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h1 id="1-How-thin-is-thin"><a href="#1-How-thin-is-thin" class="headerlink" title="1 · How thin is thin?"></a>1 · How thin is thin?</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b04_overlap.webp" alt="Top row: the view down the cue for three thin cuts, with the see-through ghost ball touching the red from the side; labels 7 mm, 3 mm and under 1 mm show how much the white overlaps the red. Bottom row: from above, the red goes off at a wider and wider angle, shown by a yellow wedge"></p>
<p>Down the cue, the white covers only a sliver of the red:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>The red turns…</th>
<th>The white overlaps it by…</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>two-thirds of a right angle (60°)</td>
<td>about 7 mm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>(70°)</td>
<td>about 3 mm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>almost a right angle (80°)</td>
<td><strong>less than 1 mm</strong></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>So a very thin cut is a &quot;nearly miss&quot;. A tiny aim error on a thin cut changes the angle a lot (post 00, picture 8).</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="2-Two-ways-to-aim-it"><a href="#2-Two-ways-to-aim-it" class="headerlink" title="2 · Two ways to aim it"></a>2 · Two ways to aim it</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b04_two_ways.webp" alt="Two close-up top views of the same thin cut. 1: the ghost ball touches the red from the side, and the white arrow points at the ghost's centre, marked with a plus. 2: a yellow dashed line runs from the white's edge to just inside the red's edge"></p>
<p>Use whichever your eye likes; many players check one with the other.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Ghost ball.</strong> It still works: put the ghost against the red's far side, and aim the white's <strong>centre</strong> at the ghost's <strong>centre</strong>. On thin cuts the ghost sits almost a whole ball out to the side.</li>
<li><strong>Edge to edge.</strong> Line up the white's <strong>edge</strong> to pass <strong>just inside</strong> the red's edge. For a near right angle, &quot;just inside&quot; means less than 1 mm.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> on very thin cuts, stand up and look from the side first. Decide how far out the ghost is, then get down and keep that picture.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="3-Watch-it-almost-sideways"><a href="#3-Watch-it-almost-sideways" class="headerlink" title="3 · Watch it: almost sideways"></a>3 · Watch it: almost sideways</h1>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b04_clip_thin.webp" alt="Clip: the red is near a side cushion, not far from a corner pocket; the white comes from below. The white barely touches the red's left side, the red runs to the right along the cushion into the corner pocket, and the white carries on almost straight to the side cushion and comes back"></div>
<p>The white comes from the left of the red and only grazes it. The red goes off <strong>to the right</strong>, about 76° from the white's line, and runs into the corner ✓. The white hardly notices and carries on.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> the red gets only a small part of the white's speed (here about a quarter). This pot needs <strong>power 4</strong>. At power 2 or 3 the red stops short of the pocket.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="4-Slow-cuts-get-dragged-a-little"><a href="#4-Slow-cuts-get-dragged-a-little" class="headerlink" title="4 · Slow cuts get dragged a little"></a>4 · Slow cuts get dragged a little</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b04_throw.webp" alt="Two top views of the same 60° cut with the white aimed at the ghost ball. A white dashed line shows where the red should go; a red line shows where it really goes, a few degrees fuller. At power 2 the gap is about 6 cm after one metre; at power 4 about 1.5 cm"></p>
<p>At the moment of contact, the white rubs on the red and drags it a little the way the white is moving. This is called <strong>throw</strong>. The red goes a touch <strong>fuller</strong> (less cut) than the ghost-ball line, more so on slow shots.</p>
<p>If your slow cuts keep missing on the thick side, three fixes all work:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Fix</th>
<th>How</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Overcut a hair</td>
<td>aim a fraction thinner than the ghost ball</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Add pace</td>
<td>a firmer hit drags less (power 4: about 1.5 cm off at 1 m instead of 6 cm)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Side</td>
<td>a touch of side the right way cancels it (post 05)</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<hr>
<h1 id="5-Where-the-white-goes"><a href="#5-Where-the-white-goes" class="headerlink" title="5 · Where the white goes"></a>5 · Where the white goes</h1>
<p>On a thin cut the white keeps <strong>most of its speed</strong> and goes on almost straight (look at the clip again). Before you play, check that path:</p>
<ul>
<li>Is there a pocket on it? A thin cut can send the white in-off.</li>
<li>Will it hit another ball?</li>
<li>Where does it stop after the cushions?</li>
</ul>
<p>Spin changes little here, because so little of the white's motion is passed on. <strong>Pace</strong> is your main control.</p>
