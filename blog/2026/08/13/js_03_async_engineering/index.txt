<h1 id="Chapter-1-—-Promises-as-a-state-machine"><a href="#Chapter-1-—-Promises-as-a-state-machine" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 1 — Promises as a state machine"></a>Chapter 1 — Promises as a state machine</h1>
<h2 id="Terminology"><a href="#Terminology" class="headerlink" title="Terminology"></a>Terminology</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pending / fulfilled / rejected</strong> — the three states. A promise leaves <em>pending</em> at most once.</li>
<li><strong>Settled</strong> — fulfilled or rejected. <strong>Resolved</strong> is different: a promise is resolved once its fate is locked in, which can happen while it is still pending (resolved <em>to</em> another promise).</li>
<li><strong>Thenable</strong> — any object with a callable <code>then</code> method. Promise machinery adopts thenables, which is why <code>resolve(otherPromise)</code> does not fulfil with a promise value.</li>
<li><strong>Reaction</strong> — a callback registered with <code>then</code>/<code>catch</code>/<code>finally</code>. Reactions always run as microtasks, never synchronously.</li>
<li><strong>Handled</strong> — a rejected promise that has at least one rejection reaction attached. The host tracks this to report unhandled rejections.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="Resolution-is-not-fulfilment"><a href="#Resolution-is-not-fulfilment" class="headerlink" title="Resolution is not fulfilment"></a>Resolution is not fulfilment</h2>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">const</span> inner = <span class="hljs-keyword">new</span> <span class="hljs-title class_">Promise</span>(<span class="hljs-function">(<span class="hljs-params">r</span>) =&gt;</span> <span class="hljs-built_in">setTimeout</span>(<span class="hljs-function">() =&gt;</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">r</span>(<span class="hljs-string">&#x27;late&#x27;</span>), <span class="hljs-number">100</span>));</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">const</span> outer = <span class="hljs-keyword">new</span> <span class="hljs-title class_">Promise</span>(<span class="hljs-function">(<span class="hljs-params">resolve</span>) =&gt;</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">resolve</span>(inner)); <span class="hljs-comment">// resolved now, pending for 100 ms,</span></span><br><span class="line">                                                        <span class="hljs-comment">// then fulfils with &#x27;late&#x27;, not with `inner`</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Resolving with a thenable queues a job that calls <code>thenable.then(resolve, reject)</code>. That job costs extra microtask ticks, which matters for ordering puzzles and for the <code>return</code> vs <code>return await</code> discussion in Chapter 3.</p>
<h2 id="The-four-combinators"><a href="#The-four-combinators" class="headerlink" title="The four combinators"></a>The four combinators</h2>
<p>All four take an iterable and return one promise. They differ in what &quot;done&quot; means.</p>
<table>

<p>The most important row is the one that is <em>not</em> in the table: <strong>no combinator cancels the losers</strong>. When <code>Promise.all</code> rejects at 20 ms, <code>A</code> and <code>C</code> keep running, their sockets stay open, and their results are thrown away. Cancellation is a separate mechanism (Chapter 4), and you have to wire it in yourself.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Common pitfall:</strong> <code>Promise.race([work(), timeout(5000)])</code> is not a timeout. It stops <em>waiting</em> after 5 s. The work carries on, holding its connection and memory, and its eventual rejection may even become unhandled.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Chapter-2-—-Unhandled-rejections"><a href="#Chapter-2-—-Unhandled-rejections" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 2 — Unhandled rejections"></a>Chapter 2 — Unhandled rejections</h1>
<p>A rejection is &quot;unhandled&quot; if no rejection reaction is attached by the time the host checks. Hosts check after the microtask queue drains, so &quot;attached later&quot; means &quot;attached in a later task&quot;.</p>

<thead>
<tr>
<th>Host</th>
<th>Behaviour</th>
</tr>

<tr>
<td>Browsers</td>
<td>Fire <code>unhandledrejection</code> on <code>window</code> (or the worker global). If a handler is attached later, fire <code>rejectionhandled</code>. The page keeps running.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Node 15+</td>
<td>Default mode <code>--unhandled-rejections=throw</code>: emit <code>process.on('unhandledRejection')</code>; if nothing listens, raise it as an uncaught exception and <strong>exit the process</strong>.</td>

<p>Three consequences follow directly from this shape:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>The body runs synchronously until the first <code>await</code>.</strong> Code before it is not deferred. A throw there still becomes a rejection, not a synchronous exception.</li>
<li><strong>Every <code>await</code> suspends, even on a non-promise.</strong> <code>await 42</code> still resumes in a microtask. Its cost is small, but never zero.</li>
<li><strong>The call stack is gone after the first <code>await</code>.</strong> The resumed frame is called from the microtask checkpoint. V8 rebuilds &quot;async stack traces&quot; from the promise chain, which only works when the chain is made of <code>await</code>s in <code>async</code> functions.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="What-an-await-costs"><a href="#What-an-await-costs" class="headerlink" title="What an await costs"></a>What an await costs</h2>
<p>The V8 team's 2018 work (and the spec change that followed) cut the cost of <code>await</code> on a native promise from at least three microtask ticks and two extra promises down to one tick in the common case. That makes micro-optimising <code>await</code> pointless in application code. What still costs real time:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Sequential awaits that could be parallel.</strong> <code>await a(); await b();</code> takes <code>a + b</code>. <code>await Promise.all([a(), b()])</code> takes <code>max(a, b)</code>. This one mistake explains a large share of slow API handlers.</li>
<li><strong><code>await</code> inside hot loops over in-memory data.</strong> Each iteration becomes a microtask. Process synchronously, and only <code>await</code> at I/O boundaries.</li>
<li><strong>Needless <code>async</code> wrappers</strong> around functions that return a promise already. Each one adds a promise and a hop.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="return-promise-vs-return-await-promise"><a href="#return-promise-vs-return-await-promise" class="headerlink" title="return promise vs return await promise"></a><code>return promise</code> vs <code>return await promise</code></h2>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">async</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">function</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">a</span>(<span class="hljs-params"></span>) { <span class="hljs-keyword">try</span> { <span class="hljs-keyword">return</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">fetchThing</span>(); } <span class="hljs-keyword">catch</span> { <span class="hljs-keyword">return</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">fallback</span>(); } }</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">// a(): the try/catch does NOT see a rejection from fetchThing()</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">async</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">function</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">b</span>(<span class="hljs-params"></span>) { <span class="hljs-keyword">try</span> { <span class="hljs-keyword">return</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">await</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">fetchThing</span>(); } <span class="hljs-keyword">catch</span> { <span class="hljs-keyword">return</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">fallback</span>(); } }</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">// b(): the rejection is thrown at the await and caught</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

The old lint rule <code>no-return-await</code> was deprecated in ESLint for these reasons. typescript-eslint's <code>return-await</code> rule, with its default <code>in-try-catch</code> option, encodes the correct policy.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-4-—-Cancellation-with-AbortSignal"><a href="#Chapter-4-—-Cancellation-with-AbortSignal" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 4 — Cancellation with AbortSignal"></a>Chapter 4 — Cancellation with AbortSignal</h1>
<p>JavaScript has no way to cancel a promise, and that is deliberate. A promise is a read-only view of a result, so cancelling the <em>view</em> does not stop the <em>work</em>. The platform's answer is a separate capability object: an <code>AbortController</code> owns an <code>AbortSignal</code>, and you pass the signal into every operation that should stop.</p>
<h2 id="Terminology-1"><a href="#Terminology-1" class="headerlink" title="Terminology"></a>Terminology</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong><code>AbortController</code></strong> — the writer side. <code>controller.abort(reason)</code> aborts its signal once. Later calls do nothing.</li>
<li><strong><code>AbortSignal</code></strong> — the reader side: <code>aborted</code>, <code>reason</code>, <code>throwIfAborted()</code>, and an <code>abort</code> event.</li>
<li><strong>Reason</strong> — any value. It defaults to a <code>DOMException</code> named <code>AbortError</code>. <code>AbortSignal.timeout(ms)</code> aborts with a <code>DOMException</code> named <code>TimeoutError</code>.</li>
<li><strong>Composite signal</strong> — <code>AbortSignal.any([s1, s2, …])</code> aborts as soon as any input aborts, with that input's reason.</li>
</ul>

Many APIs accept a signal directly: <code>fetch</code>, <code>addEventListener</code> (the listener is removed on abort), Node's <code>timers/promises</code>, <code>events.once</code>, <code>fs.readFile</code>, <code>child_process</code>, <code>stream.pipeline</code>, and most modern database drivers.</p>
<h2 id="Making-your-own-functions-abortable"><a href="#Making-your-own-functions-abortable" class="headerlink" title="Making your own functions abortable"></a>Making your own functions abortable</h2>

<p>Rules for a well-behaved abortable function:</p>
<ol>
<li>Check <code>signal.aborted</code> on entry, since the signal may already be aborted.</li>
<li>Reject with <code>signal.reason</code>, not with a new error, so that callers can tell a timeout from a user cancel.</li>
<li>Remove the <code>abort</code> listener on the success path. A long-lived signal (a whole page's lifetime, say) with thousands of leftover listeners is a real memory leak, and Node warns once a signal has more than the default maximum number of listeners.</li>
<li>Pass the signal down to everything you call.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> CPU-bound loops do not notice aborts by themselves. Call <code>signal.throwIfAborted()</code> between chunks of work, and yield to the event loop between chunks (Post 01), or the abort event cannot even be delivered.</p>
</blockquote>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> in React effects, create one <code>AbortController</code> per effect run and call <code>abort()</code> in the cleanup. That single line prevents both the stale-response race and &quot;state update on unmounted component&quot; noise. The React Deep Dive series covers this in detail.</p>
</blockquote>

<h2 id="WHATWG-streams-and-backpressure"><a href="#WHATWG-streams-and-backpressure" class="headerlink" title="WHATWG streams and backpressure"></a>WHATWG streams and backpressure</h2>
<p>Streams add what iterators lack: a <strong>queue with a size limit</strong> and a signal back upstream when the queue is full. That signal is <strong>backpressure</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="Terminology-2"><a href="#Terminology-2" class="headerlink" title="Terminology"></a>Terminology</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Queuing strategy</strong> — how chunks are measured: <code>CountQueuingStrategy</code> (one per chunk) or <code>ByteLengthQueuingStrategy</code>.</li>
<li><strong><code>highWaterMark</code></strong> — the queue size at which the stream reports &quot;full&quot;.</li>
<li><strong><code>desiredSize</code></strong> — <code>highWaterMark − queued size</code>. Zero or negative means &quot;stop producing&quot;.</li>
<li><strong>Pull source</strong> — a source whose <code>pull()</code> is called only when <code>desiredSize &gt; 0</code>. This is backpressure done for you.</li>
</ul>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span <p><code>pipeTo</code> and <code>pipeThrough</code> carry backpressure end to end: a slow writer makes the transform's queue fill, which stops reads from the network, which lets the TCP receive window close, which slows the sender. <code>pipeTo(dest, { signal })</code> also takes a signal.</p>
<p>A source with a <code>pull()</code> method gets backpressure for free: the stream calls <code>pull()</code> only while <code>desiredSize &gt; 0</code>. The failure mode is a <strong>push source that ignores <code>desiredSize</code></strong>. For example, a <code>start()</code> that subscribes to a WebSocket and calls <code>enqueue</code> for every message. The queue then grows without bound. Either check <code>controller.desiredSize</code> and pause the upstream, or drop and count messages on purpose.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-6-—-Concurrency-limits"><a href="#Chapter-6-—-Concurrency-limits" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 6 — Concurrency limits"></a>Chapter 6 — Concurrency limits</h1>
<p><code>Promise.all(items.map(fetchOne))</code> with 10,000 items starts 10,000 requests at once. The results are socket exhaustion, rate-limit responses, a database connection pool drained in milliseconds, and memory for 10,000 pending responses. Bound it.</p>

</blockquote>

</blockquote>

<h1 id="Chapter-7-—-Timeouts-retries-and-idempotency"><a href="#Chapter-7-—-Timeouts-retries-and-idempotency" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 7 — Timeouts, retries and idempotency"></a>Chapter 7 — Timeouts, retries and idempotency</h1>
<h2 id="Timeouts"><a href="#Timeouts" class="headerlink" title="Timeouts"></a>Timeouts</h2>
<p>Every network call needs a deadline, and the deadline must also <em>cancel</em> the work, which is why <code>AbortSignal.timeout()</code> beats <code>Promise.race</code>. In a multi-step operation, create one deadline per request, <code>AbortSignal.any([req.signal, AbortSignal.timeout(2_000)])</code>, and pass that same signal to every step. Each later step then gets whatever time is left, instead of its own full timeout.</p>
<h2 id="Retries-with-exponential-backoff-and-full-jitter"><a href="#Retries-with-exponential-backoff-and-full-jitter" class="headerlink" title="Retries with exponential backoff and full jitter"></a>Retries with exponential backoff and full jitter</h2>
<p>Retrying immediately makes an overloaded dependency worse. Exponential backoff spaces the attempts out. <strong>Jitter</strong> stops a thousand clients that failed together from retrying together. The AWS Architecture Blog analysis (Marc Brooker, &quot;Exponential Backoff and Jitter&quot;) found <strong>full jitter</strong>, <code>sleep = random(0, min(cap, base × 2^attempt))</code>, to be a strong default.</p>

<p><code>retryAfterMs(err)</code> is a small helper that reads the <code>Retry-After</code> response header (seconds or an HTTP date) and returns <code>undefined</code> when there is none. With <code>retries = 4</code>, <code>base = 100</code> and <code>cap = 5_000</code>, the call makes up to 5 attempts. The sleep ceilings before the four retries are 100, 200, 400 and 800 ms, so the worst-case total sleep is 1,500 ms. The actual sleep is uniform below each ceiling.</p>
<p>Retry discipline that matters more than the formula:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Retry in one layer only.</strong> If the browser, the BFF and the service each make 3 attempts (1 try + 2 retries), one user click can become 3 × 3 × 3 = <strong>27</strong> calls at the bottom layer, exactly when that layer is struggling.</li>
<li><strong>Use a retry budget.</strong> For example, allow retries only while they are under 10% of recent requests. A circuit breaker that fails fast while the dependency is down is the same idea taken further.</li>
<li><strong>Only retry idempotent operations</strong>, or make them idempotent first.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="Idempotency"><a href="#Idempotency" class="headerlink" title="Idempotency"></a>Idempotency</h2>
<p>An operation is idempotent if doing it twice has the same effect as doing it once. HTTP defines <code>GET</code>, <code>HEAD</code>, <code>PUT</code> and <code>DELETE</code> as idempotent (RFC 9110). <code>POST</code> is not. That matters because a timeout does not tell you whether the server <em>did</em> the work: the request may have succeeded and only the response was lost.</p>
<p>The standard fix is an <strong>idempotency key</strong>. The client generates a unique key per logical operation and sends it on every attempt. The server records the key with the result and returns the stored result for repeats. The pattern was popularised by Stripe's API and is being standardised as the <code>Idempotency-Key</code> header (an IETF httpapi working-group draft).</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">async</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">function</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">createPayment</span>(<span class="hljs-params">order, { signal }</span>) {</span><br><span class="line">  <span class="hljs-keyword">const</span> key = crypto.<span class="hljs-title function_">randomUUID</span>();                 <span class="hljs-comment">// once per logical operation, NOT per attempt</span></span><br><span class="line">  <span class="hljs-keyword">const</span> headers = { <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;content-type&#x27;</span>: <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;application/json&#x27;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;idempotency-key&#x27;</span>: key };</span><br><span class="line">  <span class="hljs-keyword">return</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">retry</span>(<span class="hljs-function">(<span class="hljs-params">{ signal }</span>) =&gt;</span> <span class="hljs-title function_">fetch</span>(<span class="hljs-string">&#x27;/api/payments&#x27;</span>,</span><br><span class="line">    { <span class="hljs-attr">method</span>: <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;POST&#x27;</span>, signal, headers, <span class="hljs-attr">body</span>: <span class="hljs-title class_">JSON</span>.<span class="hljs-title function_">stringify</span>(order) }).<span class="hljs-title function_">then</span>(assertOk), { signal });</span><br><span class="line">}</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Server side, the key needs a unique constraint and a &quot;claim first, then do the work&quot; order. Otherwise two concurrent attempts both see &quot;no record yet&quot; and both charge the card.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Common pitfall:</strong> generating the key inside the retried function. Every attempt gets a fresh key, so the protection disappears exactly when it is needed.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Chapter-8-—-Errors-that-keep-their-cause"><a href="#Chapter-8-—-Errors-that-keep-their-cause" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 8 — Errors that keep their cause"></a>Chapter 8 — Errors that keep their cause</h1>
<p>Async code crosses many layers, and each layer that catches and re-throws tends to lose information. Since ES2022 every <code>Error</code> constructor takes a <code>cause</code>:</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">try</span> { <span class="hljs-keyword">await</span> db.<span class="hljs-title function_">query</span>(sql); }</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">catch</span> (err) { <span class="hljs-keyword">throw</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">new</span> <span class="hljs-title class_">Error</span>(<span class="hljs-string">`loading invoice <span class="hljs-subst">${id}</span> failed`</span>, { <span class="hljs-attr">cause</span>: err }); }</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Node's <code>util.inspect</code> (and so <code>console.log</code>) prints the whole chain with <code>[cause]:</code> markers, and browser consoles show it too. When you serialise a chain for logs yourself, walk <code>e.cause</code> with a depth limit, because a chain can contain a cycle.</p>
<p>Related tools:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong><code>AggregateError</code></strong> (ES2021): several errors at once, with <code>.errors</code>. <code>Promise.any</code> uses it. Use it yourself for &quot;3 of 5 replicas failed&quot;.</li>
<li><strong><code>Error.isError</code></strong> (ES2026): a cross-realm brand check. It is true for errors from iframes or <code>vm</code> contexts, where <code>instanceof Error</code> is false.</li>
<li><strong>Custom error classes</strong> with a stable <code>name</code> and a <code>code</code> field make <code>isRetryable</code> and alerting rules simple and robust. Matching on message strings is fragile.</li>
</ul>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span><br><span class="line">10</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">class</span> <span class="hljs-title class_">HttpError</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">extends</span> <span class="hljs-title class_ inherited__">Error</span> {</span><br><span class="line">  <span class="hljs-title function_">constructor</span>(<span class="hljs-params">res, options</span>) {</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-variable language_">super</span>(<span class="hljs-string">`HTTP <span class="hljs-subst">${res.status}</span> <span class="hljs-subst">${res.statusText}</span> for <span class="hljs-subst">${res.url}</span>`</span>, options);</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-variable language_">this</span>.<span class="hljs-property">name</span> = <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;HttpError&#x27;</span>;</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-variable language_">this</span>.<span class="hljs-property">status</span> = res.<span class="hljs-property">status</span>;</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-variable language_">this</span>.<span class="hljs-property">headers</span> = res.<span class="hljs-property">headers</span>; <h2 id="Exercise-1-—-Predict-the-order"><a href="#Exercise-1-—-Predict-the-order" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 1 — Predict the order"></a>Exercise 1 — Predict the order</h2>
<p>What does this print?</p>

</details>
<h2 id="Exercise-2-—-Combinator-choice"><a href="#Exercise-2-—-Combinator-choice" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 2 — Combinator choice"></a>Exercise 2 — Combinator choice</h2>
<p>For each case pick a combinator: (a) load five widgets and show whichever succeed; (b) query three mirrors and use the first good answer; (c) a checkout that needs price, stock and tax, all or nothing.</p>
<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
<p>(a) <code>Promise.allSettled</code>, then render the <code>fulfilled</code> ones. (b) <code>Promise.any</code>. It ignores mirrors that fail and only rejects (with <code>AggregateError</code>) if all fail. Pair it with an <code>AbortController</code> so that the losers are cancelled once a winner arrives. (c) <code>Promise.all</code> with a shared signal, aborted in the <code>catch</code>, so that the other two stop once one fails.</p>
</details>

<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line">app.<span class="hljs-title function_">get</span>(<span class="hljs-string">&#x27;/me&#x27;</span>, <span class="hljs-title function_">async</span> (req, res) =&gt; {</span><br><span class="line">  <span class="hljs-keyword">const</span> profile = <span class="hljs-title function_">getProfile</span>(req.<span class="hljs-property">user</span>);</span><br><span class="line">  <span class="hljs-keyword">const</span> prefs = <span class="hljs-title function_">getPrefs</span>(req.<span class="hljs-property">user</span>);</span><br><span class="line">  res.<span class="hljs-title function_">json</span>({ <span class="hljs-attr">profile</span>: <span class="hljs-keyword">await</span> profile, <span class="hljs-attr">prefs</span>: <span class="hljs-keyword">await</span> prefs });</span><br><span class="line">});</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<h2 id="Exercise-5-—-Backoff-arithmetic"><a href="#Exercise-5-—-Backoff-arithmetic" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 5 — Backoff arithmetic"></a>Exercise 5 — Backoff arithmetic</h2>
<p>With full jitter, <code>base = 200 ms</code>, <code>cap = 3,000 ms</code> and 5 retries, list the ceilings and the worst-case total sleep.</p>
<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
<p>Ceilings <code>min(3000, 200 × 2^n)</code> for n = 0…4: 200, 400, 800, 1,600, 3,000 (3,200 capped). Worst-case total = 200 + 400 + 800 + 1,600 + 3,000 = <strong>6,000 ms</strong>. The expected total is half of that, 3,000 ms, because each sleep is uniform on <code>[0, ceiling)</code>. This total must fit inside the caller's own deadline, or the retries are wasted.</p>
</details>
