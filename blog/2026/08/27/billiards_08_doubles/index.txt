<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Find the double's line by <strong>mirroring the pocket</strong>.</li>
<li>Give a double enough pace for its long trip.</li>
<li>Tell a <strong>cross double</strong> from a <strong>cut-back double</strong>, and know the risk in each.</li>

<h1 id="1-Mirror-the-pocket"><a href="#1-Mirror-the-pocket" class="headerlink" title="1 · Mirror the pocket"></a>1 · Mirror the pocket</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b08_mirror.webp" alt="Top view of a red near the top side cushion, a little left of the top middle pocket. A dashed line goes from the red up to a yellow point on the cushion and on towards a mirror pocket above the table. The red's track goes up to that point, comes back down across the table and into the bottom middle pocket, tick. The white sits below the red, on the line"></p>
<ol>
<li>Picture the pocket <strong>reflected</strong> behind the cushion, the same distance on the other side (the mirror pocket).</li>
<li>The red must go <strong>at the mirror pocket</strong>. Where that line meets the cushion is the point the red must hit.</li>
<li>Now it is a normal pot at the mirror pocket: find the ghost ball and aim (post 00).</li>
</ol>
<p>For the red in the picture, aimed exactly at the mirror pocket, the double went in at every pace that reached the pocket. A red that has just been hit is <strong>sliding</strong>, and a sliding ball comes off a cushion close to the mirror line (post 07).</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> check the line from the other end. Stand behind the pocket and look at the cushion point: the red should sit on the reflected line.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="5-When-to-play-a-double"><a href="#5-When-to-play-a-double" class="headerlink" title="5 · When to play a double"></a>5 · When to play a double</h1>
<ul>
<li>When the direct pot is blocked or impossible.</li>
<li>When the double is close to straight (the white more or less behind the red, as in picture 1): those are the easiest.</li>
<li>As a &quot;shot to nothing&quot;: a miss sends the red back up the table, often to a safe place.</li>
</ul>
<p>Before you play it, check the white: where does it go at this pace, and is there a pocket on its path?</p>
