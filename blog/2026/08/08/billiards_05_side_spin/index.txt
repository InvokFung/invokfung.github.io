11:T2308,<p>Side spin is the most misunderstood tool on the table. It does three things at once: it pushes the white off line, curves it back, and pushes the ball you hit the other way. This lesson shows each one on its own, then two ways to aim so they don't make you miss.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Series map. <em>★ Basics:</em> 00 Aiming → 01 The hit → 02 Stop, follow, screw → 03 Where the white goes · <em>★★ Club:</em> 04 Thin cuts → <strong>05 Side spin</strong> (this post) → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker · <em>★★★ Advanced:</em> 10 Plants and sets → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → 13 Position · <em>★★★★ Hard:</em> 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → 15 Swerve and special spots.</p>

<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>See <strong>squirt</strong>, <strong>swerve</strong> and <strong>throw</strong>, one at a time.</li>
<li>Know when they cancel and when they don't.</li>
<li>Aim with side two ways: adjust the aim, or pivot the cue.</li>
<li>Use side only when you need it.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="1-Squirt-then-swerve"><a href="#1-Squirt-then-swerve" class="headerlink" title="1 · Squirt, then swerve"></a>1 · Squirt, then swerve</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b05_squirt_swerve.webp" alt="Three close-up top views of a red ball 2 metres away on a dashed aiming line. All three shots use right side. Power 2: the white arrives almost on the line, tick. Power 4: the white arrives too far left, cross. Power 2 with the cue raised: the white curves far to the right, cross"></p>
<p>Hit the white on the right, aim straight at a red 2 m away:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Squirt:</strong> the white starts a little <strong>left</strong>, away from the side.</li>
<li><strong>Swerve:</strong> as it slides, the spin curves it back <strong>right</strong>, towards the side.</li>
</ul>

<p>So side is most &quot;honest&quot; on a <strong>slow-to-medium shot with a level cue</strong>. Hard shots with side drift away from the side.</p>

<h1 id="2-Throw-side-pushes-the-red-the-other-way"><a href="#2-Throw-side-pushes-the-red-the-other-way" class="headerlink" title="2 · Throw: side pushes the red the other way"></a>2 · Throw: side pushes the red the other way</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b05_throw_side.webp" alt="Two top views of a full-ball hit on a red. Right side: the red goes a little to the left of the straight line, about 3 cm after a metre. Left side: the red goes a little to the right"></p>
<p>At the contact, the spinning white rubs the red. <strong>Right side throws the red left; left side throws it right</strong>, here about 3 cm over a metre on a slow full-ball hit.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> this is how good players fix a slow cut that keeps going in fat (post 04): a little side that throws the red towards the pocket.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="3-Two-ways-to-aim-with-side"><a href="#3-Two-ways-to-aim-with-side" class="headerlink" title="3 · Two ways to aim with side"></a>3 · Two ways to aim with side</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b05_aim.webp" alt="Two top views of the white with right side. 1: the cue moved sideways, parallel to the aiming line; the white goes off the line to the left, cross. 2: the cue pivoted from the bridge hand; the white goes along the line, tick"></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Aim, then adjust.</strong> Line up as normal, move the cue sideways for side, then turn your aim a touch <strong>towards the side</strong> you use. How much depends on your cue and the pace; learn it by practice.</li>
<li><strong>Pivot from the bridge</strong> (back-hand side). Line up centre-ball, then swing only the back hand, keeping the bridge still. The cue now points slightly towards the side, and squirt brings the white back onto the line. It cancels squirt exactly when the bridge is at your cue's <strong>pivot length</strong> (about 30 cm for the model cue here). Test yours: hit firm shots with side along the baulk line, and move the bridge until the white stays on the line.</li>
</ol>
<p>Neither is &quot;right&quot;. Method 1 is easier to learn; method 2 is easier to repeat once you know your cue.</p>

<p>Right side, power 3, aimed as if there were no side: the white arrives a little off, and over a long pot that is enough to miss ✗.</p>

</blockquote>

<td>cancel throw on a slow cut</td>
<td>04</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>curve round a ball (with a raised cue)</td>
<td>15</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>If none of these applies, <strong>play without side</strong>. A centre-ball hit is easier and more accurate.</p>
