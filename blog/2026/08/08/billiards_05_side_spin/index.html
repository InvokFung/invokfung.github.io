Billiards & Snooker — 05 · Side Spin: Squirt, Swerve and Throw

Created2026-08-08|Updated2026-08-08|Sports > Billiards
|Post View:

Side spin is the most misunderstood tool on the table. It does three things at once: it pushes the white off line, curves it back, and pushes the ball you hit the other way. This lesson shows each one on its own, then two ways to aim so they don't make you miss.

Series map. ★ Basics: 00 Aiming → 01 The hit → 02 Stop, follow, screw → 03 Where the white goes · ★★ Club: 04 Thin cuts → 05 Side spin (this post) → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker · ★★★ Advanced: 10 Plants and sets → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → 13 Position · ★★★★ Hard: 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → 15 Swerve and special spots.

Learning Outcome

  • See squirt, swerve and throw, one at a time.
  • Know when they cancel and when they don't.
  • Aim with side two ways: adjust the aim, or pivot the cue.
  • Use side only when you need it.

1 · Squirt, then swerve

Hit the white on the right, aim straight at a red 2 m away:

  • Squirt: the white starts a little left, away from the side.
  • Swerve: as it slides, the spin curves it back right, towards the side.
Shot What wins Where the white arrives
power 2, cue nearly level they almost cancel on the line ✓
power 4, cue nearly level squirt too far left ✗
power 2, cue raised swerve far right ✗

So side is most "honest" on a slow-to-medium shot with a level cue. Hard shots with side drift away from the side.

2 · Throw: side pushes the red the other way

At the contact, the spinning white rubs the red. Right side throws the red left; left side throws it right, here about 3 cm over a metre on a slow full-ball hit.

Trick: this is how good players fix a slow cut that keeps going in fat (post 04): a little side that throws the red towards the pocket.

3 · Two ways to aim with side

  1. Aim, then adjust. Line up as normal, move the cue sideways for side, then turn your aim a touch towards the side you use. How much depends on your cue and the pace; learn it by practice.
  2. Pivot from the bridge (back-hand side). Line up centre-ball, then swing only the back hand, keeping the bridge still. The cue now points slightly towards the side, and squirt brings the white back onto the line. It cancels squirt exactly when the bridge is at your cue's pivot length (about 30 cm for the model cue here). Test yours: hit firm shots with side along the baulk line, and move the bridge until the white stays on the line.

Neither is "right". Method 1 is easier to learn; method 2 is easier to repeat once you know your cue.

4 · Watch it: a long pot with side

Right side, power 3, aimed as if there were no side: the white arrives a little off, and over a long pot that is enough to miss ✗.

The same shot with the aim turned a touch to the right (less than a degree): it goes in ✓.

Common pitfall: "I put side on to get position and missed the pot." Most misses with side are long pots. Use it on long pots only when you must.

5 · When to use side

Use side to… Post
change the angle off a cushion (wider or narrower) 07
hold the white or send it further for position 06, 13
reach a ball when snookered 09, 14
cancel throw on a slow cut 04
curve round a ball (with a raised cue) 15

If none of these applies, play without side. A centre-ball hit is easier and more accurate.

6 · Try it at the table

Drill Set-up Look for
Squirt test white on the baulk line, play it up the table over the spots with right side at power 2, then 4 how far off the spots it runs
Pivot length the same with the pivot method; move the bridge closer or further the bridge length where it stays on the spots
Throw test full-ball hit on a red 30 cm away, left side, then right side the red goes a little the other way

Cheat-sheet
Squirt the white starts away from the side
Swerve then curves back towards it (more with a raised cue, slower pace, longer shot)
Throw the red goes away from the side
Aim turn a touch towards the side, or pivot from the bridge
Default no side unless you need it

References
