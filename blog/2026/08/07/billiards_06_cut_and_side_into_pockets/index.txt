<th>Tip</th>
<th>The red goes…</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>no side</td>
<td>1.8° fuller (cut throw)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>inside side, half-way</td>
<td>1.5° fuller</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>outside side, half-way</td>
<td>2.1° <strong>thinner</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>a little outside side (0.17 R)</td>
<td>on the ghost-ball line: throw cancelled</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>So <strong>a little outside side cancels throw</strong> on slow cuts; more outside side over-cuts. Inside side keeps the throw.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> a slow cut that keeps going in fat? A touch of outside side, and remember to turn the aim for squirt (section 3).</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="5-One-black-three-routes"><a href="#5-One-black-three-routes" class="headerlink" title="5 · One black, three routes"></a>5 · One black, three routes</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b06_routes.webp" alt="Top view of the black on its spot being potted into the top corner. Three white tracks leave it after bouncing off the top cushion: blue (check side) goes up towards the far side cushion, white (no side) comes back to the middle near the pink, yellow (running side) comes down the table. Each ends in a ring with a small face showing the tip spot"></p>
<p>The same black, the same pocket, three tip spots. The white comes off the top cushion at three different angles:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Tip</th>
<th>Name</th>
<th>The white comes off…</th>
<th>Good for a red…</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>◀ left of centre</td>
<td><strong>check side</strong></td>
<td>narrower, back up towards the far side cushion</td>
<td>near that side cushion</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>centre (a touch of top)</td>
<td>plain</td>
<td>straight back to the middle</td>
<td>near the pink</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>right of centre ▶</td>
<td><strong>running side</strong></td>
<td>wider, down the table</td>
<td>towards the near side</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Each side shot needed its own small aim turn (section 3).</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="6-Watch-it-running-side-off-the-black"><a href="#6-Watch-it-running-side-off-the-black" class="headerlink" title="6 · Watch it: running side off the black"></a>6 · Watch it: running side off the black</h2>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b06_clip_running.webp" alt="Clip: the black is cut into the top corner with right-hand running side; the white comes off the top cushion wide and runs down the table into the yellow ring"></div>
<p>Right-hand running side, power 3: the black goes in ✓, and the white comes off the top cushion wide and down the table.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-Very-thin-cuts"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-Very-thin-cuts" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — Very thin cuts"></a>Chapter 3 — Very thin cuts</h1>
<h2 id="7-An-80-cut-needs-power"><a href="#7-An-80-cut-needs-power" class="headerlink" title="7 · An 80° cut needs power"></a>7 · An 80° cut needs power</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s06_thin80_power.webp" alt="Two top views of the same 80-degree cut: a red 20 cm off the top cushion, potted along the cushion into the top-right corner. Power 3: the red stops short of the pocket, cross. Power 4: the red reaches the pocket, tick"></p>
<p>A red near the top cushion, cut along it into the corner. The white comes in at <strong>80°</strong>: an overlap of under 1 mm (post 02). The red gets only about a sixth of the white's speed.</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Power</th>
<th>Result (model)</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>3</td>
<td>the red stops short ✗</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>4</td>
<td>the red reaches the pocket ✓</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Thin cuts are a <strong>power</strong> decision first. Pick the pace that gets the red to the pocket, then think about the white.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="8-The-same-80-cut-three-ways"><a href="#8-The-same-80-cut-three-ways" class="headerlink" title="8 · The same 80° cut, three ways"></a>8 · The same 80° cut, three ways</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s06_thin80_ways.webp" alt="Top view of the whole table. The 80-degree cut is played three ways at power 4. No side: the white goes up and down the table and ends near the top cushion, tick. Left side: the white comes off the cushions wider and ends in the middle of the table, tick. Right side: the white comes off narrower and runs into the top middle pocket, an in-off, cross"></p>
<p>The white keeps almost all its speed and travels a long way. Side changes where, and each side needs its own aim (model, power 4):</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Tip</th>
<th>Aim turned</th>
<th>The white…</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>no side</td>
<td>plain aim</td>
<td>runs up and down, ends near the top cushion ✓</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>left side (running side here)</td>
<td>2.3° left</td>
<td>comes off wider, ends in the middle of the table ✓</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>right side (check side here)</td>
<td>2.4° right</td>
<td>comes off narrower and goes into the top middle pocket ✗ in-off</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Two good solutions, one trap. With a red near the middle of the table, the <strong>left side</strong> route is best; with a red near the top cushion, <strong>no side</strong> is simpler and more accurate.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Common pitfall:</strong> using side on a thin cut &quot;for position&quot; without checking the white's whole path. Here the check-side route finds a pocket.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="9-Watch-it-the-80-cut-with-running-side"><a href="#9-Watch-it-the-80-cut-with-running-side" class="headerlink" title="9 · Watch it: the 80° cut with running side"></a>9 · Watch it: the 80° cut with running side</h2>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/s06_clip_thin80.webp" alt="Clip from above the baulk end. The white comes up from the right side of the table and just grazes the red near the top cushion. The red runs along the cushion into the top-right corner; the white comes off the top cushion, crosses the table, and ends near the middle in a yellow ring"></div>
<p>Left side, aim turned 2.3° left, power 4. The red creeps along the cushion and drops ✓; the white travels round the table to the middle.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="10-A-thin-cut-into-the-middle-pocket"><a href="#10-A-thin-cut-into-the-middle-pocket" class="headerlink" title="10 · A thin cut into the middle pocket"></a>10 · A thin cut into the middle pocket</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s06_thin_middle.webp" alt="Top view near the top middle pocket. A red sits 20 cm out from the top cushion and 20 cm to the left of the pocket. The white comes from below right and cuts it at 75 degrees; the red runs into the middle pocket at a slant, tick. The white's dashed track runs on"></p>
<p>The red is 20 cm left of the middle pocket and 20 cm out. It goes in at <strong>42°</strong> to straight, where the middle pocket has about 6 cm of room (post 02). The white cuts it at <strong>75°</strong>, power 4 ✓.</p>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/s06_clip_thin_middle.webp" alt="Clip from behind the white: the dashed line from the red to the middle pocket, the ghost ball almost beside the red, then the white just grazes the red and the red rolls into the middle pocket"></div>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> on a thin cut into the middle, aim the red at the <strong>back of the hole</strong> (yellow dot), like section 2. The slant already uses up half the room.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="11-Choosing-a-route"><a href="#11-Choosing-a-route" class="headerlink" title="11 · Choosing a route"></a>11 · Choosing a route</h2>
<p>When more than one route works, choose the one that:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Keeps the white away from pockets and other balls</strong> on the way (section 8's trap).</li>
<li>Lands in the <strong>biggest area</strong> that leaves a pot on the next ball (post 12).</li>
<li>Needs the <strong>least side</strong>. No side is the most accurate pot.</li>
</ol>
<p>So if the plain route is good enough, play it. Use side when it opens a clearly better landing spot.</p>
