Billiards & Snooker — 16 · Cue-Ball Control: Pro Patterns

Created2026-10-18|Updated2026-10-18|Sports > Billiards
|Post View:

Post 12 showed where to send the white. This post shows how good players choose the shot that gets it there: they read every tip and power for one pot like a map, set pace before spin, open the reds off the blue, pick the key ball before the last red, and plan the six colours as one chain. Every picture is a physics-model run, drawn with the balls and the cue only.

Series map. ★ Basics: 01 Cue action and elevation → 02 Aiming and potting → 03 Tip and power → 04 Where the white goes · ★★ Club: 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles · ★★★ Advanced: 09 Escaping snookers → 10 Multi-ball shots → 11 Safety → 12 Position play · ★★★★ Hard: 13 Break-building → 14 Special shots · Reference: 15 Rules, tactics and practice · ★★★★★ Pro: 16 Cue-ball control (this post).

Learning Outcome

  • Read one pot as a map of where the white can finish.
  • Know which choices still pot the ball, and which leave it short.
  • Set the pace first, then the tip.
  • Pick the zone, then read the map backwards for the shot.
  • Open the reds off the blue, and choose the key ball before the last red.
  • Plan the six colours as one chain of short, easy moves.

Chapter 1 — Read the white's options

1 · The position map

One red, one pocket, one cut (30°). Only the tip and the power change. Each ring is where the white stops. Good players know this map for the pots they meet most, before they get down on the shot.

2 · Which choices pot the red

Not every choice works. The same map as a table (model):

Power Tip Red White stops
2 screw stops short ✗ –
2 stun stops short ✗ –
2 roll potted 22 cm from the black spot
2 follow potted 6 cm from the black spot
3 screw potted 15 cm off the bottom side cushion
3 stun potted 27 cm below the black spot
3 roll potted 38 cm from the pink spot
3 follow potted 18 cm off the bottom cushion, near the middle pocket
  • Screw and stun need pace. Backspin slows the white before contact, so at power 2 the red does not reach the pocket.
  • Follow at power 2 runs onto the black spot. In a frame the black is there: the white would kiss it.
  • Follow at power 3 heads for the middle pocket. It stops 18 cm off the cushion here; a little more pace risks an in-off.

Watch out: a shot that leaves perfect position but does not pot is the worst shot on the map. The pot comes first.

3 · Pace first, then tip

Change only the power, and the end points spread over 222 cm. Change only the tip at power 3, and they spread over 148 cm (model).

Roll, power The white travels
2 117 cm
3 217 cm
4 358 cm

So pace is the bigger lever. Choose the pace that takes the white to the zone, then use the tip to bend the path. A pace you play every day is worth more than a big spin you play once a frame.

Trick: practise the same pot at three set paces until each one lands in its own ring. That is the map in your arm.

4 · Pick the zone, then read the map backwards

Start from the next ball, not from this one:

Next ball Zone (section 1 map) The shot
the black, into a top corner near the black spot, a little off it roll, power 2
the black, from below 27 cm below the black spot stun, power 3
a red near the pink below the pink roll, power 3
a ball on the bottom side cushion 15 cm off it, level with the black screw, power 3
the blue towards the bottom middle pocket follow, power 3, watch the in-off

The pro habit: zone → ring on the map → tip and power. If two choices reach the zone, take the one with the softer pace and the plainer tip.

Chapter 2 — The reds

5 · Into the reds off the blue

Early in a frame the reds are still in a bunch behind the pink. A good way in is off the blue (coaching advice): pot the blue and let the white carry on into the reds. Here the blue is a 55° cut into the top middle pocket. Two choices (model):

Choice The white's next ball Reds moved more than 30 cm
stun, power 3 the pink ✗ 1
screw, power 4 a red ✓ 2

Stun sends the white along the tangent line, straight into the pink: a kiss you cannot plan. Screw bends the path behind that line, and the extra pace carries the white into the side of the reds.

Watch out: opening the reds is not a pot. Plan where the white ends too: here it runs on to the top end, near the black.

6 · The key ball

The key ball is the colour you take with the last red, chosen so the white lands for the yellow (coaching advice). Here the last red is gone and the white sits just above the blue. What each colour leaves (model):

Key ball Pots? The white ends The yellow next
blue, bottom middle, power 3 stun yes near the baulk cushion, 65 cm from the yellow easy: 13° into the bottom middle ✓
pink yes, 10 ways in the model power 2 roll: at the top end, 268 cm away not easy from any of the 10 ✗
black no pocket at a cut of 45° or less – –

"Easy" here means: the white 25–120 cm from the yellow, a cut of 8–35°, the yellow within 150 cm of the pocket, and both lines clear.

Trick: choose the key ball before you pot the last red, then play the last red to land on it.

Chapter 3 — The colours as one chain

7 · Plan all six first

After the last red, the colours go yellow to black and stay down. Before the yellow, good players walk the whole chain in their head: each pot sends the white to an easy angle on the next colour. Here is one chain the model found, from the white just right of the yellow:

# Ball Pocket Power Tip The white moves Next ball, cut
1 yellow bottom left 3 roll 75 cm green, 17°
2 green top middle 3 roll 72 cm brown, 22°
3 brown bottom left 3 stun 121 cm blue, 26°
4 blue top middle 2 follow 193 cm pink, 17°
5 pink bottom middle 3 roll 111 cm black, 21°
6 black top right 2 roll 82 cm –

8 · Shot by shot

Look at what the chain does not use: no power 4 or 5, no hard screw, no side. Every next cut is between 17° and 26°: an angle to work with, never straight and never thin.

Trick: an angle of about 15–30° on the next ball is what lets the next shot move the white (post 12, section 3).

9 · The long move: the blue

The biggest move in the chain is 193 cm, off the blue (shot 4). It is still a power 2 shot: follow does the work and the white runs with the natural angle up the table. The long move is played with the softest pace that gets there, so the error stays small.

Common pitfall: hitting the blue hard "to get up the table". The extra pace spreads the stopping point (section 3), and the pink cut becomes a guess.

10 · When position goes wrong

Even good players miss the zone. Then (coaching advice):

You are left with Do this
a pot, but no shot onto the next ball look for another pocket for this ball first
a long or thin pot play the pot with a safety in mind: pace that leaves the white safe if it misses (post 11)
no fair pot play the safety now, not the gamble
a ball the plan skipped re-plan from here: three balls ahead again

11 · The pro checklist

Before each shot in a break:

Ask Section
Which zone for the next ball, and the one after? 4, 7
Which ring on the map lands there? 1
Does that choice still pot the ball? 2
Can I get there with less pace? 3
Is the path clear of other balls and pockets? 2 (kiss, in-off)
Before the last red: which key ball? 6
If I miss the zone, what is the next-best shot? 10

Try it at the table

Drill Set-up Look for
The map the section 1 red and white; play the six potting choices each white stops in its own ring
Short at power 2 the same red, power 2 screw the red stops short: feel it, then add pace
Pace ladder the same red, roll, power 2, 3, 4 three clearly different stopping places
Zone first a friend names a zone; you choose tip and power land in it three times out of five
Off the blue the section 5 set-up the white reaches the reds after the blue
The chain the colours on their spots, the white just right of the yellow clear all six with power 2–3 only

Cheat-sheet
Map for one pot: every tip and power, and where the white stops
Pot first power 2 screw or stun can leave the red short
Pace first pace spreads the ending more than the tip (222 cm vs 148 cm here)
Backwards next zone → ring on the map → tip and power
Reds open them off the blue, with screw and pace
Key ball choose the colour with the last red that leaves the yellow
Colours short moves, power 2–3, a 17–26° angle on each next ball
Gone wrong another pocket, a pot with safety in mind, or the safety
