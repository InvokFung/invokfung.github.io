<h2 id="2-Starting-a-frame"><a href="#2-Starting-a-frame" class="headerlink" title="2 · Starting a frame"></a>2 · Starting a frame</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b12_breakoff.webp" alt="Top view of the whole table at the start of a frame. The white starts in the D near the yellow. <ul>
<li>The 15 reds go in a tight triangle, the front red as close to the pink as it can be <strong>without touching</strong> it, the back row parallel to the top cushion.</li>
<li>The colours go on their spots.</li>
<li>Toss a coin (or agree) for who breaks. After that, the players <strong>take turns</strong> to break, frame by frame.</li>
<li>The first shot is from <strong>in-hand</strong> (the white placed in the D), and the ball on is a red.</li>
</ul>
<p>The usual break-off is the safety from post 11: a thin touch on the end red, and back to baulk.</p>

<h2 id="3-Playing-from-the-D"><a href="#3-Playing-from-the-D" class="headerlink" title="3 · Playing from the D"></a>3 · Playing from the D</h2>
<p>The white is <strong>in-hand</strong> at the start of a frame, after it goes in a pocket or off the table, and for a re-spotted black. Then:</p>
<ul>
<li>place it <strong>on or inside the lines</strong> of the D (on the line counts);</li>
<li>you may then play in <strong>any direction</strong>, even back towards the baulk cushion;</li>
<li>if the tip touches the white while you are only placing it, it does not count as a shot.</li>
</ul>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> move the white along the D before you settle. A few centimetres often turns a thin cut into an easy one, or opens a safety line.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-2-—-Fouls"><a href="#Chapter-2-—-Fouls" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 2 — Fouls"></a>Chapter 2 — Fouls</h1>
<h2 id="4-The-fouls-you-meet-most"><a href="#4-The-fouls-you-meet-most" class="headerlink" title="4 · The fouls you meet most"></a>4 · The fouls you meet most</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s15_fouls.svg" alt="A table of the fouls met most often, each with its penalty: at least 4 points, or the value of the balls involved if higher; 7 for playing reds in successive strokes"></p>
<p>Every penalty is <strong>at least 4</strong>, and more if a higher-value ball is involved (blue 5, pink 6, black 7). The points go to your opponent; you lose nothing from your own score. If two fouls happen in one shot, only the higher penalty counts.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Common pitfall:</strong> a foul on the black ball costs 7, even if the ball on was a red. Brushing the black with your sleeve is the classic one.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="5-After-a-foul-two-choices"><a href="#5-After-a-foul-two-choices" class="headerlink" title="5 · After a foul: two choices"></a>5 · After a foul: two choices</h2>
<p>After your opponent fouls, you choose:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>play</strong> the next shot yourself from where the balls are; or</li>
<li>make your opponent <strong>play again</strong> from there.</li>
</ol>
<p>Choose 2 when the position is bad for whoever plays next: a hard snooker, or a ball on a cushion. Choose 1 when there is a pot or an easy safety. (A foul and a miss adds a third choice, below.)</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="6-The-miss-and-the-free-ball"><a href="#6-The-miss-and-the-free-ball" class="headerlink" title="6 · The miss and the free ball"></a>6 · The miss and the free ball</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s09_miss.svg" alt="Five rows, each a rule about hitting the ball on: foul, foul and a miss, three misses, when no miss is called, and the free ball"></p>
<p>Post 09 has the full story. In short:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Foul and a miss:</strong> the referee thinks you did not try your best to hit the ball on. Your opponent may also have the balls <strong>put back</strong> and make you play the same shot again.</li>
<li>If you had a <strong>clear, straight path</strong> to the full ball and missed, the next miss from the same spot gets a warning, and a third can lose the frame.</li>
<li><strong>Free ball:</strong> if a foul leaves you snookered, you may name <strong>any</strong> ball as the ball on for one shot. It scores as the ball on (1 if reds are on).</li>
</ul>
<p>Club games often play without the miss rule; agree before the frame.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="7-Touching-ball-push-and-jump"><a href="#7-Touching-ball-push-and-jump" class="headerlink" title="7 · Touching ball, push and jump"></a>7 · Touching ball, push and jump</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s14_rules.svg" alt="Two side-view sketches. Left: the white leaves the cloth and passes over a red: a jump shot, a foul. Right: the white almost touching a red; the tip still on the white when it meets the red: a push stroke, a foul; a very fine edge is allowed"></p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Spot</th>
<th>The rule (post 14)</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>The white touching a ball</td>
<td>play <strong>away</strong> from it; if that ball is on, you need not hit any ball</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>The white almost touching</td>
<td>a normal stroke is a push; a very fine edge is allowed</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>A ball in the way</td>
<td>jumping over it is always a foul; go round it</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-The-end-of-a-frame"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-The-end-of-a-frame" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — The end of a frame"></a>Chapter 3 — The end of a frame</h1>
<h2 id="8-How-a-frame-ends"><a href="#8-How-a-frame-ends" class="headerlink" title="8 · How a frame ends"></a>8 · How a frame ends</h2>
<ul>
<li>When only the <strong>black</strong> is left, the first <strong>pot or foul</strong> ends the frame.</li>
<li>If the scores are then <strong>equal</strong>, the black is <strong>re-spotted</strong>: toss a coin for who plays first, that player plays from <strong>in-hand</strong>, and again the first pot or foul wins.</li>
</ul>
<p>So a foul on the last black gives your opponent 7 points <strong>and ends the frame</strong>: if that puts them ahead, they win. On a re-spotted black, a safe first shot (thin, white back to baulk) is usually better than a long pot.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="9-Points-left-and-needing-snookers"><a href="#9-Points-left-and-needing-snookers" class="headerlink" title="9 · Points left, and needing snookers"></a>9 · Points left, and needing snookers</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s13_remaining.svg" alt="Bar chart of the most points left on the table by the number of reds left, each red taken with the black: 15 reds 147, 10 reds 107, 5 reds 67, 1 red 35, no reds 27"></p>
<p>Points left = <strong>reds × 8 + 27</strong> (post 13). When only colours are left, add up the colours still on the table:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Colour on</th>
<th>Points left</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>yellow</td>
<td>27</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>green</td>
<td>25</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>brown</td>
<td>22</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>blue</td>
<td>18</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>pink</td>
<td>13</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>black</td>
<td>7</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>If you are behind by <strong>more</strong> than the points left, you &quot;need snookers&quot;: potting alone cannot catch up.</p>
<p><strong>Conceding.</strong> You may offer to give up the frame, and your opponent may say no and play on. In WPBSA play you may <strong>not</strong> concede while you still have a chance (until somebody needs snookers); club manners are the same.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="10-Tactics-ahead-or-behind"><a href="#10-Tactics-ahead-or-behind" class="headerlink" title="10 · Tactics: ahead or behind?"></a>10 · Tactics: ahead or behind?</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s15_tactics.svg" alt="Three boxes. Ahead by more than the points left: your opponent needs snookers, play safe. Close: play your normal game. Behind by more than the points left: you need snookers. Below, the points left with only the colours: 27 from the yellow, 25 green, 22 brown, 18 blue, 13 pink, 7 black"></p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Score</th>
<th>Plan (coaching advice)</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Ahead by more than the points left</td>
<td>take only easy pots; play safe; clear balls off cushions so snookers are hard to lay</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Close</td>
<td>your normal game; pot when the odds are good, safety when they are not (post 11)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Behind by more than the points left</td>
<td>play for snookers, best behind the <strong>last colours</strong>, where few balls are left to escape to (post 09)</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> when you need snookers, pot what you can first. Each pot lowers the points you need from fouls.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-4-—-Practice"><a href="#Chapter-4-—-Practice" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 4 — Practice"></a>Chapter 4 — Practice</h1>
<h2 id="11-The-line-up"><a href="#11-The-line-up" class="headerlink" title="11 · The line-up"></a>11 · The line-up</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s15_lineup.webp" alt="Top view of the whole table. All 15 reds stand in one line along the long middle line of the table, from just past the blue to just short of the black, with a gap around the pink. The colours are on their spots and the white sits in the D"></p>
<p>The best single drill in snooker. Put the 15 reds in a line on the long middle line, between the blue and the black, colours on their spots. Then play red, colour, red, colour… as in a match:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Level 1:</strong> any colour after each red.</li>
<li><strong>Level 2:</strong> only the black, pink or blue.</li>
<li><strong>Level 3:</strong> only the black, and do not let the white touch another red.</li>
</ul>
<p>Write down your best break. Every lesson in this series (pots, stun, angles, position, opening reds) is in it.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="12-A-practice-session"><a href="#12-A-practice-session" class="headerlink" title="12 · A practice session"></a>12 · A practice session</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s15_week.svg" alt="A 70-minute practice session as six bars: straight cue 10 minutes, long blue 10, stop, follow and screw 10, the line-up 20, clearing the colours 10, safety 10"></p>
<p>Coaching advice: short drills first (cue action, a long pot, the three strokes), then a long drill that uses them all, then safety. Score every drill, for example pots out of 10, and keep the numbers. Two or three sessions a week beat one long one.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> practise the shots you miss, not only the ones you like. Pick one weak drill from posts 02–14 each week.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2 id="13-Table-manners"><a href="#13-Table-manners" class="headerlink" title="13 · Table manners"></a>13 · Table manners</h2>
<p>Custom more than rules, but expected in every club:</p>
<ul>
<li>shake hands before and after the frame;</li>
<li>when your opponent plays, sit or stand <strong>still</strong>, away from their line of sight, and stay quiet;</li>
<li>call your own fouls in a friendly game, even if nobody saw;</li>
<li>a raised hand for a lucky pot (a fluke) is polite;</li>
<li>do not concede while the frame can still be won (section 9);</li>
<li>leave the table tidy: balls back, chalk off the cushions.</li>
</ul>
