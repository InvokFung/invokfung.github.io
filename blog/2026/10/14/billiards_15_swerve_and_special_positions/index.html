Billiards & Snooker — 15 · Swerve and Special Spots

Created2026-10-14|Updated2026-10-14|Sports > Billiards
|Post View:

The last lesson in this series is a toolbox for awkward spots. When a ball blocks the line, you can sometimes curve the white round it. When the white stops touching a ball, special rules apply. And a ball hanging in the jaws is easier than it looks, with one trap. For each spot there is more than one answer; the goal is to see them all and choose.

Series map. ★ Basics: 00 Aiming → 01 The hit → 02 Stop, follow, screw → 03 Where the white goes · ★★ Club: 04 Thin cuts → 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker · ★★★ Advanced: 10 Plants and sets → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → 13 Position · ★★★★ Hard: 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → 15 Swerve and special spots (this post).

Learning Outcome

  • Curve the white round a ball with a raised cue and side (swerve).
  • Know what to do when the white is touching a ball.
  • Pot a ball in the jaws without going in-off.
  • Compare several answers for the same spot.

1 · Swerve round a ball

Post 05 showed that side with a raised cue curves the white towards the side. Here it is used on purpose:

  1. Aim past the blocking ball, on the side away from the curve (here left).
  2. Use side towards the red (here right-hand side, half-way out).
  3. Raise the back of the cue (here about 20°) and play at medium pace (power 3).
Cue The same aim and side
level goes straight, misses the red ✗
raised 20° curves round the blue into the red ✓

The room is small: in the model, aims across only about 1° made it round the blue and onto the red.

Watch out: a raised cue makes a clean hit harder, and too much elevation is a massé, a much bigger curve that is hard to control. Practise swerve before you need it in a match, and prefer a cushion escape (posts 09, 14) when one is open.

2 · A touching ball

When the white stops touching another ball, the referee calls "touching ball". Then:

  • You must play away from it (green). Playing into it (red area) moves it: a foul (a push stroke).
  • If the touching ball is a ball on (one you may hit), you do not need to hit another ball.
  • If it is not on, you must still hit a ball on as usual.

So a touching ball is a chance as well as a problem: you can play away to a safe place with no ball to hit, when the touching ball is on.

3 · A ball in the jaws

A red hanging over the pocket goes in from almost any contact:

Red Room in the aim
in the jaws (power 1) 5.4°: any contact pots it
50 cm out (power 2) 1.0°

Common pitfall: the white follows it in. At power 1 no aim went in-off. At power 2, the fullest hits (within 0.4° of the red's centre) sent the white in-off too. Play it soft, or a little thin, so the white goes away from the pocket.

4 · Many answers: how to choose

Most awkward spots have more than one way out. List them, then choose:

Spot Answers Choose by…
a ball blocks the red one cushion (09), two or three cushions (14), swerve (this post) the most room; swerve only when cushions are shut
touching a ball on play away to safety, or pot another red what is safest for you afterwards
red in the jaws pot it soft, or thin; use it as a target for a plant (10) the white's path after
red near the cushion cut it, double it (08), or play safe (12) the double kiss risk (11)

Trick: say the options out loud before you get down. Players who only see one answer often miss a better one.

5 · Try it at the table

Drill Set-up Look for
Swerve ladder the white, a blocking colour 60 cm on, a red 80 cm beyond curve round with the cue raised a little more each time
Touching the white touching a red play away without the red moving (ask a friend to watch it)
Jaws a red over the corner pocket, the white 1 m away pot it ten times without an in-off

Cheat-sheet
Swerve aim past the ball, side towards the target, cue raised, medium pace
Room small; prefer a cushion when one is open
Touching ball play away; no need to hit another ball if it is a ball on
Jaws any contact pots it; soft or thin to stay out
Choose list every answer first
