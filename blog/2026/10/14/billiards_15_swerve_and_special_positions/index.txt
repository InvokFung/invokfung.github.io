11:T2624,<p>The last lesson in this series is a toolbox for awkward spots. When a ball blocks the line, you can sometimes <strong>curve the white round it</strong>. When the white stops touching a ball, special rules apply. And a ball hanging in the jaws is easier than it looks, with one trap. For each spot there is more than one answer; the goal is to see them all and choose.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Series map. <em>★ Basics:</em> 00 Aiming → 01 The hit → 02 Stop, follow, screw → 03 Where the white goes · <em>★★ Club:</em> 04 Thin cuts → 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker · <em>★★★ Advanced:</em> 10 Plants and sets → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → 13 Position · <em>★★★★ Hard:</em> 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → <strong>15 Swerve and special spots</strong> (this post).</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Curve the white round a ball with a <strong>raised cue and side</strong> (swerve).</li>
<li>Know what to do when the white is <strong>touching</strong> a ball.</li>
<li>Pot a ball <strong>in the jaws</strong> without going in-off.</li>
<li>Compare several answers for the same spot.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="1-Swerve-round-a-ball"><a href="#1-Swerve-round-a-ball" class="headerlink" title="1 · Swerve round a ball"></a>1 · Swerve round a ball</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b15_swerve.webp" alt="Two top views of the white, the blue in the middle and a red behind it, all on one line. Both shots aim to the left of the blue with right-hand side, power 3. Cue level: the white goes straight and misses everything, cross. Cue raised 20 degrees: the white starts left, passes the blue and curves right into the red, tick"></p>
<p>Post 05 showed that side with a <strong>raised cue</strong> curves the white towards the side. Here it is used on purpose:</p>
<ol>
<li>Aim <strong>past</strong> the blocking ball, on the side away from the curve (here left).</li>
<li>Use side towards the red (here right-hand side, half-way out).</li>
<li>Raise the back of the cue (here about 20°) and play at medium pace (power 3).</li>
</ol>

<tr>
<th>Cue</th>
<th>The same aim and side</th>

<td>goes straight, misses the red ✗</td>

<td>curves round the blue into the red ✓</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The room is small: in the model, aims across only about <strong>1°</strong> made it round the blue and onto the red.</p>
<div class="bclip"><img class="no-lightbox" src="/blog/assets/billiards/b15_clip_swerve.webp" alt="Clip from behind the white with the cue raised and the tip on the right of the white. The white starts to the left of the blue, curves back to the right after passing it, and hits the red"></div>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> a raised cue makes a clean hit harder, and too much elevation is a <strong>massé</strong>, a much bigger curve that is hard to control. Practise swerve before you need it in a match, and prefer a cushion escape (posts 09, 14) when one is open.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="2-A-touching-ball"><a href="#2-A-touching-ball" class="headerlink" title="2 · A touching ball"></a>2 · A touching ball</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b15_touching.webp" alt="Close top view of the white touching a red. A large green area on the side away from the red shows where you may play; a red area on the red's side shows where you may not"></p>
<p>When the white stops touching another ball, the referee calls <strong>&quot;touching ball&quot;</strong>. Then:</p>
<ul>
<li>You must play <strong>away</strong> from it (green). Playing into it (red area) moves it: a foul (a push stroke).</li>
<li>If the touching ball is a ball <strong>on</strong> (one you may hit), you do not need to hit another ball.</li>
<li>If it is <strong>not</strong> on, you must still hit a ball on as usual.</li>
</ul>
<p>So a touching ball is a chance as well as a problem: you can play away to a safe place with no ball to hit, when the touching ball is on.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="3-A-ball-in-the-jaws"><a href="#3-A-ball-in-the-jaws" class="headerlink" title="3 · A ball in the jaws"></a>3 · A ball in the jaws</h1>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b15_jaws.webp" alt="Two top views of the white and a red near the top right corner. 1: the red hangs in the jaws of the pocket; a wide green wedge from the white shows every aim that pots it, 5.4 degrees. 2: the red 50 cm out on the same line; the wedge is a thin sliver, 1.0 degree"></p>
<p>A red hanging over the pocket goes in from <strong>almost any contact</strong>:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Red</th>
<th>Room in the aim</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>in the jaws (power 1)</td>
<td>5.4°: any contact pots it</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>50 cm out (power 2)</td>
<td>1.0°</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Common pitfall:</strong> the white follows it in. At power 1 no aim went in-off. At power 2, the <strong>fullest</strong> hits (within 0.4° of the red's centre) sent the white in-off too. Play it <strong>soft</strong>, or a little <strong>thin</strong>, so the white goes away from the pocket.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>Most awkward spots have more than one way out. List them, then choose:</p>

<td>the white's path after</td>
