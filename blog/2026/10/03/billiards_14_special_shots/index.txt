</blockquote>

<h2 id="5-Cueing-over-a-ball"><a href="#5-Cueing-over-a-ball" class="headerlink" title="5 · Cueing over a ball"></a>5 · Cueing over a ball</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s01_over_ball.webp" alt="3D side view of the white with a red 15 cm behind it. The cue passes over the red and comes down to the white, raised about 17 degrees; a yellow wedge shows the angle"></p>
<p>When a ball sits just behind the white, the cue has to go <strong>over</strong> it. With a red 15 cm behind, the cue rises about <strong>17°</strong>, so the tip meets the white from above: expect a little swerve if you use side, and less screw. Out of reach, the spider or swan holds the cue over the balls at a similar angle (post 01).</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-2-—-Two-fouls-to-know"><a href="#Chapter-2-—-Two-fouls-to-know" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 2 — Two fouls to know"></a>Chapter 2 — Two fouls to know</h1>
<h2 id="6-The-jump-shot-is-a-foul"><a href="#6-The-jump-shot-is-a-foul" class="headerlink" title="6 · The jump shot is a foul"></a>6 · The jump shot is a foul</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/s14_rules.svg" alt="Two side-view sketches. Left: the white leaves the cloth and passes over a red: a jump shot, a foul. Right: the white almost touching a red; the tip still on the white when it meets the red: a push stroke, a foul; a very fine edge is allowed"></p>
<p>A <strong>jump shot</strong> is the white passing over any part of a ball (WPBSA, simplified; it is allowed only when the white first hits another ball and then jumps). The penalty is the value of the ball on, at least 4.</p>
<p>Unlike pool, snooker has <strong>no legal jump shot</strong> over a ball in the way. The answers are a cushion, a swerve or a massé.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="7-The-push-stroke-and-the-fine-edge"><a href="#7-The-push-stroke-and-the-fine-edge" class="headerlink" title="7 · The push stroke and the fine edge"></a>7 · The push stroke and the fine edge</h2>
<p>A <strong>push stroke</strong> is the tip still touching the white after it starts moving, or when the white meets the ball (picture above, right). The penalty is the value of the ball on or the ball hit, at least 4.</p>
<p>When the white is <strong>almost touching</strong> a ball, a normal stroke is a push. The rule allows one way out: a <strong>very fine edge</strong>, so thin that the tip has gone before the contact. Otherwise, play a different ball, or away.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="8-A-touching-ball"><a href="#8-A-touching-ball" class="headerlink" title="8 · A touching ball"></a>8 · A touching ball</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b15_touching.webp" alt="Close top view of the white touching a red. A large green area on the side away from the red shows where you may play; a red area on the red's side shows where you may not"></p>
<p>If the white stops touching a ball on (or one that could be on), the referee calls <strong>&quot;touching ball&quot;</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li>play the white <strong>away</strong> from it (green) without moving it; moving it is a push stroke;</li>
<li>if that ball is on, you do <strong>not</strong> need to hit any ball at all;</li>
<li>if it could be on (a colour after a red), declare it, then the same applies.</li>
</ul>
<p>So a touching ball is often a gift: play away to a safe place with nothing to hit.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="9-A-ball-in-the-jaws"><a href="#9-A-ball-in-the-jaws" class="headerlink" title="9 · A ball in the jaws"></a>9 · A ball in the jaws</h2>
<p><img src="/blog/assets/billiards/b15_jaws.webp" alt="Two top views of the white and a red near the top right corner. 1: the red hangs in the jaws of the pocket; a wide green wedge from the white shows every aim that pots it, 5.4 degrees. 2: the red 50 cm out on the same line; the wedge is a thin sliver, 1.0 degree"></p>
