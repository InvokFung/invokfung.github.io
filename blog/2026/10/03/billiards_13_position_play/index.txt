11:T1d9c,<p>A break is not a list of pots. It is a chain: each pot must leave the white where the next pot is easy. That is <strong>position play</strong>, and it uses everything so far: stop, follow and screw (02), where the white goes (03), side (06) and cushions (07). This lesson shows how to think about it: aim for a zone, compare routes, and keep an angle.</p>

<p>Series map. <em>★ Basics:</em> 00 Aiming → 01 The hit → 02 Stop, follow, screw → 03 Where the white goes · <em>★★ Club:</em> 04 Thin cuts → 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker · <em>★★★ Advanced:</em> 10 Plants and sets → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → <strong>13 Position</strong> (this post) · <em>★★★★ Hard:</em> 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → 15 Swerve and special spots.</p>

<h1 id="4-How-to-plan-position"><a href="#4-How-to-plan-position" class="headerlink" title="4 · How to plan position"></a>4 · How to plan position</h1>
<ol>
<li><strong>Look two balls ahead.</strong> Where must the white be for the next pot, and for the one after?</li>
<li><strong>Draw the zone</strong>, not a spot. Bigger is better: a zone that is long along the white's path forgives pace errors.</li>
<li><strong>Pick the route</strong> whose path runs <strong>along</strong> the zone, not across it. Then a little too hard or too soft still lands in it.</li>
<li><strong>Choose the simplest tip</strong> that does it: no side if no side works (post 06).</li>
<li><strong>Check the path</strong> for kisses and pockets (post 11).</li>
</ol>
