Billiards & Snooker — 13 · Position Play

Created2026-10-03|Updated2026-10-03|Sports > Billiards
|Post View:

A break is not a list of pots. It is a chain: each pot must leave the white where the next pot is easy. That is position play, and it uses everything so far: stop, follow and screw (02), where the white goes (03), side (06) and cushions (07). This lesson shows how to think about it: aim for a zone, compare routes, and keep an angle.

Series map. ★ Basics: 00 Aiming → 01 The hit → 02 Stop, follow, screw → 03 Where the white goes · ★★ Club: 04 Thin cuts → 05 Side spin → 06 Cut and side into pockets → 07 Cushions → 08 Doubles → 09 Escaping a snooker · ★★★ Advanced: 10 Plants and sets → 11 Kisses and the pack → 12 Safety → 13 Position (this post) · ★★★★ Hard: 14 Two- and three-cushion escapes → 15 Swerve and special spots.

Learning Outcome

  • Aim the white at a zone, not a spot.
  • Compare routes (stun, follow, screw) by where each lands.
  • Know why a small angle on the next ball is better than straight.

1 · A zone, not a point

After this red, you want the black. The green wedges show every place where the pot on the black is comfortable: a cut of 35° or less, from 25 cm to 1 m away, into either top corner. Anywhere in green is fine. That is much easier to hit than a single spot.

Route (power 3) White ends Next black
stun (a little below centre) by the end cushion no comfortable pot ✗
follow (above centre) off the end cushion, in the green a 17° cut ✓
screw (well below centre) just past the red, in the green a 2° cut, almost straight

Two routes reach the zone. Which one is better? That is the next section.

2 · Keep an angle

From each landing spot, pot the black three ways (screw, stun, follow) and look where the white goes:

Black from… The three routes reach…
almost straight (2° cut) only 13 cm off one line
a 17° angle up to 99 cm to the side

A dead-straight pot is easy to pot, but the white can only go forward or back along one line. A small angle lets the white go across the table to the next red. So here the follow route is the better choice.

Trick: good players want an angle of roughly a quarter to a third of a right angle on the next ball. Enough to move the white, not so much that the pot gets hard.

3 · Watch it: follow into the zone

Above centre, power 3: the red drops, the white comes off the end cushion and lands in the green ✓.

4 · How to plan position

  1. Look two balls ahead. Where must the white be for the next pot, and for the one after?
  2. Draw the zone, not a spot. Bigger is better: a zone that is long along the white's path forgives pace errors.
  3. Pick the route whose path runs along the zone, not across it. Then a little too hard or too soft still lands in it.
  4. Choose the simplest tip that does it: no side if no side works (post 06).
  5. Check the path for kisses and pockets (post 11).

5 · Try it at the table

Drill Set-up Look for
Three routes a red near a corner, the black on its spot land in the zone with follow, then with screw
Angle ladder the black from straight, 10°, 20°, 30° how far you can send the white after it
Line-up three reds across the table, then the black pot all four without losing the angle

Cheat-sheet
Zone every place with a comfortable next pot; aim for the middle of it
Route the one whose path runs along the zone
Angle a small one (about 15–30°) beats straight
Tip the simplest that works; side only when needed
Plan two balls ahead
