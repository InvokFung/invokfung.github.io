<h2 id="Terminology"><a href="#Terminology" class="headerlink" title="Terminology"></a>Terminology</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Join</strong> — a combination of rows from two relations according to a predicate.</li>
<li><strong>Join predicate</strong> — the <code>ON</code> condition. Distinct from the <code>WHERE</code> filter, and the distinction <em>matters</em> for outer joins.</li>
<li><strong>Semi-join</strong> — returns rows of the left relation that have at least one match, with no duplication. <code>EXISTS</code>, <code>IN</code>.</li>
<li><strong>Anti-join</strong> — returns rows of the left relation that have <strong>no</strong> match. <code>NOT EXISTS</code>.</li>
<li><strong>Lateral</strong> — a subquery on the right of a join that may reference columns from the left.</li>
</ul>

<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line"> name  | order_id</span><br><span class="line">-------+----------</span><br><span class="line"> Ada   |       10</span><br><span class="line"> Ada   |       11</span><br><span class="line"> Grace |       12</span><br><span class="line"> (null)|       13     ← the orphan surfaces</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p><code>RIGHT JOIN</code> is <code>LEFT JOIN</code> with the tables written in the other order. Most codebases standardise on <code>LEFT</code> for readability; the one place <code>RIGHT</code> earns its keep is when a long chain of joins would otherwise need reordering.</p>

<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line"> name  | order_id</span><br><span class="line">-------+----------</span><br><span class="line"> Ada   |       10</span><br><span class="line"> Ada   |       11</span><br><span class="line"> Grace |       12</span><br><span class="line"> Kat   |   (null)</span><br><span class="line"> Alan  |   (null)</span><br><span class="line"> (null)|       13</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Six rows: the three matches, the two unmatched customers, the one unmatched order. <code>FULL JOIN</code> is the reconciliation tool — &quot;which rows exist on one side but not the other&quot; is a <code>FULL JOIN</code> with <code>WHERE a.id IS NULL OR b.id IS NULL</code>.</p>
<h2 id="CROSS-JOIN-—-every-pairing"><a href="#CROSS-JOIN-—-every-pairing" class="headerlink" title="CROSS JOIN — every pairing"></a><code>CROSS JOIN</code> — every pairing</h2>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-built_in">count</span>(<span class="hljs-operator">*</span>) <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers <span class="hljs-keyword">CROSS</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> orders;   <span class="hljs-comment">-- 4 × 4 = 16</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The legitimate use is generating a <strong>dense grid</strong> so that empty combinations appear as rows rather than going missing — every customer crossed with every calendar day, so zero-revenue days show up as zeroes instead of gaps:</p>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> c.name, d::<span class="hljs-type">date</span>, <span class="hljs-built_in">coalesce</span>(<span class="hljs-built_in">sum</span>(o.total), <span class="hljs-number">0</span>) <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> revenue</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">CROSS</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> generate_series(<span class="hljs-string">&#x27;2026-06-01&#x27;</span>::<span class="hljs-type">date</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;2026-06-30&#x27;</span>, <span class="hljs-type">interval</span> <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;1 day&#x27;</span>) d</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">LEFT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> orders o <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> o.customer_id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> c.id</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">GROUP</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> c.name, d;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Any <em>accidental</em> cross join is a missing <code>ON</code> clause, and shows up in <code>EXPLAIN</code> as a Nested Loop with no join condition and a wildly large row estimate.</p>
<h2 id="ON-vs-WHERE-in-an-outer-join"><a href="#ON-vs-WHERE-in-an-outer-join" class="headerlink" title="ON vs WHERE in an outer join"></a><code>ON</code> vs <code>WHERE</code> in an outer join</h2>
<p>The single most common outer-join bug:</p>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span><br><span class="line">10</span><br><span class="line">11</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">-- WRONG: silently becomes an inner join</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> c.name, o.id</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">LEFT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> orders o <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> o.customer_id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> c.id</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> o.status <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;paid&#x27;</span>;          <span class="hljs-comment">-- NULL = &#x27;paid&#x27; is NULL → Kat and Alan filtered out</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">-- RIGHT: the condition belongs to the join</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> c.name, o.id</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">LEFT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> orders o <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> o.customer_id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> c.id <span class="hljs-keyword">AND</span> o.status <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;paid&#x27;</span>;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p><code>ON</code> decides what <em>matches</em>; <code>WHERE</code> decides what <em>survives</em>. In an outer join, any <code>WHERE</code> predicate on the nullable side removes the padded <code>NULL</code> rows and degrades the outer join to an inner one. For an inner join the two are interchangeable and the planner treats them identically.</p>
<h2 id="USING-and-NATURAL"><a href="#USING-and-NATURAL" class="headerlink" title="USING and NATURAL"></a><code>USING</code> and <code>NATURAL</code></h2>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> customers <span class="hljs-keyword">USING</span> (id);     <span class="hljs-comment">-- merges the equally-named column</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders <span class="hljs-keyword">NATURAL</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> customers;        <span class="hljs-comment">-- joins on ALL equally-named columns</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p><code>USING (col)</code> is fine and produces one merged output column. <code>NATURAL JOIN</code> should be treated as forbidden: it silently changes meaning when someone adds a column named <code>created_at</code> to both tables.</p>
<h2 id="Self-join"><a href="#Self-join" class="headerlink" title="Self-join"></a>Self-join</h2>
<p>A table joined to itself — for hierarchies, adjacency, and pairwise comparison. Aliases become mandatory.</p>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span><br><span class="line">10</span><br><span class="line">11</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">CREATE</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">TABLE</span> employees (id <span class="hljs-type">int</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">PRIMARY</span> KEY, name text, manager_id <span class="hljs-type">int</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">REFERENCES</span> employees(id));</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> e.name <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> employee, m.name <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> manager</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> employees e</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">LEFT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> employees m <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> m.id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> e.manager_id;</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">-- pairwise, without duplicate mirrored pairs</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> a.name, b.name</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers a</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> customers b <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> a.country <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> b.country <span class="hljs-keyword">AND</span> a.id <span class="hljs-operator">&lt;</span> b.id;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The <code>a.id &lt; b.id</code> idiom is how you get unordered pairs exactly once instead of twice plus the self-pair.</p>
<h2 id="LATERAL-—-a-correlated-subquery-in-the-FROM-clause"><a href="#LATERAL-—-a-correlated-subquery-in-the-FROM-clause" class="headerlink" title="LATERAL — a correlated subquery in the FROM clause"></a><code>LATERAL</code> — a correlated subquery in the <code>FROM</code> clause</h2>
<p>Normally a subquery in <code>FROM</code> cannot see the other <code>FROM</code> items. <code>LATERAL</code> (PostgreSQL 9.3+) lifts that restriction, which makes &quot;top N per group&quot; and &quot;most recent per group&quot; natural to express.</p>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span><br><span class="line">10</span><br><span class="line">11</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">-- the two most recent orders per customer</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> c.name, o.id, o.total</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">CROSS</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">LATERAL</span> (</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> o.id, o.total</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders o</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> o.customer_id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> c.id</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">ORDER</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> o.id <span class="hljs-keyword">DESC</span></span><br><span class="line">    LIMIT <span class="hljs-number">2</span></span><br><span class="line">) o;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Use <code>LEFT JOIN LATERAL (...) ON true</code> instead of <code>CROSS JOIN LATERAL</code> if customers with no orders must still appear.</p>
<p><code>LATERAL</code> is often the <em>fastest</em> greatest-n-per-group formulation when an index supports the inner <code>ORDER BY … LIMIT</code>, because it performs one small index scan per outer row rather than sorting the whole table. Set-returning functions in <code>FROM</code> are implicitly lateral:</p>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> p.id, t.tag <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> posts p, <span class="hljs-built_in">unnest</span>(p.tags) <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> t(tag);</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<h1 id="Chapter-2-—-Semi-joins-anti-joins-and-the-NOT-IN-trap"><a href="#Chapter-2-—-Semi-joins-anti-joins-and-the-NOT-IN-trap" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 2 — Semi-joins, anti-joins, and the NOT IN trap"></a>Chapter 2 — Semi-joins, anti-joins, and the <code>NOT IN</code> trap</h1>
<h2 id="Semi-join-has-at-least-one"><a href="#Semi-join-has-at-least-one" class="headerlink" title="Semi-join: &quot;has at least one&quot;"></a>Semi-join: &quot;has at least one&quot;</h2>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">-- all three are semi-joins; the planner produces the same shape</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c <span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">EXISTS</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-number">1</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders o <span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> o.customer_id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> c.id);</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c <span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> c.id <span class="hljs-keyword">IN</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> customer_id <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders);</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">DISTINCT</span> c.<span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> orders o <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> o.customer_id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> c.id;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Prefer <code>EXISTS</code>. It expresses the intent, never duplicates rows, and needs no <code>DISTINCT</code> to clean up afterwards. The <code>JOIN … DISTINCT</code> form is the one to avoid: it materialises the duplicates and then sorts them away.</p>
<h2 id="Anti-join-has-none"><a href="#Anti-join-has-none" class="headerlink" title="Anti-join: &quot;has none&quot;"></a>Anti-join: &quot;has none&quot;</h2>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span><br><span class="line">10</span><br><span class="line">11</span><br><span class="line">12</span><br><span class="line">13</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">-- (a) NOT EXISTS — correct, and plans as an Anti Join</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">NOT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">EXISTS</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-number">1</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders o <span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> o.customer_id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> c.id);</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">-- (b) LEFT JOIN ... IS NULL — also correct, also plans as an Anti Join</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> c.<span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">LEFT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> orders o <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> o.customer_id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> c.id</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> o.id <span class="hljs-keyword">IS</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">NULL</span>;</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">-- (c) NOT IN — BROKEN on our data</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> c.id <span class="hljs-keyword">NOT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">IN</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> customer_id <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders);</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>(a) and (b) both return Kat and Alan. <strong>(c) returns zero rows</strong>, because order 13 has <code>customer_id IS NULL</code>, so every <code>NOT IN</code> comparison evaluates to <code>NULL</code> rather than <code>true</code>, and <code>WHERE</code> keeps only <code>true</code>. No error, no warning — just an empty result that looks plausible.</p>
<p>The correctness argument is the important one, but there is a performance argument too: because <code>NOT IN</code> must preserve those <code>NULL</code> semantics, the planner cannot use a hash anti-join. It typically produces a <code>Filter</code> over a hashed <code>SubPlan</code> instead:</p>

<p>The rule that explains most <code>GROUP BY</code> errors: every column in the select list must be either <strong>in the <code>GROUP BY</code></strong> or <strong>inside an aggregate</strong>. PostgreSQL relaxes this in exactly one case — if you group by a table's primary key, every other column of that table is functionally dependent and may be selected bare:</p>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> c.id, c.name, c.country, <span class="hljs-built_in">count</span>(o.id)     <span class="hljs-comment">-- legal: c.id is the PK</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c <span class="hljs-keyword">LEFT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> orders o <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> o.customer_id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> c.id</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">GROUP</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> c.id;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p><code>WHERE</code> filters rows <em>before</em> grouping; <code>HAVING</code> filters groups <em>after</em>. Putting a non-aggregate condition in <code>HAVING</code> works but forces the aggregation to process rows it will then discard — move it to <code>WHERE</code>.</p>

<p><code>FILTER</code> (SQL:2003, PostgreSQL 9.4+) replaces the <code>sum(CASE WHEN … THEN … ELSE 0 END)</code> idiom. It composes with window functions too.</p>
<h2 id="GROUPING-SETS-ROLLUP-CUBE"><a href="#GROUPING-SETS-ROLLUP-CUBE" class="headerlink" title="GROUPING SETS, ROLLUP, CUBE"></a><code>GROUPING SETS</code>, <code>ROLLUP</code>, <code>CUBE</code></h2>
<p>One query, several grouping levels, one pass over the data.</p>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> c.country, o.status, <span class="hljs-built_in">sum</span>(o.total) <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> revenue</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> orders o <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> o.customer_id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> c.id</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">GROUP</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">GROUPING</span> SETS ((c.country, o.status), (c.country), (o.status), ())</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">ORDER</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> c.country NULLS <span class="hljs-keyword">LAST</span>, o.status NULLS <span class="hljs-keyword">LAST</span>;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span><br><span class="line">10</span><br><span class="line">11</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line"> country | status  | revenue</span><br><span class="line">---------+---------+---------</span><br><span class="line"> GB      | paid    |  120.00</span><br><span class="line"> GB      | pending |   35.50</span><br><span class="line"> GB      | (null)  |  155.50    ← subtotal for GB</span><br><span class="line"> US      | paid    |  899.99</span><br><span class="line"> US      | (null)  |  899.99    ← subtotal for US</span><br><span class="line"> (null)  | paid    | 1019.99    ← subtotal for paid</span><br><span class="line"> (null)  | pending |   35.50</span><br><span class="line"> (null)  | (null)  | 1055.49    ← grand total</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-4-—-DISTINCT-ON"><a href="#Chapter-4-—-DISTINCT-ON" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 4 — DISTINCT ON"></a>Chapter 4 — <code>DISTINCT ON</code></h1>
<p>PostgreSQL-specific, and one of the reasons people miss PostgreSQL on other databases. It keeps the <strong>first row of each group</strong>, where &quot;first&quot; is defined by <code>ORDER BY</code>.</p>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">-- the most expensive order per customer</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">DISTINCT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> (o.customer_id)</span><br><span class="line">       o.customer_id, o.id, o.total, o.status</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders o</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">ORDER</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> o.customer_id, o.total <span class="hljs-keyword">DESC</span>;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line"> customer_id | id | total  | status</span><br><span class="line">-------------+----+--------+--------</span><br><span class="line">      (null) | 13 |  10.00 | paid</span><br><span class="line">           1 | 10 | 120.00 | paid</span><br><span class="line">           2 | 12 | 899.99 | paid</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Two hard rules:</p>
<ol>
<li><code>ORDER BY</code> <strong>must begin</strong> with exactly the <code>DISTINCT ON</code> expressions. Otherwise PostgreSQL raises an error, because &quot;first&quot; would be undefined.</li>
<li>The remaining <code>ORDER BY</code> terms choose which row within the group wins.</li>
</ol>
<p>If the final output needs a different order, wrap it:</p>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> (</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">DISTINCT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> (customer_id) <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">ORDER</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> customer_id, total <span class="hljs-keyword">DESC</span></span><br><span class="line">) t</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">ORDER</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> total <span class="hljs-keyword">DESC</span>;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<h2 id="DISTINCT-ON-vs-window-function-vs-LATERAL"><a href="#DISTINCT-ON-vs-window-function-vs-LATERAL" class="headerlink" title="DISTINCT ON vs window function vs LATERAL"></a><code>DISTINCT ON</code> vs window function vs <code>LATERAL</code></h2>
<p>All three solve greatest-n-per-group:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Approach</th>
<th>Best when</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><code>DISTINCT ON</code></td>
<td>n = 1, concise, and a matching index makes the sort free</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>row_number() OVER (PARTITION BY … ORDER BY …) = n</code></td>
<td>n &gt; 1, or you need the rank value itself</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>LATERAL (SELECT … ORDER BY … LIMIT n)</code></td>
<td>Few outer rows, many inner rows, and an index serves the inner <code>ORDER BY … LIMIT</code></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>With an index on <code>(customer_id, total DESC)</code>, <code>DISTINCT ON</code> can walk the index and skip within each group rather than sorting the table. With a small <code>customers</code> table and a huge <code>orders</code> table, <code>LATERAL</code> usually wins. Measure with <code>EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)</code> — Post 03.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-5-—-Window-functions"><a href="#Chapter-5-—-Window-functions" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 5 — Window functions"></a>Chapter 5 — Window functions</h1>
<p>An aggregate collapses many rows into one. A <strong>window function</strong> computes over a set of related rows and returns one value <strong>per input row</strong>, leaving the row count untouched. That single property solves running totals, rankings, row-to-row deltas, and percent-of-total without a self-join.</p>

<h2 id="Frames-ROWS-vs-RANGE-vs-GROUPS"><a href="#Frames-ROWS-vs-RANGE-vs-GROUPS" class="headerlink" title="Frames: ROWS vs RANGE vs GROUPS"></a>Frames: <code>ROWS</code> vs <code>RANGE</code> vs <code>GROUPS</code></h2>
<p>The frame decides which rows of the partition the function can see for the current row. This is where most window-function surprises live.</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">frame_mode  BETWEEN frame_start AND frame_end [ frame_exclusion ]</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line">frame_mode:   ROWS | RANGE | GROUPS</span><br><span class="line">frame_start:  UNBOUNDED PRECEDING | n PRECEDING | CURRENT ROW | n FOLLOWING</span><br><span class="line">frame_end:    n PRECEDING | CURRENT ROW | n FOLLOWING | UNBOUNDED FOLLOWING</span><br><span class="line">exclusion:    EXCLUDE CURRENT ROW | EXCLUDE GROUP | EXCLUDE TIES | EXCLUDE NO OTHERS</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Mode</th>
<th><code>n PRECEDING</code> counts</th>
<th>Use for</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><code>ROWS</code></td>
<td>Physical rows</td>
<td>Moving averages over the last N rows</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>RANGE</code></td>
<td>A value offset in the <code>ORDER BY</code> column</td>
<td>&quot;Last 7 days&quot; regardless of how many rows fall in it</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>GROUPS</code></td>
<td>Peer groups (rows with equal <code>ORDER BY</code> values)</td>
<td>&quot;The previous 3 distinct dates&quot;</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p><code>RANGE</code> with an offset and <code>GROUPS</code> mode, plus the <code>EXCLUDE</code> clauses, arrived in <strong>PostgreSQL 11</strong>. Before that only <code>ROWS</code> accepted offsets.</p>
<h3 id="The-default-frame-and-why-it-bites"><a href="#The-default-frame-and-why-it-bites" class="headerlink" title="The default frame, and why it bites"></a>The default frame, and why it bites</h3>
<ul>
<li>With <strong>no <code>ORDER BY</code></strong>: the frame is the entire partition. <code>sum()</code> gives the partition total for every row.</li>
<li>With an <strong><code>ORDER BY</code></strong>: the default frame is <code>RANGE BETWEEN UNBOUNDED PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW</code> — which in <code>RANGE</code> mode includes <strong>all peers of the current row</strong>, not just the rows up to it.</li>
</ul>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">-- three orders with total = 100, 100, 200, ordered by total</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> total, <span class="hljs-built_in">sum</span>(total) <span class="hljs-keyword">OVER</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">ORDER</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> total)                           <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> range_default,</span><br><span class="line">              <span class="hljs-built_in">sum</span>(total) <span class="hljs-keyword">OVER</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">ORDER</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> total <span class="hljs-keyword">ROWS</span> UNBOUNDED PRECEDING)  <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> rows_frame</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">VALUES</span> (<span class="hljs-number">100</span>),(<span class="hljs-number">100</span>),(<span class="hljs-number">200</span>)) v(total);</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line"> total | range_default | rows_frame</span><br><span class="line">-------+---------------+------------</span><br><span class="line">   100 |           200 |        100</span><br><span class="line">   100 |           200 |        200</span><br><span class="line">   200 |           400 |        400</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The two tied rows both see the full peer group under <code>RANGE</code>. If you want a strict row-by-row running total, say <code>ROWS</code> explicitly.</p>
<h3 id="The-last_value-trap"><a href="#The-last_value-trap" class="headerlink" title="The last_value trap"></a>The <code>last_value</code> trap</h3>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">-- returns the CURRENT row&#x27;s value, because the default frame ends at CURRENT ROW</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-built_in">last_value</span>(total) <span class="hljs-keyword">OVER</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">PARTITION</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> customer_id <span class="hljs-keyword">ORDER</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> id)</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">-- what you actually meant</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-built_in">last_value</span>(total) <span class="hljs-keyword">OVER</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">PARTITION</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> customer_id <span class="hljs-keyword">ORDER</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> id</span><br><span class="line">                        <span class="hljs-keyword">ROWS</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BETWEEN</span> UNBOUNDED PRECEDING <span class="hljs-keyword">AND</span> UNBOUNDED FOLLOWING)</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p><code>first_value</code> does not have this problem because the default frame starts at <code>UNBOUNDED PRECEDING</code>. An alternative that avoids the whole issue: <code>first_value(total) OVER (… ORDER BY id DESC)</code>.</p>

<h2 id="Where-window-functions-sit-in-evaluation-order"><a href="#Where-window-functions-sit-in-evaluation-order" class="headerlink" title="Where window functions sit in evaluation order"></a>Where window functions sit in evaluation order</h2>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">FROM → WHERE → GROUP BY → HAVING → WINDOW functions → SELECT list → DISTINCT → ORDER BY → LIMIT</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Two consequences:</p>
<ol>
<li>A window function <strong>cannot</strong> appear in <code>WHERE</code>, <code>GROUP BY</code>, or <code>HAVING</code> — those run first. To filter on one, wrap the query:</li>
</ol>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> (</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> o.<span class="hljs-operator">*</span>, <span class="hljs-built_in">row_number</span>() <span class="hljs-keyword">OVER</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">PARTITION</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> customer_id <span class="hljs-keyword">ORDER</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> total <span class="hljs-keyword">DESC</span>) <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> rn</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders o</span><br><span class="line">) t</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> rn <span class="hljs-operator">&lt;=</span> <span class="hljs-number">2</span>;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<ol start="2">
<li>Window functions run <strong>after</strong> aggregation, so they can operate on aggregate results directly:</li>
</ol>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> c.country, <span class="hljs-built_in">sum</span>(o.total) <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> revenue,</span><br><span class="line">       <span class="hljs-built_in">rank</span>() <span class="hljs-keyword">OVER</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">ORDER</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> <span class="hljs-built_in">sum</span>(o.total) <span class="hljs-keyword">DESC</span>) <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> country_rank</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> customers c <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> orders o <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> o.customer_id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> c.id</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">GROUP</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> c.country;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> the &quot;gaps and islands&quot; problem — grouping consecutive runs — is <code>row_number()</code> minus a second <code>row_number()</code>. <code>SELECT grp, min(day), max(day) FROM (SELECT day, day - (row_number() OVER (ORDER BY day))::int AS grp FROM active_days) t GROUP BY grp;</code> collapses consecutive dates into ranges. Worth memorising; it appears constantly in streak, session, and uptime calculations.</p>

<h2 id="Basic-CTEs"><a href="#Basic-CTEs" class="headerlink" title="Basic CTEs"></a>Basic CTEs</h2>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span><br><span class="line">10</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">WITH</span> paid <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> (</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders <span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> status <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;paid&#x27;</span></span><br><span class="line">),</span><br><span class="line">by_customer <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> (</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> customer_id, <span class="hljs-built_in">sum</span>(total) <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> revenue <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> paid <span class="hljs-keyword">GROUP</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> customer_id</span><br><span class="line">)</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> c.name, b.revenue</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> by_customer b <span class="hljs-keyword">JOIN</span> customers c <span class="hljs-keyword">ON</span> c.id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> b.customer_id</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">ORDER</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">BY</span> b.revenue <span class="hljs-keyword">DESC</span>;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>CTEs make a long query readable by naming its stages, and they can be referenced multiple times. Data-modifying CTEs are a PostgreSQL strength:</p>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">WITH</span> archived <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> (</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">DELETE</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders <span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> status <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;cancelled&#x27;</span> RETURNING <span class="hljs-operator">*</span></span><br><span class="line">)</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">INSERT</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">INTO</span> orders_archive <span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> archived;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>All sub-statements of one query see the same snapshot and execute atomically.</p>
<h2 id="The-PostgreSQL-12-fencing-change"><a href="#The-PostgreSQL-12-fencing-change" class="headerlink" title="The PostgreSQL 12 fencing change"></a>The PostgreSQL 12 fencing change</h2>
<p>Before <strong>PostgreSQL 12</strong>, every <code>WITH</code> clause was an <strong>optimisation fence</strong>: it was always materialised into a temporary result, and predicates from the outer query could never be pushed into it. That made CTEs a manual planner hint — sometimes helpful, often a performance trap.</p>
<p>From PostgreSQL 12, a CTE that is <strong>non-recursive, side-effect-free, and referenced exactly once</strong> is inlined by default, exactly like a subquery. Two keywords give explicit control:</p>
<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span><br><span class="line">10</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">WITH</span> heavy <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> MATERIALIZED (          <span class="hljs-comment">-- force the old behaviour: compute once, fence</span></span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> huge_table <span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> expensive_function(x)</span><br><span class="line">)</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> heavy <span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> <span class="hljs-number">42</span>;    <span class="hljs-comment">-- filter applied AFTER materialisation</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">WITH</span> cheap <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">NOT</span> MATERIALIZED (      <span class="hljs-comment">-- force inlining even when referenced many times</span></span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> id, total <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> orders</span><br><span class="line">)</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> cheap <span class="hljs-keyword">WHERE</span> id <span class="hljs-operator">=</span> <span class="hljs-number">42</span>;    <span class="hljs-comment">-- filter pushed down into the CTE</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Situation</th>
<th>Choose</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>

</tr>

<td><code>MATERIALIZED</code></td>

<td><code>NOT MATERIALIZED</code> or the default</td>

<blockquote>
<p><strong>Common pitfall:</strong> code written against PostgreSQL 11 or earlier sometimes uses a CTE deliberately as a fence, with a comment saying so or with no comment at all. Upgrading to 12+ inlines it and the plan changes. If a query regresses after a major upgrade, a CTE that lost its fence is a prime suspect — add <code>MATERIALIZED</code> to test the hypothesis.</p>
</blockquote>

<pre><code class="language-sql"><figure class="highlight sql"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="sql"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">WITH</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">RECURSIVE</span> name (cols) <span class="hljs-keyword">AS</span> (</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-operator">&lt;</span>non<span class="hljs-operator">-</span><span class="hljs-keyword">recursive</span> term<span class="hljs-operator">&gt;</span>          <span class="hljs-comment">-- the seed</span></span><br><span class="line">  <span class="hljs-keyword">UNION</span> [<span class="hljs-keyword">ALL</span>]</span><br><span class="line">    <span class="hljs-operator">&lt;</span><span class="hljs-keyword">recursive</span> term<span class="hljs-operator">&gt;</span>              <span class="hljs-comment">-- references `name`</span></span><br><span class="line">)</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">SELECT</span> <span class="hljs-operator">*</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">FROM</span> name;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<blockquote>

<h1 id="Cheat-sheet"><a href="#Cheat-sheet" class="headerlink" title="Cheat-sheet"></a>Cheat-sheet</h1>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>What</th>
<th>How</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Keep all left rows</td>
<td><code>LEFT JOIN b ON …</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Filter the nullable side</td>
<td>Put the condition in <code>ON</code>, not <code>WHERE</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Reconcile two sets</td>
<td><code>FULL OUTER JOIN … WHERE a.id IS NULL OR b.id IS NULL</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Correlated subquery in <code>FROM</code></td>
<td><code>LEFT JOIN LATERAL (…) ON true</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>&quot;Has at least one&quot;</td>
<td><code>WHERE EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM … WHERE …)</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>&quot;Has none&quot;</td>
<td><code>WHERE NOT EXISTS (…)</code> — never <code>NOT IN</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Conditional aggregate</td>
<td><code>count(*) FILTER (WHERE cond)</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Median / p95</td>
<td><code>percentile_cont(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY x)</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Concatenate a group</td>
<td><code>string_agg(x, ', ' ORDER BY x)</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Subtotals + grand total</td>
<td><code>GROUP BY ROLLUP (a, b)</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Top 1 per group</td>
<td><code>SELECT DISTINCT ON (k) … ORDER BY k, sort_col DESC</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Top N per group</td>
<td><code>row_number() OVER (PARTITION BY k ORDER BY …)</code> in a subquery</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Percent of total</td>
<td><code>x / sum(x) OVER ()</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Running total</td>
<td><code>sum(x) OVER (ORDER BY d ROWS UNBOUNDED PRECEDING)</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Calendar moving average</td>
<td><code>avg(x) OVER (ORDER BY d RANGE BETWEEN interval '6 days' PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW)</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Previous / next row</td>
<td><code>lag(x)</code> / <code>lead(x)</code></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Filter on a window result</td>
<td>Wrap in a subquery or CTE</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Force / prevent CTE inlining</td>
<td><code>AS MATERIALIZED</code> / <code>AS NOT MATERIALIZED</code> (PG 12+)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Tree traversal</td>
<td><code>WITH RECURSIVE … UNION ALL … JOIN cte</code></td>
