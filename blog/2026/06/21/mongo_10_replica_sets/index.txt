11:T777c,<p>A single <code>mongod</code> is a single point of failure. A <strong>replica set</strong> is a group of <code>mongod</code> processes that maintain copies of the same data and automatically elect a new primary when one fails. Three or more members get you durability, automatic failover, optional read scaling on secondaries, and zero-downtime maintenance. This post covers the architecture, the oplog, election protocol, write-concern interaction with replication, secondary read trade-offs, and the canonical 3-member + arbiter topologies.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Series map: 00 Foundations → … → 09 Transactions → <strong>10 Replica Sets</strong> (this post) → 11 Sharding → 12 Change Streams → … → 17 Drivers &amp; Case Studies.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Explain PRIMARY / SECONDARY / ARBITER roles.</li>
<li>Initiate a replica set and inspect <code>rs.status()</code>.</li>
<li>Read the oplog and understand replication lag.</li>
<li>Force an election + observe automatic failover.</li>
<li>Configure priorities, hidden members, delayed members.</li>

</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-1-—-Why-replication"><a href="#Chapter-1-—-Why-replication" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 1 — Why replication"></a>Chapter 1 — Why replication</h1>
<p>Three things at once:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Durability</strong> — N copies of the data; loss of one server doesn't lose data.</li>
<li><strong>High availability</strong> — automatic failover; clients reconnect to a new primary in seconds.</li>
<li><strong>Read scaling</strong> (optional) — secondaries serve eventual-consistent reads.</li>
</ol>
<p>Replication doesn't scale <strong>writes</strong> — every write still goes through one primary. For write scaling, you shard (Post 11) on top of replica sets.</p>
<hr>

<p>A typical minimal production set: <strong>3 data-bearing members</strong> (1 primary + 2 secondaries). Loss of any one keeps a majority alive.</p>
<p>Avoid:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Even number of voting members</strong> without an arbiter → election can split. Always odd.</li>
<li><strong>Arbiter-only failover safety in 2026</strong> → arbiters can't vote on majority writes; prefer a 3-data-member setup unless cost-constrained. (See &quot;election + write concern&quot; pitfall in Chapter 8.)</li>
</ul>

<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">mongodb://host1:27017,host2:27017,host3:27017/?replicaSet=rs0</span><br><span class="line">mongodb+srv://cluster.abcd.mongodb.net/?retryWrites=true     # SRV form, Atlas</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-5-—-The-oplog"><a href="#Chapter-5-—-The-oplog" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 5 — The oplog"></a>Chapter 5 — The oplog</h1>
<p>The <strong>oplog</strong> is a capped collection (<code>local.oplog.rs</code>) on every replica-set member that records every write applied. Secondaries tail it and replay it to stay in sync.</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line">use local</span><br><span class="line">db.<span class="hljs-property">oplog</span>.<span class="hljs-property">rs</span>.<span class="hljs-title function_">find</span>().<span class="hljs-title function_">sort</span>({ <span class="hljs-attr">ts</span>: -<span class="hljs-number">1</span> }).<span class="hljs-title function_">limit</span>(<span class="hljs-number">5</span>)</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Each entry: timestamp, operation type (insert/update/delete), namespace, document or update spec.</p>
<h2 id="Oplog-size-matters"><a href="#Oplog-size-matters" class="headerlink" title="Oplog size matters"></a>Oplog size matters</h2>
<p>Default size: 5 % of free disk (with a 50 GB upper cap on most platforms). If a secondary falls behind by more oplog entries than fit, it goes <strong>STALE</strong> and must do a full resync — slow.</p>
<p>Tune via <code>--oplogSizeMB</code> at start, or <code>replSetResizeOplog</code> admin command. Atlas auto-sizes.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> if you regularly run multi-hour ingest jobs while secondaries catch up, increase oplog size. The &quot;1 day of writes fits&quot; rule of thumb prevents most stale-member surprises.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-6-—-Replication-mechanics-lag"><a href="#Chapter-6-—-Replication-mechanics-lag" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 6 — Replication mechanics + lag"></a>Chapter 6 — Replication mechanics + lag</h1>
<ol>
<li>Client writes to PRIMARY.</li>
<li>PRIMARY records the write to its own oplog and applies it.</li>
<li>Each SECONDARY tails the primary's oplog and applies in order.</li>
<li>Once a majority of voters have applied the write, it's &quot;majority committed&quot;.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Replication lag</strong> = how far behind the primary a secondary is in oplog time.</p>
<p>Inspect:</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line">rs.<span class="hljs-title function_">printSecondaryReplicationInfo</span>()</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">// returns each secondary&#x27;s lag in seconds</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Causes of lag:</p>
<ul>
<li>Big write spike (secondaries can't keep up).</li>
<li>Slow disk on a secondary.</li>
<li>Network latency (especially across regions).</li>
<li>Long oplog entries (huge updates).</li>
</ul>
<p>Persistent lag &gt; a few seconds = warning. Lag growing unbounded = crisis (eventually goes STALE).</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-7-—-Elections"><a href="#Chapter-7-—-Elections" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 7 — Elections"></a>Chapter 7 — Elections</h1>
<p>If the PRIMARY becomes unreachable, voting members hold an election (under the hood: Raft-like protocol Version 1, &quot;PV1&quot;).</p>
<p>Steps:</p>
<ol>
<li>A secondary that hasn't heard from the primary for <code>electionTimeoutMillis</code> (default 10 s) calls an election.</li>
<li>Other voting members vote yes if the candidate's oplog is up-to-date enough.</li>
<li>Majority of votes → new PRIMARY.</li>
<li>Drivers detect the change via heartbeats; subsequent writes go to the new primary.</li>
</ol>
<p>Typical failover: <strong>10-20 seconds</strong> for clients to reconnect; <strong>retryable writes</strong> (Post 08) hide most of the disruption.</p>
<h2 id="Force-a-manual-failover-test"><a href="#Force-a-manual-failover-test" class="headerlink" title="Force a manual failover (test)"></a>Force a manual failover (test)</h2>

<h3 id="Arbiter-pitfall"><a href="#Arbiter-pitfall" class="headerlink" title="Arbiter pitfall"></a>Arbiter pitfall</h3>
<p>If you have a 2-data + 1-arbiter set (total 3 voters, majority = 2), arbiters can vote in elections but <strong>don't store data</strong>. A write reaching only the primary + the arbiter (waiting for the second data member) can be lost in failover. Modern guidance: <strong>avoid arbiters in 2026 for any system where data loss isn't acceptable</strong>. Use 3 data-bearing members.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-13-—-Rolling-maintenance-pattern"><a href="#Chapter-13-—-Rolling-maintenance-pattern" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 13 — Rolling maintenance pattern"></a>Chapter 13 — Rolling maintenance pattern</h1>
<p>To upgrade or restart members without downtime:</p>
<ol>
<li>Pick a secondary. Drain its connections.</li>
<li>Stop + upgrade + restart it. Wait for SECONDARY state.</li>
<li>Repeat for the next secondary.</li>
<li>Step down the primary (<code>rs.stepDown()</code>); it becomes secondary; another member becomes primary.</li>
<li>Stop + upgrade + restart the old primary. Wait for SECONDARY.</li>
</ol>
<p>Zero downtime as long as a majority is always up. This is the &quot;rolling upgrade&quot; pattern for any major version bump or OS patch.</p>
<p>Atlas does this automatically when you click &quot;Upgrade&quot;.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-14-—-Common-pitfalls"><a href="#Chapter-14-—-Common-pitfalls" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 14 — Common pitfalls"></a>Chapter 14 — Common pitfalls</h1>

<p>Configure member 2 with <code>secondaryDelaySecs: 60</code>. Insert a doc. Immediately read from member 2 (direct connection) — should not see it. Wait 90 s, read again — should see it.</p>
