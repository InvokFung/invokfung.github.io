f:Tdb99,<p>Prompting is the practice of writing inputs that steer a Large Language Model (LLM) toward useful outputs. It is part interface design, part rhetoric, and part empirical science. This note covers the foundations and the techniques that have been validated by published research, with worked examples you can copy.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Explain what a prompt is and why model behaviour is sensitive to its phrasing.</li>
<li>Decompose a prompt into its functional parts (instruction, context, examples, format spec).</li>
<li>Apply core techniques: zero-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought, role conditioning, decomposition, and self-consistency.</li>
<li>Recognise common anti-patterns and explain why they fail.</li>
<li>Design an evaluation harness with a golden set + rubric + A/B framework.</li>
<li>Read a research paper on prompting and judge whether its claims will transfer to your task.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-1-Foundations"><a href="#Chapter-1-Foundations" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 1 - Foundations"></a>Chapter 1 - Foundations</h1>
<h2 id="Terminology"><a href="#Terminology" class="headerlink" title="Terminology"></a>Terminology</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Prompt</strong> — the full token sequence the model conditions on before generating the next token.</li>
<li><strong>Completion</strong> — the model's generated response.</li>
<li><strong>Token</strong> — sub-word unit (~4 characters of English on average). All limits (context window, throughput, billing) are measured in tokens.</li>
<li><strong>Context window</strong> — maximum prompt+completion length the model can attend to.</li>
<li><strong>In-context learning (ICL)</strong> — model adapts its behaviour from examples in the prompt without weight updates. Reported in <em>Language Models are Few-Shot Learners</em>, Brown et al. (2020).</li>
<li><strong>Temperature</strong> — sampling parameter; <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">0</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">0</span></span></span></span> is greedy, higher values increase diversity.</li>
<li><strong>Top-p / Top-k</strong> — nucleus / top-k sampling, alternatives to temperature.</li>
<li><strong>System / developer message</strong> — privileged prompt segment used to set persistent behaviour. Mechanism formalised by <em>The Instruction Hierarchy</em>, Wallace et al. (2024).</li>
</ul>

class="mpunct">,</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mord"><span class="mord mathnormal">t</span><span class="msupsub"><span class="vlist-t vlist-t2"><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.3011em;"><span style="top:-2.55em;margin-left:0em;margin-right:0.05em;"><span class="pstrut" style="height:2.7em;"></span><span class="sizing reset-size6 size3 mtight"><span class="mord mtight"><span class="mord mathnormal mtight">n</span><span class="mbin mtight">−</span><span class="mord mtight">1</span></span></span></span></span><span class="vlist-s">​</span></span><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.2083em;"><span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span>. Two prompts that read as semantically equivalent to a human can produce very different distributions because the model is sensitive to token-level cues it has learned to associate with answer quality (formality, code-block fences, &quot;Let's think step by step&quot;, etc.).</p>
<p>This sensitivity has been measured empirically. Lu et al. (2022) showed that simply reordering few-shot examples can swing accuracy on classification benchmarks by tens of percentage points. Sclar et al. (2024) showed that benign formatting changes (newline vs. colon as separator) shift task accuracy by a comparable margin.</p>
<p>The practical consequence: <strong>prompts are programs</strong>. They deserve version control, testing, and review.</p>
<h2 id="How-the-model-reads-a-prompt"><a href="#How-the-model-reads-a-prompt" class="headerlink" title="How the model &quot;reads&quot; a prompt"></a>How the model &quot;reads&quot; a prompt</h2>
<p>A typical chat-tuned model receives a structured sequence — for instance OpenAI's ChatML or Anthropic's <code>Human:</code>/<code>Assistant:</code> turn markers — where each turn is tagged with a role. The model has been fine-tuned (instruction-tuned + RLHF or DPO) to:</p>
<ol>
<li>Treat the system / developer message as a high-priority constraint.</li>
<li>Treat user messages as questions or requests.</li>
<li>Treat its own prior responses as commitments to maintain consistency with.</li>
</ol>
<p>The training procedure was first detailed in <em>InstructGPT</em>, Ouyang et al. (2022) and the Anthropic RLHF paper, Bai et al. (2022). The hierarchy of trust between roles was later sharpened in <em>The Instruction Hierarchy</em>, Wallace et al. (2024).</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-3-Core-Techniques"><a href="#Chapter-3-Core-Techniques" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 - Core Techniques"></a>Chapter 3 - Core Techniques</h1>
<h2 id="Zero-shot"><a href="#Zero-shot" class="headerlink" title="Zero-shot"></a>Zero-shot</h2>
<p>Ask the question directly with no examples. Works when the task is well-represented in the training distribution (translation, summarisation, common QA).</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">Translate to French: &quot;The cat sat on the mat.&quot;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<h2 id="Few-shot-in-context-learning"><a href="#Few-shot-in-context-learning" class="headerlink" title="Few-shot (in-context learning)"></a>Few-shot (in-context learning)</h2>
<p>Provide <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.03148em;">k</span></span></span></span> input/output pairs, then the new input. Brown et al. (2020) showed few-shot prompting closes a large fraction of the gap to fine-tuning on many tasks without any gradient updates.</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">Q: What is 17 + 5?      A: 22</span><br><span class="line">Q: What is 31 + 9?      A: 40</span><br><span class="line">Q: What is 124 + 76?    A:</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Tips:</p>
<ul>
<li>Order examples easy → hard (Lu et al., 2022).</li>
<li>Make examples cover the failure modes you observe in zero-shot, not the easy cases.</li>
<li>Keep formatting identical across shots; a stray newline can degrade accuracy (Sclar et al., 2024).</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="Chain-of-Thought-CoT-prompting"><a href="#Chain-of-Thought-CoT-prompting" class="headerlink" title="Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting"></a>Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting</h2>
<p>Ask the model to <strong>show its work</strong> before committing to an answer. Reported in <em>Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models</em>, Wei et al. (2022). Improves arithmetic, symbolic, and commonsense reasoning on large models.</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">Q: Roger has 5 tennis balls. He buys 2 more cans of tennis balls. Each can has 3 tennis balls. How many tennis balls does he have now?</span><br><span class="line">A: Let&#x27;s think step by step.</span><br><span class="line">   Roger starts with 5.</span><br><span class="line">   He buys 2 cans × 3 balls = 6 more.</span><br><span class="line">   Total = 5 + 6 = 11.</span><br><span class="line">   The answer is 11.</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Two variants worth knowing:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Zero-shot CoT</strong>: append &quot;Let's think step by step.&quot; Kojima et al. (2022). Cheap and surprisingly effective.</li>
<li><strong>Self-consistency</strong>: sample <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span></span></span></span> chains at temperature <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>&gt;</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">&gt;0</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.5782em;vertical-align:-0.0391em;"></span><span class="mrel">&gt;</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">0</span></span></span></span>, take the majority vote on the final answer. Wang et al. (2022). Improves arithmetic accuracy further at <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi><mo>×</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n \times</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6667em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span><span class="mord">×</span></span></span></span> cost.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="Role-persona-conditioning"><a href="#Role-persona-conditioning" class="headerlink" title="Role / persona conditioning"></a>Role / persona conditioning</h2>
<p>Setting an expert persona biases vocabulary, depth, and inferred audience. The effect is real but smaller than CoT or few-shot; treat it as fine-tuning of <em>style</em>, not capability.</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">You are a tenured statistics professor teaching a graduate seminar.</span><br><span class="line">Explain the bias-variance trade-off using a regression example.</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<h2 id="Decomposition"><a href="#Decomposition" class="headerlink" title="Decomposition"></a>Decomposition</h2>
<p>Break a hard problem into ordered sub-problems and prompt each separately, chaining outputs. Equivalent to Khot et al. (2023) &quot;Decomposed Prompting&quot; or <em>least-to-most prompting</em> by Zhou et al. (2023).</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">Step 1 prompt: Extract every named entity from &lt;text&gt;. Return JSON list.</span><br><span class="line">Step 2 prompt: For each entity in &lt;list from Step 1&gt;, classify as person/org/location.</span><br><span class="line">Step 3 prompt: For each person, look up role using &lt;retrieval tool&gt;.</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The chain is easier to debug than a single monolithic prompt; you can swap or rerun any step.</p>
<h2 id="ReAct-Reasoning-Acting"><a href="#ReAct-Reasoning-Acting" class="headerlink" title="ReAct (Reasoning + Acting)"></a>ReAct (Reasoning + Acting)</h2>
<p>Interleave reasoning steps with tool calls. Yao et al. (2023). The model alternates between <code>Thought:</code> (a reasoning step), <code>Action:</code> (a tool call), and <code>Observation:</code> (tool result), until ready to emit an <code>Answer:</code>.</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">Question: Who is the current CEO of OpenAI and when were they born?</span><br><span class="line">Thought: I need to look up the current CEO.</span><br><span class="line">Action: search[&quot;current CEO of OpenAI&quot;]</span><br><span class="line">Observation: Sam Altman.</span><br><span class="line">Thought: Now find their birth date.</span><br><span class="line">Action: search[&quot;Sam Altman date of birth&quot;]</span><br><span class="line">Observation: April 22, 1985.</span><br><span class="line">Answer: Sam Altman, born April 22, 1985.</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>ReAct is the foundation pattern under most &quot;agent&quot; frameworks (LangChain, LlamaIndex, modern function-calling SDKs).</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-4-Structural-Patterns"><a href="#Chapter-4-Structural-Patterns" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 4 - Structural Patterns"></a>Chapter 4 - Structural Patterns</h1>
<h2 id="Delimiters"><a href="#Delimiters" class="headerlink" title="Delimiters"></a>Delimiters</h2>
<p>Use unambiguous markers around user-supplied or retrieved content. Three formats all work, in roughly equal measure:</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">1.  Triple backtick fences:    ```...```</span><br><span class="line">2.  Triple angle brackets:     &lt;&lt;&lt;...&gt;&gt;&gt;</span><br><span class="line">3.  XML-style tags:             &lt;article&gt;...&lt;/article&gt;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>XML tags have the advantage of being self-documenting and survive whitespace normalisation. Anthropic explicitly recommends XML in their prompt-engineering documentation.</p>
<h2 id="Markdown-structure"><a href="#Markdown-structure" class="headerlink" title="Markdown structure"></a>Markdown structure</h2>
<p>Models trained on heavy Markdown corpora respond well to headings (<code>## Step 1</code>), lists, and tables. Use the same conventions in your prompt that you want to see in the output.</p>
<h2 id="Numbered-constraints"><a href="#Numbered-constraints" class="headerlink" title="Numbered constraints"></a>Numbered constraints</h2>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">Constraints:</span><br><span class="line">1. Output must be valid JSON parsable by `JSON.parse`.</span><br><span class="line">2. All numeric fields must be integers.</span><br><span class="line">3. If you are uncertain, set `confidence` &lt; 0.5.</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Numbered constraints are easier for the model to enumerate against than a paragraph of &quot;and also, make sure to...&quot;</p>
<h2 id="Format-specifications"><a href="#Format-specifications" class="headerlink" title="Format specifications"></a>Format specifications</h2>
<p>Three escalating strengths:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Strength</th>
<th>Mechanism</th>
<th>Reliability</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Soft</td>
<td>Natural-language description (&quot;return JSON&quot;)</td>
<td>~60-80%</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Schema</td>
<td>Provide explicit schema (TypeScript / JSON Schema / Pydantic)</td>
<td>~90%</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Hard</td>
<td>Use the provider's structured-output / JSON-mode API</td>
<td>~100% (provider-enforced)</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>If you need a guaranteed shape, use the provider's structured output. Soft format is convenient but will fail on tail cases.</p>

<h2 id="Tree-of-Thoughts-ToT"><a href="#Tree-of-Thoughts-ToT" class="headerlink" title="Tree of Thoughts (ToT)"></a>Tree of Thoughts (ToT)</h2>
<p>Yao et al. (2023). Generalises CoT from a chain to a search tree. The model proposes multiple next-thoughts at each step, a value model scores each, and a search algorithm (BFS/DFS) explores. Helps on combinatorial problems (Game of 24, crosswords) where a single chain commits too early.</p>
<h2 id="Reflexion"><a href="#Reflexion" class="headerlink" title="Reflexion"></a>Reflexion</h2>
<p>Shinn et al. (2023). After a wrong attempt, the model writes a critique of its own trace; that critique is appended to context for the next attempt. The agent improves across attempts within a single session.</p>
<h2 id="Step-Back-prompting"><a href="#Step-Back-prompting" class="headerlink" title="Step-Back prompting"></a>Step-Back prompting</h2>
<p>Zheng et al. (2024). Before answering a specific question, ask the model to first derive the <strong>general principle</strong> that governs the question. The principle then conditions the specific answer. Improves abstract / multi-step reasoning.</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">Question: A car accelerates from 10 m/s to 30 m/s over 4 s. What is the distance?</span><br><span class="line">Step back: What kinematic formula applies when acceleration is uniform and initial+final velocities are known?</span><br><span class="line">Principle: s = (v0 + v) / 2 × t.</span><br><span class="line">Apply: s = (10 + 30) / 2 × 4 = 80 m.</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-6-Common-Anti-patterns"><a href="#Chapter-6-Common-Anti-patterns" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 6 - Common Anti-patterns"></a>Chapter 6 - Common Anti-patterns</h1>
<h2 id="Vague-verbs"><a href="#Vague-verbs" class="headerlink" title="Vague verbs"></a>Vague verbs</h2>
<p><code>improve</code>, <code>optimise</code>, <code>make better</code>, <code>enhance</code> are unmeasurable. The model has to guess a direction. Replace with a measurable verb: <code>shorten to 100 words</code>, <code>rewrite at a 10th-grade reading level</code>, <code>remove all passive voice</code>.</p>
<h2 id="Conflicting-constraints"><a href="#Conflicting-constraints" class="headerlink" title="Conflicting constraints"></a>Conflicting constraints</h2>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">Be concise. Cover every detail. Use simple words. Use precise terminology.</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The model resolves contradictions arbitrarily. Pick one side of each axis and state it.</p>
<h2 id="Leading-questions"><a href="#Leading-questions" class="headerlink" title="Leading questions"></a>Leading questions</h2>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">&quot;Why is Python better than JavaScript for backend work?&quot;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The framing forces the model to defend a premise instead of evaluating it. Prefer:</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">&quot;Compare Python and JavaScript for backend work along: performance, ecosystem, hiring, deployment. Cite trade-offs.&quot;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<h2 id="Long-irrelevant-context"><a href="#Long-irrelevant-context" class="headerlink" title="Long irrelevant context"></a>Long irrelevant context</h2>
<p>Liu et al. (2023) <em>Lost in the Middle</em> showed models degrade on retrieval tasks when relevant information sits in the middle of a long context. Prune aggressively. If you must include long retrieved documents, put the most relevant snippets at the <strong>start</strong> or the <strong>end</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="Negation-only-instructions"><a href="#Negation-only-instructions" class="headerlink" title="Negation-only instructions"></a>Negation-only instructions</h2>
<p><code>Do not list more than 3 items.</code> is weaker than <code>List exactly 3 items.</code> Models follow positive constraints more reliably than negative ones.</p>
<h2 id="Polite-filler"><a href="#Polite-filler" class="headerlink" title="&quot;Polite filler&quot;"></a>&quot;Polite filler&quot;</h2>
<p><code>Please, if you could, kindly attempt to consider...</code> adds tokens without affecting behaviour. Strip it.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-7-Evaluation"><a href="#Chapter-7-Evaluation" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 7 - Evaluation"></a>Chapter 7 - Evaluation</h1>
<p>A prompt is a piece of software, so evaluate it like software.</p>
<h2 id="Golden-set"><a href="#Golden-set" class="headerlink" title="Golden set"></a>Golden set</h2>
<p>A small, curated set of representative inputs with hand-graded expected outputs. 30–100 items is enough to detect a 5pp accuracy shift.</p>
<h2 id="Rubric"><a href="#Rubric" class="headerlink" title="Rubric"></a>Rubric</h2>
<p>Decompose &quot;is the output good?&quot; into yes/no axes. For a summarisation task you might score:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Factual</strong> (no hallucinated entities)</li>
<li><strong>Complete</strong> (all named main points present)</li>
<li><strong>Concise</strong> (within length limit)</li>
<li><strong>Format</strong> (parses as JSON)</li>
<li><strong>Refusal</strong> (correctly refused when no claim present)</li>
</ul>
<p>Each axis scored 0/1 by a human grader or a separate &quot;judge&quot; model.</p>
<h2 id="LLM-as-judge"><a href="#LLM-as-judge" class="headerlink" title="LLM-as-judge"></a>LLM-as-judge</h2>
<p>Zheng et al. (2023). A stronger model grades a weaker model's output against a rubric. Cheap, scales, but biased toward outputs that look like the judge's style. Use a different family of model as the judge where possible, and spot-check 10% with a human.</p>
<h2 id="AB-framework"><a href="#AB-framework" class="headerlink" title="A/B framework"></a>A/B framework</h2>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line">v1 prompt + golden set → score_v1</span><br><span class="line">v2 prompt + golden set → score_v2</span><br><span class="line">Δ = score_v2 - score_v1</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Track <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">Δ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\Delta</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord">Δ</span></span></span></span> per axis, not just overall. A change that improves factuality but hurts conciseness is still a regression on the conciseness axis.</p>
<h2 id="Statistical-sanity"><a href="#Statistical-sanity" class="headerlink" title="Statistical sanity"></a>Statistical sanity</h2>
<p>With <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>100</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n=100</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">100</span></span></span></span> binary outcomes, the 95% confidence interval on accuracy is roughly <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>±</mo><mn>10</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\pm 10</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.7278em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mord">±</span><span class="mord">10</span></span></span></span>pp around the observed rate. Differences smaller than that are noise. Increase <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>n</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">n</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span></span></span></span> or repeat the run to tighten.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-8-Tool-Use-and-Structured-Output"><a href="#Chapter-8-Tool-Use-and-Structured-Output" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 8 - Tool Use and Structured Output"></a>Chapter 8 - Tool Use and Structured Output</h1>
<p>Modern model providers expose two APIs that should replace most prompt-engineering hacks:</p>
<h2 id="Function-calling-tool-use"><a href="#Function-calling-tool-use" class="headerlink" title="Function calling / tool use"></a>Function calling / tool use</h2>
<p>You declare a JSON schema for one or more tools; the model picks one and emits arguments matching the schema. The runtime executes the tool and returns the result, which you append to the conversation and prompt again.</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">tools:</span><br><span class="line">  - name: get_weather</span><br><span class="line">    parameters: { location: string, unit: enum[&quot;c&quot;,&quot;f&quot;] }</span><br><span class="line">  - name: web_search</span><br><span class="line">    parameters: { query: string }</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The model is fine-tuned to know when to call a tool versus answer directly. This is more reliable than ReAct in raw text because the model emits parsed JSON via the API.</p>
<h2 id="Structured-output-JSON-Pydantic-mode"><a href="#Structured-output-JSON-Pydantic-mode" class="headerlink" title="Structured output (JSON / Pydantic mode)"></a>Structured output (JSON / Pydantic mode)</h2>
<p>You supply the full output schema; the provider's decoder constrains generation so the response is guaranteed valid against the schema. Eliminates &quot;the model added a friendly preamble that broke my parser&quot; failures.</p>
<h2 id="When-NOT-to-use-tools"><a href="#When-NOT-to-use-tools" class="headerlink" title="When NOT to use tools"></a>When NOT to use tools</h2>
<p>If the task is pure transformation of text the model already has the knowledge for (summarise, translate, classify), tools add latency and cost. Use tools when you need fresh data, deterministic computation, or side effects.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-9-Putting-It-Together"><a href="#Chapter-9-Putting-It-Together" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 9 - Putting It Together"></a>Chapter 9 - Putting It Together</h1>
<p>A production prompt for &quot;extract action items from a meeting transcript&quot; — combining roles, delimiters, few-shot, format, and a guardrail:</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span><br><span class="line">10</span><br><span class="line">11</span><br><span class="line">12</span><br><span class="line">13</span><br><span class="line">14</span><br><span class="line">15</span><br><span class="line">16</span><br><span class="line">17</span><br><span class="line">18</span><br><span class="line">19</span><br><span class="line">20</span><br><span class="line">21</span><br><span class="line">22</span><br><span class="line">23</span><br><span class="line">24</span><br><span class="line">25</span><br><span class="line">26</span><br><span class="line">27</span><br><span class="line">28</span><br><span class="line">29</span><br><span class="line">30</span><br><span class="line">31</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">You are a meeting analyst.</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line">Your task: extract every action item from the transcript below. An action item</span><br><span class="line">is a commitment a specific named person made to do a specific thing by an</span><br><span class="line">optional deadline.</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line">Examples:</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line">Transcript:</span><br><span class="line">  Alice: I&#x27;ll send the deck to Bob by Friday.</span><br><span class="line">  Bob: Sounds good.</span><br><span class="line">Output:</span><br><span class="line">  [{&quot;owner&quot;: &quot;Alice&quot;, &quot;task&quot;: &quot;send the deck to Bob&quot;, &quot;due&quot;: &quot;Friday&quot;}]</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line">Transcript:</span><br><span class="line">  Carlos: Maybe we should think about the budget.</span><br><span class="line">  Dana: Yeah, let&#x27;s think about it.</span><br><span class="line">Output:</span><br><span class="line">  []</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line">Transcript:</span><br><span class="line">&lt;&lt;&lt;</span><br><span class="line">{transcript}</span><br><span class="line">&gt;&gt;&gt;</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line">Rules:</span><br><span class="line">1. <h2 id="Quick-sanity-checks-before-shipping"><a href="#Quick-sanity-checks-before-shipping" class="headerlink" title="Quick sanity checks before shipping"></a>Quick sanity checks before shipping</h2>
<ol>
<li>Does the prompt have all six anatomy parts (role, instruction, context, examples, format, stop)?</li>
<li>Is there a golden set of ≥ 30 items with rubric scores?</li>
<li>Have you tested the failure modes, not just the happy path?</li>
<li>Are you using structured output for any machine-parsed response?</li>
<li>Have you token-counted the prompt under realistic context lengths?</li>
<li>Have you versioned the prompt in git with a short rationale per change?</li>
</ol>
