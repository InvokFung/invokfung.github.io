Violin Notes — 02 · Grade 2 Two-Octave Scales & Slur Control

Created2026-05-22|Updated2026-05-22|Music
|Post View:

Grade 2 ABRSM violin doubles your reach: two-octave scales, the first minor key, dotted rhythms, and longer slurs that demand real bow planning. The hand shape from Grade 1 stays, but it now travels — string crossings within a scale become the central skill.

Series map: 00 Beginner → 01 Grade 1 → 02 Grade 2 (this post) → 03 Grade 3 → … → 08 Grade 8.

Learning Outcome

  • Play two-octave scales of G and D major, one-octave C and F major.
  • Play A natural minor (one octave) and recognise the relative-minor concept.
  • Use dotted-quaver + semiquaver rhythms accurately.
  • Slur 4–8 notes per bow with even tone.
  • Read fluently up to top D / E above the staff (E string, 4th finger).
  • Play 4–6 Grade 2 pieces with hands together, musically shaped.

Chapter 1 — Two-octave scales arrive

A two-octave scale spans two strings (Grade 1 used two strings for one octave); now you traverse three strings.

G major (two octaves)

Grade 2 typically asks for the scale to reach G5 (3rd finger on E in 1st position). Some editions extend to A5.

D major (two octaves)

Top note is A5.

C major (one octave) — first piece with no sharps

Trick: C major is the shock key for violin beginners. Every other scale you've played has the half-step between 2 and 3. C major has it between 1 and 2 on the D string. Mark the music with an arrow over the F natural for the first 2 weeks.

F major (one octave)

Half-step lands between finger 1 and 2 on the A string (B♭ → C). The B♭ requires placing finger 1 low — closer to the nut than normal.

Where I got stuck: "low 1" felt unnatural for weeks. The finger wants to land where it always does. Fix: physically mark a tape line for "low 1" position on the A string for one practice session. Once your finger learns the distance, peel the tape off.

Chapter 2 — The first minor scale: A natural minor

A natural minor uses the same notes as C major but starts on A:

Fingering (one octave starting from open A):

Or starting on the G string for low-octave practice: 1=A (low) ... but Grade 2 starts on open A.

What "natural minor" means

Three forms of minor exist:

Form Pattern (intervals) Where used
Natural W-H-W-W-H-W-W Grade 2 introduction
Harmonic Raises 7th by half-step Grade 3+
Melodic Raises 6th + 7th going up, naturals coming down Grade 4+

Grade 2 only requires natural minor. Memorise it now; the others build on it.

Trick: A natural minor = the white notes from A to A. C major = the white notes from C to C. They share all notes — they're relative. Every major key has a relative minor a minor 3rd (3 semitones) below. Memorise this pairing now: it saves hours later.

Bow distribution for dotted rhythms

The long note gets more bow, the short note gets less. Roughly 3:1 ratio matching the duration:

If you give them equal bow, the short note sounds louder (more bow speed in less time). Wrong feel.

Chapter 4 — Longer slurs and bow planning

Grade 1 slurs were 2–4 notes. Grade 2 introduces 6, 8, even a full scale (8 notes) on one bow.

The bow-budget rule

Total bow length = ~25 inches (varies by player + bow).
For 8 notes in one bow: 25 ÷ 8 ≈ 3 inches per note.

You cannot play loud + slow + long-slurred. Pick two. Grade 2 long slurs are usually mezzo-piano and steady — the bow conserves itself.

Slur with string crossing

Example: slur "open A → 1st finger B → open E" in one bow. Two string changes inside one stroke. The right wrist + elbow tilt to roll between strings without breaking the sound.

Where I got stuck: every slur with a string crossing scratched on the crossing point. Fix: slow practice with the metronome at 40 bpm, paying attention to the angle of the bow hair at the crossing. The bow should tilt gradually, not snap. Drill: 5 minutes a day for 2 weeks → solved.

Practice progression

  1. 2-note slur, same string.
  2. 2-note slur, crossing strings.
  3. 4-note slur, same string.
  4. 4-note slur, crossing strings.
  5. 8-note slur = full scale.

Don't jump steps. Each stage takes about a week.

Chapter 5 — Intervals (Grade 2 theory)

Grade 2 theory paper introduces interval size within the major and natural minor keys:

Size Example (C major) Notes spanned
2nd C–D 2
3rd C–E 3
4th C–F 4
5th C–G 5
6th C–A 6
7th C–B 7
8ve C–C (high) 8

Trick: count letter names inclusive. C to E = C, D, E = 3 letters = a 3rd. Never count semitones for size at Grade 2 — that's Grade 3 (interval quality).

Practise: pick a key, name every interval from the tonic. Then play each on the violin. Linking name + sound + hand position triples retention.

Listening assignment

Watch a recording of Itzhak Perlman or Hilary Hahn playing Bach Partita No. 2 — Allemande (don't try to play it; just listen). Notice how each note has identical bow weight despite enormous variety in speed. That's the Grade 2 goal: even tone across changes.

Chapter 7 — Intonation upgrade: cross-string checking

Grade 1 used drones on open strings. Grade 2 adds two-string checks at the same pitch:

Pitch Way 1 Way 2
A4 Open A 3rd finger on D
E5 Open E 3rd finger on A
D5 3rd finger on A (also = open string interval check)

If the two ways give the same pitch, you're in tune. If they fight, one finger is off.

Trick: end every scale with a check-note. Play G major scale up to G5, then immediately play open G and the G5 together as a double-stop. If the octave rings, your top note is true. If it beats, adjust.

Tape removal

If you used tape on the fingerboard in Grade 1, remove it by Grade 2 exam. The eyes-on-fingerboard habit costs you sight-reading speed.

Chapter 8 — Aural / Listening Skills

ABRSM Grade 2 aural adds:

  • Clap back rhythm in 6/8 (new at Grade 2).
  • Sing back a 4-bar phrase.
  • Identify if a melody is in major or minor.
  • Identify the time signature (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, or 6/8).

Major vs minor identification

Major = "bright, happy". Minor = "sad, darker". The most reliable test: the third. Major 3rd (C–E) = 4 semitones. Minor 3rd (C–E♭) = 3 semitones.

Trick: sing "Happy Birthday" (major) immediately, then "Hey Jude" verse (also major) — internalise the colour. Then sing "Greensleeves" (Dorian, sounds minor) or "House of the Rising Sun" (minor) — feel the gloom. After a week of A/B comparisons, you'll identify them in 2 seconds.

Chapter 9 — Common Grade 2 Pitfalls

Pitfall Fix
Low 1 (B♭, F♮) lands at normal 1 position Mark for first week; physical drill
Bow runs out before slur ends Bow-budget rule; mark "mp" in pencil to save hair
Dotted rhythm becomes triplet (long-short-short instead of long-short) Subdivide in semiquavers aloud
Scratching on slurred string crossing Slow practice, watch the hair angle
C major fingering forgotten Drill C major scale daily for 2 weeks
Eyes stuck on fingerboard Cover with a small towel during scale practice

Useful Shortcut

Cheat-sheet

What How
Two-octave G major G string → D string → A string → E string (up to G5)
C major weirdness Half-step between 1 and 2 on D string (not 2-3)
F major / B♭ "Low 1" on A string
A natural minor Same notes as C major, starts on A
Dotted-quaver + semiquaver Long:short = 3:1 in duration AND bow
Long slur bow budget Divide bow length by note count; save weight
Relative minor 3 semitones below the major (C maj ↔ A min)
Cross-string intonation check Same-name notes on two strings should match exactly

Daily routine (35 min)

Block Time Content
Tune + 5th check 2 min Tuner + double-stop
Bow warm-up 5 min Long open bows + 8-note slurs on each string
Scale 8 min Two-octave today's key + arpeggio + relative minor
Sight-read 4 min New 8-bar piece in the week's key
Repertoire 14 min Current piece, slow → tempo
Cool-down 2 min Air-shake bow arm, release tension
