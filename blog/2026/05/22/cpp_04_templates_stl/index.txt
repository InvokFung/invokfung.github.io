<h1 id="Chapter-5-—-Iterators"><a href="#Chapter-5-—-Iterators" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 5 — Iterators"></a>Chapter 5 — Iterators</h1>
<p>Every STL container has <code>begin()</code> and <code>end()</code>. <code>end()</code> is <strong>one past</strong> the last element.</p>
<pre><code class="language-cpp"><figure class="highlight cpp"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="cpp"><span class="line">std::vector&lt;<span class="hljs-type">int</span>&gt; v{<span class="hljs-number">10</span>, <span class="hljs-number">20</span>, <span class="hljs-number">30</span>};</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">for</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">auto</span> it = v.<span class="hljs-built_in">begin</span>(); it != v.<span class="hljs-built_in">end</span>(); ++it) {</span><br><span class="line">    std::cout &lt;&lt; *it &lt;&lt; <span class="hljs-string">&quot; &quot;</span>;       <span class="hljs-comment">// dereference like a pointer</span></span><br><span class="line">}</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Categories (most→least powerful):</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Random-access</strong>: <code>vector</code>, <code>array</code>, <code>deque</code>. Supports <code>it + n</code>, <code>it[i]</code>, <code>it1 - it2</code>.</li>
<li><strong>Bidirectional</strong>: <code>list</code>, <code>map</code>, <code>set</code>. Supports <code>++it</code>, <code>--it</code>.</li>
<li><strong>Forward</strong>: forward-only.</li>
<li><strong>Input / Output</strong>: single-pass only (e.g., reading from a stream).</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-9-—-Choosing-a-container"><a href="#Chapter-9-—-Choosing-a-container" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 9 — Choosing a container"></a>Chapter 9 — Choosing a container</h1>
<p>Decision tree:</p>
<ol>
<li>Default = <code>std::vector</code>. Right answer ~80% of the time.</li>
<li>Need keys → values? <code>std::unordered_map</code> (or <code>map</code> if you need sorted iteration).</li>
<li>Need unique-set membership? <code>std::unordered_set</code> (or <code>set</code> if sorted).</li>
<li>Inserting at both ends? <code>std::deque</code>.</li>
<li>Fixed-size? <code>std::array</code> (zero overhead, size in the type).</li>
<li>Lots of middle insertions and you have iterators? <code>std::list</code> — but profile first; it's often slower than <code>vector</code> due to cache effects.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Rule:</em> benchmark before choosing anything other than <code>vector</code> for performance reasons. The cache wins almost every fight.</p>
</blockquote>
