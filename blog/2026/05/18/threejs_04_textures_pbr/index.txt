11:T8d85,<p>Textures are the data-rich half of a 3D material — colour, roughness, normal detail, AO, emission, displacement. Done right they make a low-poly model look fully detailed; done wrong they look washed-out, banded, or blurry. This post covers loading textures, the colour-space rules every modern Three.js project must follow, mipmaps + anisotropy, the full PBR map set, environment maps and image-based lighting (IBL), and KTX2 compressed textures for shipping mobile-friendly builds.</p>

<p>Series map: 00 Foundations → 01 Geometry &amp; Materials → 02 Cameras &amp; Controls → 03 Lighting &amp; Shadows → <strong>04 Textures &amp; PBR</strong> (this post) → 05 Asset Pipeline → 06 Animation → 07 Instancing &amp; Batching → 08 Low-End Perf → 09 Shaders → 10 Post-Processing → 11 Physics → 12 R3F → 13 AI 3D.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-2-—-Colour-space-the-bug-that-ruins-your-scene"><a href="#Chapter-2-—-Colour-space-the-bug-that-ruins-your-scene" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 2 — Colour space (the bug that ruins your scene)"></a>Chapter 2 — Colour space (the bug that ruins your scene)</h1>
<p>PBR shading does its math in <strong>linear</strong> light space. Image files (JPG, PNG) are stored in <strong>sRGB</strong> (gamma-encoded for storage efficiency, perception-uniform on monitors).</p>
<p>If you load an sRGB image without telling Three.js, the shader treats the gamma-encoded values as linear → washed-out, oversaturated, wrong-coloured result.</p>

<p>GLTFLoader sets colour spaces correctly for you. Hand-loaded <code>TextureLoader</code> does not — you must set them.</p>
<p>Renderer side:</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line">renderer.<span class="hljs-property">outputColorSpace</span> = <span class="hljs-variable constant_">THREE</span>.<span class="hljs-property">SRGBColorSpace</span>;    <span class="hljs-comment">// default since r152</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>This converts the linear render back to sRGB for the monitor.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> if your scene looks washed-out (light grey instead of vibrant), the first suspect is <strong>colour space</strong>. Set <code>colorSpace = SRGBColorSpace</code> on every colour texture and the output back to sRGB. The fix is one line per file.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Chapter-4-—-Mipmaps-anisotropy"><a href="#Chapter-4-—-Mipmaps-anisotropy" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 4 — Mipmaps + anisotropy"></a>Chapter 4 — Mipmaps + anisotropy</h1>
<p>A <strong>mipmap</strong> is a chain of pre-downscaled copies of the texture (1024 → 512 → 256 → 128 → ... → 1). The GPU samples the right level for the distance, avoiding flicker and improving cache use.</p>
<p>Mipmaps generate automatically as long as <strong>texture dimensions are powers of two</strong> (<code>256</code>, <code>512</code>, <code>1024</code>, <code>2048</code>...) and the wrap mode is <code>RepeatWrapping</code>. Otherwise:</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line">tex.<span class="hljs-property">minFilter</span> = <span class="hljs-variable constant_">THREE</span>.<span class="hljs-property">LinearFilter</span>;     <span class="hljs-comment">// no mipmaps</span></span><br><span class="line">tex.<span class="hljs-property">generateMipmaps</span> = <span class="hljs-literal">false</span>;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<h2 id="Anisotropic-filtering"><a href="#Anisotropic-filtering" class="headerlink" title="Anisotropic filtering"></a>Anisotropic filtering</h2>
<p>Default mipmapping looks blurry on surfaces seen at glancing angles (floors viewed from afar). Anisotropic filtering samples more along the view direction:</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">const</span> max = renderer.<span class="hljs-property">capabilities</span>.<span class="hljs-title function_">getMaxAnisotropy</span>();   <span class="hljs-comment">// typically 16</span></span><br><span class="line">tex.<span class="hljs-property">anisotropy</span> = <span class="hljs-title class_">Math</span>.<span class="hljs-title function_">min</span>(<span class="hljs-number">8</span>, max);</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Set <code>anisotropy</code> to 4-8 for ground textures, 1 (default) for most others. Cost is small but not free.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> if your &quot;muddy&quot; texture on a distant floor turns sharp when you crank <code>tex.anisotropy = 16</code>, anisotropy was the problem — not the texture resolution.</p>
</blockquote>

<h2 id="Packed-maps-the-modern-convention"><a href="#Packed-maps-the-modern-convention" class="headerlink" title="Packed maps (the modern convention)"></a>Packed maps (the modern convention)</h2>
<p>Roughness, metalness, and AO are each single-channel. Packing them into one RGB image saves a texture slot:</p>
<ul>
<li>R = AO</li>
<li>G = Roughness</li>
<li>B = Metalness</li>
</ul>
<p>This is the glTF 2.0 convention. Both <code>roughnessMap</code> and <code>metalnessMap</code> can point at the same texture; Three.js reads the right channel.</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">const</span> orm = loader.<span class="hljs-title function_">load</span>(<span class="hljs-string">&#x27;/wood/orm.jpg&#x27;</span>);     <span class="hljs-comment">// ao + rough + metal</span></span><br><span class="line">orm.<span class="hljs-property">colorSpace</span> = <span class="hljs-variable constant_">THREE</span>.<span class="hljs-property">NoColorSpace</span>;</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line">mat.<span class="hljs-property">aoMap</span>        = orm;</span><br><span class="line">mat.<span class="hljs-property">roughnessMap</span> = orm;</span><br><span class="line">mat.<span class="hljs-property">metalnessMap</span> = orm;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<p>A normal map encodes a surface normal per pixel as RGB. The shader fakes high-frequency detail without geometry.</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">const</span> normal = loader.<span class="hljs-title function_">load</span>(<span class="hljs-string">&#x27;/normal.png&#x27;</span>);</span><br><span class="line">normal.<span class="hljs-property">colorSpace</span> = <span class="hljs-variable constant_">THREE</span>.<span class="hljs-property">NoColorSpace</span>;</span><br><span class="line"> </span><br><span class="line">mat.<span class="hljs-property">normalMap</span> = normal;</span><br><span class="line">mat.<span class="hljs-property">normalScale</span>.<span class="hljs-title function_">set</span>(<span class="hljs-number">1.0</span>, <span class="hljs-number">1.0</span>);    <span class="hljs-comment">// y can be negated for some authoring tools</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<h2 id="Two-normal-map-conventions"><a href="#Two-normal-map-conventions" class="headerlink" title="Two normal-map conventions"></a>Two normal-map conventions</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>OpenGL</strong> convention: green channel is +Y up. (Three.js default.)</li>
<li><strong>DirectX</strong> convention: green channel is +Y down.</li>
</ul>
<p>If a normal map looks &quot;inverted&quot; (bumps look like dents), flip the green channel: <code>mat.normalScale.set(1, -1)</code>.</p>
<p>Most glTF normal maps follow OpenGL. Substance exports either; check the export setting.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-9-—-Texture-atlases"><a href="#Chapter-9-—-Texture-atlases" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 9 — Texture atlases"></a>Chapter 9 — Texture atlases</h1>
<p>Combining many small textures into one big &quot;atlas&quot; saves texture-binding overhead. With UV remapping per object, you can render many meshes that share the atlas in fewer draw calls.</p>
<p>Authoring tools (Blender → UV Editor, gltf-transform <code>dedup</code>, custom packers like <code>MaxRectsBin</code>) generate atlases. At runtime, every mesh references the same atlas texture with different UV ranges.</p>
<p>Pairs naturally with <strong>InstancedMesh</strong> (Post 07) and texture <strong>arrays</strong> (one texture sampler, N layers).</p>

<h2 id="Exercise-1-—-Wood-plank"><a href="#Exercise-1-—-Wood-plank" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 1 — Wood plank"></a>Exercise 1 — Wood plank</h2>
<p>Download a wood material from <a href="https://polyhaven.com/textures" target="_blank" rel="noopener external nofollow noreferrer">polyhaven.com</a>. Apply Color + Normal + Roughness + AO maps to a <code>PlaneGeometry(4, 4)</code>. Set colour spaces correctly. Repeat 4× in each direction.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-2-—-Colour-space-comparison"><a href="#Exercise-2-—-Colour-space-comparison" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 2 — Colour-space comparison"></a>Exercise 2 — Colour-space comparison</h2>
<p>Same mesh, two versions side by side:</p>
<ol>
<li><code>colorMap.colorSpace = THREE.SRGBColorSpace</code></li>
<li><code>colorMap.colorSpace = THREE.NoColorSpace</code></li>
</ol>
<p>Note how the second one washes out under PBR lighting.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-3-—-IBL-via-HDR"><a href="#Exercise-3-—-IBL-via-HDR" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 3 — IBL via HDR"></a>Exercise 3 — IBL via HDR</h2>
<p>Load a 1K HDRI from polyhaven. Set <code>scene.environment</code>. A <code>MeshStandardMaterial</code> chrome sphere should now reflect the environment realistically.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-4-—-KTX2-round-trip"><a href="#Exercise-4-—-KTX2-round-trip" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 4 — KTX2 round-trip"></a>Exercise 4 — KTX2 round-trip</h2>
<p>Convert one of your PNG textures to KTX2 with <code>toktx</code>. Load with KTX2Loader. Compare <code>renderer.info.memory.textures</code> before/after.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-5-—-Tile-a-wall"><a href="#Exercise-5-—-Tile-a-wall" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 5 — Tile a wall"></a>Exercise 5 — Tile a wall</h2>
