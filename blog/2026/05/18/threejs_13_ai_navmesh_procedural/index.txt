<h2 id="Workflow-for-AI-generated-mesh-→-Threejs"><a href="#Workflow-for-AI-generated-mesh-→-Threejs" class="headerlink" title="Workflow for AI-generated mesh → Three.js"></a>Workflow for AI-generated mesh → Three.js</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Generate</strong> at the tool's UI. Iterate on prompt + reference images until happy.</li>
<li><strong>Download glTF</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Clean up in Blender</strong> — fix the inevitable issues:
<ul>
<li>Floating / disconnected geometry → merge by distance.</li>
<li>Hyper-dense topology → decimate.</li>
<li>Wrong scale / orientation → fix root transform.</li>
<li>Inverted normals → recalculate.</li>
<li>Texture seams or bake noise → repaint patches.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Optimise</strong> with <code>gltf-transform optimize</code> (Post 05).</li>
<li><strong>Drop into your Three.js scene</strong> with <code>GLTFLoader</code>.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> AI-generated meshes look right at &quot;hero camera&quot; angles and wrong from close-up. Always test from your actual camera. For backgrounds it doesn't matter; for player-inspected props, plan to clean up.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="Production-readiness-in-2026"><a href="#Production-readiness-in-2026" class="headerlink" title="Production readiness in 2026"></a>Production readiness in 2026</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Prototype quality</strong> is trivial — minutes from prompt to scene.</li>
<li><strong>Production quality</strong> still needs human review for: hero characters, branded assets, anything inspected closely, manufacturing geometry, anything with text on it (still flaky in 2026).</li>
<li><strong>Round-trip test always</strong> — export from AI tool, import into your engine, render at production camera, before committing.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-NavMesh-pathfinding"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-NavMesh-pathfinding" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — NavMesh + pathfinding"></a>Chapter 3 — NavMesh + pathfinding</h1>
<p>For AI-driven characters that need to walk around obstacles, <strong>NavMesh</strong> (Navigation Mesh) is the standard. A NavMesh is a polygon graph of the walkable surface; pathfinding queries return a sequence of polygons + a straight path inside them.</p>
<p>The canonical library for JS is <a href="https://github.com/isaac-mason/recast-navigation-js" target="_blank" rel="noopener external nofollow noreferrer"><strong>recast-navigation-js</strong></a> — a WASM port of the C++ Recast (NavMesh construction) + Detour (pathfinding) libraries used in Unreal, Unity, and most game engines.</p>

<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">// L-system rules (very simplified)</span></span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> s = <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;F&#x27;</span>;</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">for</span> (<span class="hljs-keyword">let</span> i = <span class="hljs-number">0</span>; i &lt; <span class="hljs-number">4</span>; i++) {</span><br><span class="line">  s = s.<span class="hljs-title function_">replaceAll</span>(<span class="hljs-string">&#x27;F&#x27;</span>, <span class="hljs-string">&#x27;FF+[+F-F-F]-[-F+F+F]&#x27;</span>);</span><br><span class="line">}</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-comment">// interpret s as turtle commands → draw cylinders/branches in 3D</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Useful for plants, branches, trees.</p>
<h2 id="Wave-Function-Collapse-WFC"><a href="#Wave-Function-Collapse-WFC" class="headerlink" title="Wave Function Collapse (WFC)"></a>Wave Function Collapse (WFC)</h2>
<p>Generate tile-based maps from a small example. Libraries: <code>wavefunctioncollapse</code> (npm). Use for dungeons, city blocks, decorative patterns.</p>
<h2 id="Voronoi-Delaunay"><a href="#Voronoi-Delaunay" class="headerlink" title="Voronoi / Delaunay"></a>Voronoi / Delaunay</h2>
<p>Cell partitioning — useful for procedural ground textures, cracked surfaces, cell-shaded foliage clumps. Library: <code>d3-delaunay</code>.</p>
<h2 id="Marching-cubes"><a href="#Marching-cubes" class="headerlink" title="Marching cubes"></a>Marching cubes</h2>
<p>Convert a 3D scalar field (e.g., from noise) into a triangle mesh — generates caves, blob shapes, organic terrain. Three.js has <code>MarchingCubes</code> in addons.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> &quot;looks too perfect / too repetitive&quot; is the procedural-generation tell. Layer multiple techniques (noise + WFC + hand-placed accents). Add small artist-authored variation on top to break uniformity.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-5-—-Gaussian-Splatting-3DGS"><a href="#Chapter-5-—-Gaussian-Splatting-3DGS" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 5 — Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)"></a>Chapter 5 — Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)</h1>
<p>Gaussian Splatting (2023) is a new way to represent and render 3D scenes — a cloud of millions of view-dependent ellipsoids (&quot;splats&quot;) rather than a triangle mesh. Captured from photos with tools like Luma AI, Polycam, PostShot. Renders extremely fast even on mobile.</p>
<p>Status in 2026:</p>
<ul>
<li>Three.js doesn't yet ship a built-in 3DGS renderer.</li>
<li>Use <strong><code>@mkkellogg/gaussian-splats-3d</code></strong> or <strong><code>luma-web</code></strong> to render <code>.splat</code> / <code>.ply</code> files in a Three.js scene.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-6-—-Pipeline-integration"><a href="#Chapter-6-—-Pipeline-integration" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 6 — Pipeline integration"></a>Chapter 6 — Pipeline integration</h1>
<p>Putting it all together — a realistic mixed pipeline for a game / interactive experience:</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span><br><span class="line">10</span><br><span class="line">11</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">1. Concept       — designer sketches, mood boards.</span><br><span class="line">2. Hero assets   — human-authored in Blender (player character, key items).</span><br><span class="line">3. Background    — AI-generated (Meshy/Tripo) + light human cleanup.</span><br><span class="line">4. Terrain       — procedural noise + hand-painted layers.</span><br><span class="line">5. Optimise      — gltf-transform, LOD, instancing.</span><br><span class="line">10. Ship         — adaptive quality on mobile.</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Three.js (or R3F) sits in the middle, integrating all of it through <code>GLTFLoader</code>, custom loaders, NavMesh queries, and the patterns from posts 00-12.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-7-—-Ethical-legal-notes"><a href="#Chapter-7-—-Ethical-legal-notes" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 7 — Ethical + legal notes"></a>Chapter 7 — Ethical + legal notes</h1>
<p>AI-generated 3D models:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Licensing of training data</strong> is contested. Most commercial tools (Meshy, Tripo, Luma) claim model output is yours to use; check current ToS.</li>
<li><strong>Open-source models</strong> (TripoSR, Hunyuan3D) have permissive licences for output but may have non-commercial training-data caveats.</li>
<li><strong>Brand / IP</strong> prompts (&quot;a Star Wars X-wing&quot;) will produce IP-infringing outputs; you carry the legal risk, not the tool.</li>
<li><strong>Disclose AI-generated content</strong> where required by platform rules (App Store, Steam, etc.).</li>
<li><strong>Workflow auditability</strong> — log prompts + tool + version per asset for future review.</li>
</ul>
<p>NavMesh + procedural generation aren't ethically loaded; standard library licensing applies (most are MIT / Apache-2.0).</p>
<p>Gaussian Splat captures of real places — same as photography: respect private property, get model releases for people, follow GDPR / local rules for identifiable people.</p>

<h2 id="Exercise-3-—-NavMesh-on-a-room"><a href="#Exercise-3-—-NavMesh-on-a-room" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 3 — NavMesh on a room"></a>Exercise 3 — NavMesh on a room</h2>
<p>Build a simple &quot;room&quot; scene: floor + several walls + a couple of obstacles. Generate a NavMesh via <code>recast-navigation-js</code>. Visualise it (the NavMesh has a <code>.getDebugTriangles()</code> method or similar).</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-4-—-Click-to-walk"><a href="#Exercise-4-—-Click-to-walk" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 4 — Click-to-walk"></a>Exercise 4 — Click-to-walk</h2>

<h2 id="Exercise-5-—-Procedural-terrain"><a href="#Exercise-5-—-Procedural-terrain" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 5 — Procedural terrain"></a>Exercise 5 — Procedural terrain</h2>

<h2 id="Exercise-6-—-Gaussian-Splat-viewer"><a href="#Exercise-6-—-Gaussian-Splat-viewer" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 6 — Gaussian Splat viewer"></a>Exercise 6 — Gaussian Splat viewer</h2>
