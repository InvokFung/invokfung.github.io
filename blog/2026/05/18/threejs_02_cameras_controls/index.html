Three.js Course — 02 · Cameras, Controls, and Coordinate Systems

Created2026-05-18|Updated2026-05-18|Programming > 3D
|Post View:

Camera defines what the user sees; controls let them navigate. Three.js ships several of each, and the right choice depends on the experience you want — a product viewer (OrbitControls), a first-person walker (PointerLockControls), a top-down map (MapControls), or a CAD-style turntable (TrackballControls). This post covers the camera types, the built-in controls, how the coordinate system works, and the most useful camera utilities (lookAt, frustum culling, raycasting from screen, helper visualisations).

Learning Outcome

  • Choose between PerspectiveCamera and OrthographicCamera.
  • Tune FOV, near, far planes correctly.
  • Use OrbitControls, MapControls, PointerLockControls, TrackballControls, ArcballControls, FirstPersonControls, FlyControls.
  • Manipulate position + lookAt in world and local space.
  • Use helpers (CameraHelper, AxesHelper, GridHelper) for debugging.
  • Pick objects with a Raycaster from a mouse click.

Chapter 1 — Coordinate system + units

Three.js uses a right-handed coordinate system with Y-up:

  • Units are arbitrary — pick one (often metres for physics-friendly scenes) and stay consistent.
  • Default camera sits at the origin looking down -Z.
  • A BoxGeometry(1, 1, 1) is centred at the local origin, sides of length 1.

Trick: mismatched scales across imported assets (one in mm, one in m, one in inches) is the #1 "why is my model invisible / dust-sized" bug. Standardise the unit before importing. Most exporters offer a scale factor.

Chapter 2 — PerspectiveCamera

Mimics a real lens: distant objects appear smaller, parallel lines converge.

Knob Effect Typical
fov wider = more in frame, more "fish-eye" feel 45-75 game; 25-35 cinematic
aspect match canvas size w/h
near nearer than this is clipped 0.1 (units of metres ≈ 10 cm)
far further than this is clipped 1000

The near/far depth problem

Depth buffer precision is non-linear — most precision is packed near the camera. A huge far / near ratio (e.g., 0.001 / 10000 = 10⁷) causes z-fighting (flickering coplanar surfaces).

Rule: keep far / near < ~10⁴. If your scene needs both centimetre detail and kilometres of distance, use a logarithmic depth buffer: new THREE.WebGLRenderer({ logarithmicDepthBuffer: true }).

Chapter 5 — Built-in controls

All controls live at three/addons/controls/* and need the camera + the canvas DOM element.

Controls Feel Typical use
OrbitControls orbit a target, dolly with scroll, pan with right-click product viewers, scene exploration
MapControls OrbitControls tuned for top-down maps — left-click pans maps, terrain, 2.5D dashboards
TrackballControls free orbit, no up-axis lock CAD, scientific visualisation
ArcballControls trackball with gizmo on screen precise manipulation
PointerLockControls first-person mouse-look (locks pointer) FPS games, walk-throughs
FirstPersonControls WASD + mouse, no pointer lock exploratory scenes
FlyControls 6-DoF flight space scenes
TransformControls gizmo to translate/rotate/scale objects editors, level builders
DragControls click-drag objects across the screen plane UI, prototypes

Performance tips for raycasting

  • intersectObjects(...) walks every triangle by default — slow with > 10k tris.
  • For large scenes, use a BVH (Bounding Volume Hierarchy): the three-mesh-bvh library accelerates raycasts up to 100× and reuses the BVH per geometry.
  • For purely UI hit-tests over flat icons, raycast against a low-poly invisible proxy mesh, not the full visual model.

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1 — OrbitControls + helpers

Scene with a cube, OrbitControls, AxesHelper, GridHelper. Orbit around, confirm axes and grid behave intuitively.

Exercise 2 — Orthographic isometric

Switch the camera to OrthographicCamera at position (10, 10, 10) lookAt(0,0,0). Render a few cubes. Note the isometric look.

Exercise 3 — Pick + recolour

Implement Chapter 7 raycasting. Click on any object → set its material.color to red.

Exercise 4 — First-person walk

PointerLockControls + WASD. Spawn a few box "obstacles" on a plane. Walk around. (No collision yet — Post 11 covers physics.)

Exercise 5 — Chase camera

A small ball moves in a circle. The camera follows behind at fixed distance + slight elevation. Use lerp for smoothing.

Exercise 6 — Minimap

Render the main scene from a perspective camera; in a 200×200 corner viewport render from a top-down orthographic camera. Sync them on each frame.

Cheat-sheet

What How
Perspective camera new THREE.PerspectiveCamera(fov, aspect, near, far)
Ortho camera new THREE.OrthographicCamera(l, r, t, b, near, far)
After changing camera params camera.updateProjectionMatrix()
Look at camera.lookAt(x, y, z) (after setting position)
OrbitControls default most use-cases
MapControls top-down pannable
PointerLockControls FPS feel
TransformControls gizmo to move objects
Axes / grid helper debugging
Raycaster raycaster.setFromCamera(ndc, camera); intersectObjects
BVH for raycast three-mesh-bvh library
Damping controls.enableDamping = true + controls.update()
Group with camera child move rig, camera follows
Multiple viewports setViewport + setScissor per render
Avoid z-fighting keep far/near < 10⁴; or logarithmicDepthBuffer: true
