<h1 id="Chapter-1-—-What-Threejs-is"><a href="#Chapter-1-—-What-Threejs-is" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 1 — What Three.js is"></a>Chapter 1 — What Three.js is</h1>
<h2 id="Terminology"><a href="#Terminology" class="headerlink" title="Terminology"></a>Terminology</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>WebGL</strong> — a browser API exposing OpenGL ES 2.0 (WebGL1) or 3.0 (WebGL2) for GPU rendering on a <code>&lt;canvas&gt;</code>.</li>
<li><strong>WebGPU</strong> — modern successor to WebGL; lower overhead, compute shaders, parallel command encoding. Supported in every major browser since Safari 26 (September 2025).</li>
<li><strong>Three.js</strong> — open-source JS library (Mr.doob et al., since 2010) on top of WebGL/WebGPU. Provides scene graph, cameras, lights, materials, geometry, animation, loaders, controls, post-processing.</li>
<li><strong>Scene graph</strong> — tree of <code>Object3D</code> nodes (meshes, groups, lights, cameras) representing the 3D world.</li>
<li><strong>Render loop</strong> — a function called every browser frame (<code>requestAnimationFrame</code>) that updates state and tells the renderer to draw.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="Why-Threejs-not-raw-WebGL"><a href="#Why-Threejs-not-raw-WebGL" class="headerlink" title="Why Three.js (not raw WebGL)"></a>Why Three.js (not raw WebGL)</h2>
<p>Raw WebGL requires hundreds of lines of boilerplate to draw a single textured cube: shaders, buffers, uniforms, projection math, all hand-rolled. Three.js collapses that into ~10 lines and adds dozens of features (PBR materials, GLTF loader, shadow maps, OrbitControls, post-processing, animation mixers) that would each take days to implement.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-The-scene-camera-renderer-trio"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-The-scene-camera-renderer-trio" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — The scene-camera-renderer trio"></a>Chapter 3 — The scene-camera-renderer trio</h1>
<p>Every Three.js app needs three things:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Scene</strong> — the container of objects (<code>THREE.Scene</code>).</li>
<li><strong>Camera</strong> — the viewpoint (<code>THREE.PerspectiveCamera</code> or <code>THREE.OrthographicCamera</code>).</li>
<li><strong>Renderer</strong> — produces pixels from scene + camera (<code>THREE.WebGLRenderer</code> or <code>THREE.WebGPURenderer</code>).</li>
</ol>
<p>Plus:</p>
<ol start="4">
<li><strong>Mesh</strong> = geometry + material — what you actually see.</li>
<li><strong>Render loop</strong> — drives <code>renderer.render(scene, camera)</code> once per frame.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="setAnimationLoop-vs-requestAnimationFrame"><a href="#setAnimationLoop-vs-requestAnimationFrame" class="headerlink" title="setAnimationLoop vs requestAnimationFrame"></a><code>setAnimationLoop</code> vs <code>requestAnimationFrame</code></h2>
<p><code>renderer.setAnimationLoop(fn)</code> is preferred over a manual <code>requestAnimationFrame</code> loop because:</p>
<ul>
<li>It works automatically inside WebXR sessions (the XR runtime drives the loop).</li>
<li>The WebGPURenderer requires it.</li>
<li>Pausing is one call: <code>renderer.setAnimationLoop(null)</code>.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-6-—-Mental-model-matrices-transforms-the-render-path"><a href="#Chapter-6-—-Mental-model-matrices-transforms-the-render-path" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 6 — Mental model: matrices, transforms, the render path"></a>Chapter 6 — Mental model: matrices, transforms, the render path</h1>
<p>Three.js hides almost all the math, but understanding the pipeline helps debug:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Local transform</strong> — each Object3D has <code>position</code>, <code>rotation</code> (Euler or Quaternion), <code>scale</code>. These compose into a 4×4 <code>matrix</code>.</li>
<li><strong>World transform</strong> — <code>matrixWorld</code> = parent's <code>matrixWorld</code> × local <code>matrix</code>. Updated automatically before rendering.</li>
<li><strong>View matrix</strong> — inverse of the camera's world matrix.</li>
<li><strong>Projection matrix</strong> — derived from camera type (perspective or orthographic).</li>
<li>The vertex shader does <code>gl_Position = projection × view × world × position</code>.</li>
</ol>
<p>Practical implications:</p>
<ul>
<li>Move a parent group → all children move with it.</li>
<li><code>object.position.set(...)</code> is in <strong>local</strong> space. For world coordinates, use <code>object.getWorldPosition(target)</code>.</li>
<li>Rotation order matters with Euler (<code>object.rotation.order = 'YXZ'</code> for camera-like).</li>
<li>Use quaternions for safe interpolation and avoiding gimbal lock.</li>
</ul>
