11:Ta3ff,<p>The single biggest performance trick in real-time 3D is <strong>don't issue one draw call per object</strong>. The CPU side of WebGL/WebGPU spends most of its budget setting up state for each draw call; reducing 9,000 draw calls to 300 takes a slideshow back to 60 fps without changing visual quality. This post covers the four standard tools — <strong><code>InstancedMesh</code></strong> (the &quot;clone trick&quot; for many copies of one shape), <strong><code>BatchedMesh</code></strong> (many different shapes, one material), <strong>geometry merging</strong> (one giant mesh), and <strong>LOD</strong> (less detail for distant objects) — and the draw-call budget rules to know when to reach for each.</p>

<p>Series map: 00 Foundations → 01 Geometry &amp; Materials → 02 Cameras &amp; Controls → 03 Lighting &amp; Shadows → 04 Textures &amp; PBR → 05 Asset Pipeline → 06 Animation → <strong>07 Instancing &amp; Batching</strong> (this post) → 08 Low-End Perf → 09 Shaders → 10 Post-Processing → 11 Physics → 12 R3F → 13 AI 3D.</p>

<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Recognise the draw-call budget and how to measure it.</li>
<li>Use <code>InstancedMesh</code> to render N copies of one geometry with per-instance transforms + colours.</li>
<li>Use <code>BatchedMesh</code> (since r156) when geometries differ but the material is shared.</li>
<li>Merge static geometries into one with <code>BufferGeometryUtils.mergeGeometries</code>.</li>
<li>Use <code>LOD</code> (Level of Detail) so distant objects render simplified versions.</li>
<li>Pick the right tool for &quot;10,000 trees&quot;, &quot;1,000 unique buildings&quot;, &quot;a city of 50,000 windows&quot;.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-1-—-The-draw-call-budget"><a href="#Chapter-1-—-The-draw-call-budget" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 1 — The draw-call budget"></a>Chapter 1 — The draw-call budget</h1>
<p>Every <code>Mesh</code> in the scene typically produces one draw call. Each draw call has CPU overhead: bind shader, set uniforms, set attribute pointers, issue the GPU command. Modern GPUs draw millions of triangles per frame easily; CPUs choke on thousands of draw calls per frame.</p>
<p>Rough targets for <strong>60 fps</strong>:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Device</th>
<th>Safe draw-call count</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Desktop / high-end laptop</td>
<td>≤ 1,000</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Mid-range mobile (mid-2020s)</td>
<td>≤ 100-200</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Low-end mobile / older hardware</td>
<td>≤ 50-100</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Triangle counts matter much less than draw-call counts. 1 mesh with 500k triangles renders fine; 5,000 meshes with 100 triangles each will not.</p>

<p>If <code>calls</code> ≫ 200 and the scene is laggy, the problem is the draw-call count, not GPU rasterisation. The fix is one of the patterns in this post.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-2-—-InstancedMesh-—-the-clone-trick"><a href="#Chapter-2-—-InstancedMesh-—-the-clone-trick" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 2 — InstancedMesh — the &quot;clone trick&quot;"></a>Chapter 2 — <code>InstancedMesh</code> — the &quot;clone trick&quot;</h1>
<p><code>InstancedMesh</code> draws N copies of <strong>one geometry</strong> with <strong>one material</strong> in <strong>one draw call</strong>, each copy with its own 4×4 matrix (transform) and optionally its own colour.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-BatchedMesh-—-many-shapes-one-material"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-BatchedMesh-—-many-shapes-one-material" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — BatchedMesh — many shapes, one material"></a>Chapter 3 — <code>BatchedMesh</code> — many shapes, one material</h1>

<h2 id="When-BatchedMesh-underperforms"><a href="#When-BatchedMesh-underperforms" class="headerlink" title="When BatchedMesh underperforms"></a>When BatchedMesh underperforms</h2>
<p>The current Three.js BatchedMesh emulates instancing via <code>multiDraw</code> rather than <code>multiDraw*Instanced</code>. For workloads of &quot;many copies of the same geometry&quot;, InstancedMesh is faster. For &quot;many different geometries, same material&quot;, BatchedMesh wins.</p>
<p>Decision:</p>
<ul>
<li>All instances identical geometry → <strong>InstancedMesh</strong>.</li>
<li>Different geometries, same material → <strong>BatchedMesh</strong>.</li>
<li>Different materials → can't batch directly; merge materials (Chapter 6) or accept separate draw calls.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-6-—-Merging-materials-with-a-texture-atlas"><a href="#Chapter-6-—-Merging-materials-with-a-texture-atlas" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 6 — Merging materials with a texture atlas"></a>Chapter 6 — Merging materials with a texture atlas</h1>
<p>To batch objects that look different (different colours, different decals), pack their textures into a single atlas + remap UVs. Then they share one material → can be merged or instanced.</p>
<p>Tools: Blender bake to atlas; <code>gltf-transform dedup</code>; manual UV editing.</p>
<p>This combination — atlas + InstancedMesh — is how you draw a forest with many different leaf colours in 1 draw call.</p>

<p>Three.js culls per-mesh: meshes whose bounding sphere is outside the camera frustum are skipped.</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line">mesh.<span class="hljs-property">frustumCulled</span> = <span class="hljs-literal">true</span>;       <span class="hljs-comment">// default</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>For <code>InstancedMesh</code> and <code>BatchedMesh</code>, frustum culling tests the <strong>entire batch's bounding sphere</strong> — if any part is in view, the whole batch renders. To get per-instance culling, you have two choices:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Manual chunking</strong> — split a 10k-instance forest into 100 chunks of 100 instances. Each chunk has its own InstancedMesh with a tighter bounding sphere.</li>
<li><strong>GPU culling</strong> with <code>BatchedMesh.setVisibleAt(i, bool)</code> — newer Three.js supports per-instance visibility for BatchedMesh.</li>
</ol>
<p>For <strong>occlusion culling</strong> (skipping objects hidden by walls), Three.js doesn't ship native support. Libraries like <a href="https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh" target="_blank" rel="noopener external nofollow noreferrer">three-mesh-bvh</a> can accelerate visibility queries; production engines often use a custom hierarchical-Z buffer approach.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-9-—-Hidden-cost-tools-that-hurt-batching"><a href="#Chapter-9-—-Hidden-cost-tools-that-hurt-batching" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 9 — Hidden cost: tools that hurt batching"></a>Chapter 9 — Hidden cost: tools that hurt batching</h1>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Thing</th>
<th>Cost</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Different materials</td>
<td>breaks batching — each material is a separate draw call</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>castShadow = true</code> per mesh</td>
<td>doubles the draw calls (one for camera, one for shadow camera)</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Transparency</td>
<td>each transparent mesh sorted + drawn separately</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Postprocess passes</td>
<td>each pass = full-screen draw</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Per-instance materials</td>
<td>impossible — instances share material</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Plan visual variation that doesn't break batching: per-instance colour, texture atlas, vertex-shader-driven variation rather than separate materials.</p>

<h2 id="Exercise-5-—-Per-instance-click-hide"><a href="#Exercise-5-—-Per-instance-click-hide" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 5 — Per-instance click + hide"></a>Exercise 5 — Per-instance click + hide</h2>

<h2 id="Exercise-6-—-Chunked-InstancedMesh"><a href="#Exercise-6-—-Chunked-InstancedMesh" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 6 — Chunked InstancedMesh"></a>Exercise 6 — Chunked InstancedMesh</h2>
<p>For a 200×200 forest, split into 10×10 chunks of 20×20 trees. Each chunk = one InstancedMesh. Confirm frustum culling now skips chunks behind the camera.</p>
