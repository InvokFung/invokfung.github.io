11:T8695,<p>Lighting decides whether a 3D scene looks alive or flat. Shadows decide whether objects look grounded or floating. Three.js ships six light types, three shadow-map algorithms, an add-on for cascaded shadow maps, and several &quot;fake&quot; shadow techniques (contact shadow, baked AO, shadow catcher). This post covers all of them, plus the bias / map-size / camera tuning that makes shadows actually look good rather than buggy.</p>

<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Use all six light types: <code>AmbientLight</code>, <code>HemisphereLight</code>, <code>DirectionalLight</code>, <code>PointLight</code>, <code>SpotLight</code>, <code>RectAreaLight</code>.</li>
<li>Enable + configure shadow maps without artefacts.</li>
<li>Choose between <code>BasicShadowMap</code>, <code>PCFShadowMap</code>, <code>PCFSoftShadowMap</code>, <code>VSMShadowMap</code>.</li>
<li>Tune <code>shadow.bias</code>, <code>normalBias</code>, <code>radius</code>, <code>mapSize</code>, and the shadow camera frustum.</li>
<li>Use Cascaded Shadow Maps (<code>three-csm</code>) for large outdoor terrains.</li>
<li>Pick when to use a &quot;contact shadow&quot; (cheap fake) or a baked AO map instead.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-4-—-The-shadow-camera"><a href="#Chapter-4-—-The-shadow-camera" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 4 — The shadow camera"></a>Chapter 4 — The shadow camera</h1>
<p>A shadow-casting light renders the scene from its own viewpoint into a depth texture (the shadow map). That viewpoint is a camera — orthographic for <code>DirectionalLight</code>, perspective for <code>SpotLight</code>.</p>
<p>The frustum must:</p>
<ul>
<li>Be <strong>as small as possible</strong> while enclosing every shadow caster.</li>
<li>Not crop the receiving floor / walls.</li>
</ul>
<p>If too large → wasted precision → blocky shadows. If too small → shadows fade out where the frustum ends.</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">const</span> cam = sun.<span class="hljs-property">shadow</span>.<span class="hljs-property">camera</span>;</span><br><span class="line">cam.<span class="hljs-property">near</span> = <span class="hljs-number">1</span>;</span><br><span class="line">cam.<span class="hljs-property">far</span>  = <span class="hljs-number">50</span>;</span><br><span class="line">cam.<span class="hljs-property">left</span> = cam.<span class="hljs-property">bottom</span> = -<span class="hljs-number">25</span>;</span><br><span class="line">cam.<span class="hljs-property">right</span> = cam.<span class="hljs-property">top</span>   =  <span class="hljs-number">25</span>;</span><br><span class="line">cam.<span class="hljs-title function_">updateProjectionMatrix</span>();    <span class="hljs-comment">// required after changes</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> add <code>scene.add(new THREE.CameraHelper(sun.shadow.camera))</code> while tuning. You see the shadow camera frustum in real time. Remove for production.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Chapter-7-—-Contact-shadows-AO-shadow-catcher-tricks"><a href="#Chapter-7-—-Contact-shadows-AO-shadow-catcher-tricks" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 7 — Contact shadows + AO + shadow-catcher tricks"></a>Chapter 7 — Contact shadows + AO + shadow-catcher tricks</h1>
<p>Real-time shadows aren't always needed. Cheaper effects often look better.</p>
<h2 id="Contact-shadow-AccumulativeShadows-soft-ground-shadow"><a href="#Contact-shadow-AccumulativeShadows-soft-ground-shadow" class="headerlink" title="Contact shadow (&quot;AccumulativeShadows&quot; / soft ground shadow)"></a>Contact shadow (&quot;AccumulativeShadows&quot; / soft ground shadow)</h2>
<p>A pre-baked or accumulated render of the object from above, splatted onto a transparent plane under it. Looks gorgeous, costs nearly nothing per frame.</p>
<ul>
<li>Drei: <code>&lt;AccumulativeShadows&gt;</code> + <code>&lt;RandomizedLight&gt;</code> — a one-liner in R3F.</li>
<li>Vanilla: render the object N times with jittered light, composite into a render target.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="Ambient-occlusion-AO"><a href="#Ambient-occlusion-AO" class="headerlink" title="Ambient occlusion (AO)"></a>Ambient occlusion (AO)</h2>
<p>The &quot;darkening in corners + crevices&quot; effect that ground objects in shadow without computing every light.</p>
<p>Two paths:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Baked AO</strong> — bake in Blender / Substance, export as <code>aoMap</code> texture, apply via <code>material.aoMap = ...; material.aoMapIntensity = 1</code>.</li>
<li><strong>Screen-space AO</strong> (SSAO) — runtime post-processing pass (Post 10).</li>
</ol>
<p>Baked is cheaper + higher quality where the geometry is static.</p>
<h2 id="Shadow-catcher"><a href="#Shadow-catcher" class="headerlink" title="Shadow catcher"></a>Shadow catcher</h2>
<p>A plane that <strong>only displays the shadow</strong> of objects above it (transparent everywhere else). Useful for product viewers on a transparent background:</p>
<ul>
<li>Drei: <code>&lt;ShadowMaterial&gt;</code> on a plane.</li>
<li>Vanilla: <code>new THREE.ShadowMaterial({ opacity: 0.5 })</code>.</li>
</ul>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">const</span> plane = <span class="hljs-keyword">new</span> <span class="hljs-variable constant_">THREE</span>.<span class="hljs-title class_">Mesh</span>(</span><br><span class="line">  <span class="hljs-keyword">new</span> <span class="hljs-variable constant_">THREE</span>.<span class="hljs-title class_">PlaneGeometry</span>(<span class="hljs-number">20</span>, <span class="hljs-number">20</span>),</span><br><span class="line">  <span class="hljs-keyword">new</span> <span class="hljs-variable constant_">THREE</span>.<span class="hljs-title class_">ShadowMaterial</span>({ <span class="hljs-attr">opacity</span>: <span class="hljs-number">0.4</span> })</span><br><span class="line">);</span><br><span class="line">plane.<span class="hljs-property">rotation</span>.<span class="hljs-property">x</span> = -<span class="hljs-title class_">Math</span>.<span class="hljs-property">PI</span> / <span class="hljs-number">2</span>;</span><br><span class="line">plane.<span class="hljs-property">receiveShadow</span> = <span class="hljs-literal">true</span>;</span><br><span class="line">scene.<span class="hljs-title function_">add</span>(plane);</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<h1 id="Chapter-8-—-Performance-budget-for-shadows"><a href="#Chapter-8-—-Performance-budget-for-shadows" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 8 — Performance budget for shadows"></a>Chapter 8 — Performance budget for shadows</h1>
<p>Shadows are often the most expensive part of a Three.js scene. Rough budget:</p>

<td>~0</td>
</tr>
<tr>

<h1 id="Chapter-9-—-frustumCulled-matrixAutoUpdate-and-shadow-update-modes"><a href="#Chapter-9-—-frustumCulled-matrixAutoUpdate-and-shadow-update-modes" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 9 — frustumCulled, matrixAutoUpdate, and shadow update modes"></a>Chapter 9 — <code>frustumCulled</code>, <code>matrixAutoUpdate</code>, and shadow update modes</h1>
<p>Two perf flags worth knowing in the lighting context:</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line">sun.<span class="hljs-property">shadow</span>.<span class="hljs-property">autoUpdate</span> = <span class="hljs-literal">false</span>;        <span class="hljs-comment">// freeze the shadow map (static scene)</span></span><br><span class="line">sun.<span class="hljs-property">shadow</span>.<span class="hljs-property">needsUpdate</span> = <span class="hljs-literal">true</span>;        <span class="hljs-comment">// ask for a one-shot re-render</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>For mostly-static scenes (architectural visualisations), turning off <code>autoUpdate</code> and only flagging <code>needsUpdate</code> when something moves is a free speed boost.</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line">mesh.<span class="hljs-property">frustumCulled</span> = <span class="hljs-literal">true</span>;            <span class="hljs-comment">// default — meshes outside camera are skipped</span></span><br><span class="line">mesh.<span class="hljs-property">matrixAutoUpdate</span> = <span class="hljs-literal">false</span>;        <span class="hljs-comment">// if mesh never moves</span></span><br><span class="line">mesh.<span class="hljs-title function_">updateMatrix</span>();                  <span class="hljs-comment">// call once after final transform</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<p>A cube on a plane. One DirectionalLight. Walk through the three-step ritual. Confirm a shadow appears.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-2-—-Compare-shadow-types"><a href="#Exercise-2-—-Compare-shadow-types" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 2 — Compare shadow types"></a>Exercise 2 — Compare shadow types</h2>
<p>Same scene, cycle through <code>BasicShadowMap</code>, <code>PCFShadowMap</code>, <code>PCFSoftShadowMap</code>, <code>VSMShadowMap</code>. Note difference in edges + framerate.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-3-—-Tune-bias"><a href="#Exercise-3-—-Tune-bias" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 3 — Tune bias"></a>Exercise 3 — Tune bias</h2>
<p>Deliberately set <code>bias = 0</code>. Observe acne. Lower to <code>-0.001</code> until it disappears. Then experiment with <code>normalBias</code> on a slanted surface.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-4-—-Cascaded-shadow-maps"><a href="#Exercise-4-—-Cascaded-shadow-maps" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 4 — Cascaded shadow maps"></a>Exercise 4 — Cascaded shadow maps</h2>
<p>Build a 200×200 terrain (<code>PlaneGeometry(200, 200)</code>). Place 100 trees (cubes for now) on it. Compare a single 4096 shadow map vs a 4-cascade CSM. Note the close-up shadow sharpness.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-5-—-Shadow-catcher"><a href="#Exercise-5-—-Shadow-catcher" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 5 — Shadow catcher"></a>Exercise 5 — Shadow catcher</h2>
<p>Load a single product model (any GLB) on a transparent <code>ShadowMaterial</code> plane. Render with <code>renderer.setClearAlpha(0)</code>. The result: model + soft shadow, no ground visible.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-6-—-Lightmap-workflow"><a href="#Exercise-6-—-Lightmap-workflow" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 6 — Lightmap workflow"></a>Exercise 6 — Lightmap workflow</h2>
<p>In Blender, build a simple room (4 walls + floor), bake AO, export the AO texture. In Three.js, load the room as GLB, attach the AO texture as <code>aoMap</code>. Compare with and without.</p>
