Three.js Course — 03 · Lighting & Shadows (PCF, PCFSoft, VSM, CSM, Contact)

Created2026-05-18|Updated2026-05-18|Programming > 3D
|Post View:

Lighting decides whether a 3D scene looks alive or flat. Shadows decide whether objects look grounded or floating. Three.js ships six light types, three shadow-map algorithms, an add-on for cascaded shadow maps, and several "fake" shadow techniques (contact shadow, baked AO, shadow catcher). This post covers all of them, plus the bias / map-size / camera tuning that makes shadows actually look good rather than buggy.

Series map: 00 Foundations → 01 Geometry & Materials → 02 Cameras & Controls → 03 Lighting & Shadows (this post) → 04 Textures & PBR → 05 Asset Pipeline → 06 Animation → 07 Instancing & Batching → 08 Low-End Perf → 09 Shaders → 10 Post-Processing → 11 Physics → 12 R3F → 13 AI 3D.

Learning Outcome

  • Use all six light types: AmbientLight, HemisphereLight, DirectionalLight, PointLight, SpotLight, RectAreaLight.
  • Enable + configure shadow maps without artefacts.
  • Choose between BasicShadowMap, PCFShadowMap, PCFSoftShadowMap, VSMShadowMap.
  • Tune shadow.bias, normalBias, radius, mapSize, and the shadow camera frustum.
  • Use Cascaded Shadow Maps (three-csm) for large outdoor terrains.
  • Pick when to use a "contact shadow" (cheap fake) or a baked AO map instead.

Chapter 1 — Light types

Light Direction Cast shadow? Cost When
AmbientLight uniform from all directions no free scene "fill" so dark sides aren't black
HemisphereLight sky colour above + ground colour below no very cheap outdoor fill (sky → blue, ground → green)
DirectionalLight parallel rays (like the sun) yes cheap-ish sun, key light
PointLight omni from a single point yes (6-face cube map) medium lamps, fires
SpotLight cone from a point yes medium flashlights, stage lights
RectAreaLight rectangle (area light) no (yet) medium windows, softboxes; needs RectAreaLightUniformsLib init

Intensity in 2026

Since Three.js r155, physically-correct lighting is the default. Intensity is in candela (point/spot) or lumens, and MeshStandardMaterial interprets it physically. A DirectionalLight intensity of 1.0 ≈ noon sun. A small PointLight for a desk lamp might be ~20-100.

VSM has a unique benefit: the blur happens in screen space, so blur radius doesn't cost more shadow-map texture. Watch for light-bleeding artefacts at corners — usually fixed by adjusting shadow.bias and vsm parameters or by mixing with a tiny shadow.radius.

Chapter 4 — The shadow camera

A shadow-casting light renders the scene from its own viewpoint into a depth texture (the shadow map). That viewpoint is a camera — orthographic for DirectionalLight, perspective for SpotLight.

The frustum must:

  • Be as small as possible while enclosing every shadow caster.
  • Not crop the receiving floor / walls.

If too large → wasted precision → blocky shadows. If too small → shadows fade out where the frustum ends.

Trick: add scene.add(new THREE.CameraHelper(sun.shadow.camera)) while tuning. You see the shadow camera frustum in real time. Remove for production.

Chapter 5 — Bias, normalBias, radius, mapSize

Four knobs decide whether shadows look right or covered in stripes / floating / pixelated.

Knob What Typical
mapSize resolution of the shadow texture 1024 / 2048 / 4096 (powers of 2)
bias offsets depth to prevent shadow acne (self-shadowing artefacts) -0.0005
normalBias offsets along surface normal — fixes "peter panning" (shadows detached from feet) 0.02
radius softening radius (PCF / VSM) 1-8

Symptoms / fixes:

Symptom Fix
Banding / stripes on lit surfaces ("shadow acne") increase bias magnitude (more negative)
Shadows detached from objects ("peter pan") increase normalBias
Edges very pixelated bigger mapSize (cost: GPU memory + fill rate)
Shadows fade to nothing at distance enlarge shadow camera frustum
Light-bleeding at corners (VSM) reduce radius, or fall back to PCFSoft

Chapter 6 — Cascaded Shadow Maps (CSM)

For huge outdoor terrains, a single shadow map is either pixelated everywhere (too big a frustum) or sharp near and gone far (too small). CSM splits the camera frustum into N slices (cascades), each with its own shadow map at decreasing resolution as distance grows.

Three.js doesn't ship CSM; use three-csm (or its r3f cousin react-three-csm):

Use CSM whenever the visible terrain is much larger than the camera's close-up area — landscape games, open worlds, architectural visualisations of city blocks.

Chapter 7 — Contact shadows + AO + shadow-catcher tricks

Real-time shadows aren't always needed. Cheaper effects often look better.

Contact shadow ("AccumulativeShadows" / soft ground shadow)

A pre-baked or accumulated render of the object from above, splatted onto a transparent plane under it. Looks gorgeous, costs nearly nothing per frame.

  • Drei: <AccumulativeShadows> + <RandomizedLight> — a one-liner in R3F.
  • Vanilla: render the object N times with jittered light, composite into a render target.

Ambient occlusion (AO)

The "darkening in corners + crevices" effect that ground objects in shadow without computing every light.

Two paths:

  1. Baked AO — bake in Blender / Substance, export as aoMap texture, apply via material.aoMap = ...; material.aoMapIntensity = 1.
  2. Screen-space AO (SSAO) — runtime post-processing pass (Post 10).

Baked is cheaper + higher quality where the geometry is static.

Shadow catcher

A plane that only displays the shadow of objects above it (transparent everywhere else). Useful for product viewers on a transparent background:

  • Drei: <ShadowMaterial> on a plane.
  • Vanilla: new THREE.ShadowMaterial({ opacity: 0.5 }).

Chapter 8 — Performance budget for shadows

Shadows are often the most expensive part of a Three.js scene. Rough budget:

Setup Frame cost (mid-range desktop)
No shadows ~0
1 DirectionalLight, 1024 map, PCF ~0.5-1 ms
1 DirectionalLight, 2048 map, PCFSoft ~1-3 ms
1 DirectionalLight, 4096 map, VSM ~3-8 ms
4-cascade CSM, 2048 each ~6-15 ms
PointLight shadow (6-face cube) ~3-5× a DirectionalLight
3+ shadow-casting lights quickly exceeds budget on mobile

Mobile cuts these costs in half on optimistic GPUs and 3× on bad ones. Plan for ≤ 1 shadow-casting light + 1024 map on phones.

Trick: for indoor scenes with many lights, bake the lightmap (Blender → Cycles → bake → export texture → assign as lightMap). Static lighting becomes a texture — zero per-frame cost. Real-time lights are only used for dynamic objects.

Chapter 9 — frustumCulled, matrixAutoUpdate, and shadow update modes

Two perf flags worth knowing in the lighting context:

For mostly-static scenes (architectural visualisations), turning off autoUpdate and only flagging needsUpdate when something moves is a free speed boost.

These also help shadow rendering: the shadow camera does its own frustum culling, so meshes with stale matrices waste work.

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1 — Basic shadow setup

A cube on a plane. One DirectionalLight. Walk through the three-step ritual. Confirm a shadow appears.

Exercise 2 — Compare shadow types

Same scene, cycle through BasicShadowMap, PCFShadowMap, PCFSoftShadowMap, VSMShadowMap. Note difference in edges + framerate.

Exercise 3 — Tune bias

Deliberately set bias = 0. Observe acne. Lower to -0.001 until it disappears. Then experiment with normalBias on a slanted surface.

Exercise 4 — Cascaded shadow maps

Build a 200×200 terrain (PlaneGeometry(200, 200)). Place 100 trees (cubes for now) on it. Compare a single 4096 shadow map vs a 4-cascade CSM. Note the close-up shadow sharpness.

Exercise 5 — Shadow catcher

Load a single product model (any GLB) on a transparent ShadowMaterial plane. Render with renderer.setClearAlpha(0). The result: model + soft shadow, no ground visible.

Exercise 6 — Lightmap workflow

In Blender, build a simple room (4 walls + floor), bake AO, export the AO texture. In Three.js, load the room as GLB, attach the AO texture as aoMap. Compare with and without.

Cheat-sheet

What How
Enable shadows renderer.shadowMap.enabled = true
Soft shadow type renderer.shadowMap.type = THREE.PCFSoftShadowMap
Cast light.castShadow = true + mesh.castShadow = true
Receive mesh.receiveShadow = true (and floor)
Shadow camera frustum tighten via shadow.camera.{left,right,top,bottom,near,far}
After frustum change shadow.camera.updateProjectionMatrix()
Shadow resolution light.shadow.mapSize.set(2048, 2048)
Bias defaults bias = -0.0005, normalBias = 0.02
Debug new THREE.CameraHelper(light.shadow.camera)
Cascaded shadows three-csm library, 2-4 cascades
Contact shadow accumulated splat or Drei <AccumulativeShadows>
AO bake to aoMap for static; SSAO in post for dynamic
Static scene speedup shadow.autoUpdate = false; flag needsUpdate on change
Physically correct in 2026 default since r155; tune intensities accordingly
