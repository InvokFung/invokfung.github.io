11:T9a29,<p>Post-processing is the cinematic polish — bloom on bright highlights, depth of field on out-of-focus regions, ambient occlusion in crevices, anti-aliasing on jagged edges. Three.js does it via the <strong>EffectComposer</strong> pipeline: render the main scene into a texture, then pass that texture through one or more screen-space passes that read in / write out. This post covers EffectComposer, the standard passes, anti-aliasing options, tone mapping, the colour management pipeline, and the <strong>postprocessing</strong> library (<code>pmndrs/postprocessing</code>) that ships better-quality effects than the built-ins.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Series map: 00 Foundations → 01 Geometry &amp; Materials → 02 Cameras &amp; Controls → 03 Lighting &amp; Shadows → 04 Textures &amp; PBR → 05 Asset Pipeline → 06 Animation → 07 Instancing &amp; Batching → 08 Low-End Perf → 09 Shaders → <strong>10 Post-Processing</strong> (this post) → 11 Physics → 12 R3F → 13 AI 3D.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Build an EffectComposer with <code>RenderPass</code> + one or more effect passes.</li>
<li>Use the standard passes: Bloom, SSAO, DOF, Outline, FXAA / SMAA / TAA.</li>
<li>Pick a tone-mapping operator (ACES, Filmic, Neutral, Cineon, Reinhard).</li>
<li>Understand the linear → tone-map → sRGB pipeline.</li>
<li>Use <code>pmndrs/postprocessing</code> for higher-quality effects with better performance.</li>
<li>Be disciplined about postprocess cost on mobile.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-1-—-Why-postprocess"><a href="#Chapter-1-—-Why-postprocess" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 1 — Why postprocess"></a>Chapter 1 — Why postprocess</h1>
<p>The raw render is &quot;physically correct&quot; but rarely cinematic. Postprocess passes simulate camera and human-vision effects:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bloom</strong> — bright pixels bleed into surrounding area; mimics camera lens glare.</li>
<li><strong>Depth of field (DOF)</strong> — pixels at the focus distance are sharp; others blurred.</li>
<li><strong>Screen-space ambient occlusion (SSAO)</strong> — darken creases the global lighting missed.</li>
<li><strong>Outline</strong> — highlight selected objects with a coloured edge.</li>
<li><strong>God rays / volumetric light</strong> — visible shafts of light through atmosphere.</li>
<li><strong>Motion blur</strong> — fast objects smear.</li>
<li><strong>Anti-aliasing</strong> — smooth jagged edges (geometry AA in WebGL is limited).</li>
<li><strong>Tone mapping</strong> — squash HDR values into the displayable 0-1 range.</li>
<li><strong>Colour grading</strong> — LUT / curve adjustments for a chosen mood.</li>
</ul>
<p>Each is a full-screen pass. Each costs ms. Use sparingly on mobile (Post 08).</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-Common-passes"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-Common-passes" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — Common passes"></a>Chapter 3 — Common passes</h1>
<h2 id="UnrealBloomPass"><a href="#UnrealBloomPass" class="headerlink" title="UnrealBloomPass"></a>UnrealBloomPass</h2>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-keyword">new</span> <span class="hljs-title class_">UnrealBloomPass</span>(resolution, strength, radius, threshold)</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<h1 id="Chapter-5-—-Tone-mapping"><a href="#Chapter-5-—-Tone-mapping" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 5 — Tone mapping"></a>Chapter 5 — Tone mapping</h1>
<p>Real-world brightness has a huge dynamic range (sun = 1.6 billion lux, candle = 1 lux). Monitors display 0-1. <strong>Tone mapping</strong> is the squashing function that maps HDR scene values into displayable range.</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line">renderer.<span class="hljs-property">toneMapping</span> = <span class="hljs-variable constant_">THREE</span>.<span class="hljs-property">ACESFilmicToneMapping</span>;</span><br><span class="line">renderer.<span class="hljs-property">toneMappingExposure</span> = <span class="hljs-number">1.0</span>;</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Options:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Operator</th>
<th>Style</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><code>NoToneMapping</code></td>
<td>linear clip; blown-out highlights — usually wrong</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>LinearToneMapping</code></td>
<td>tiny boost; simple</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>ReinhardToneMapping</code></td>
<td>classic; mid-tones favoured</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>CineonToneMapping</code></td>
<td>film-stock emulation</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>ACESFilmicToneMapping</code></td>
<td>industry standard cinematic look; <strong>default recommendation</strong></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>AgXToneMapping</code> (newer)</td>
<td>Filmic's modern successor; very natural skin / sky</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>NeutralToneMapping</code> (since r166)</td>
<td>Khronos PBR Neutral — preserves albedo colour for product viz</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>ACES is the safe modern default. For product viewers / e-commerce, <strong>Neutral</strong> preserves base colours better.</p>
<p><code>toneMappingExposure</code> is the equivalent of camera ISO. Increase to brighten the scene; decrease to darken.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Trick:</strong> when adopting <code>ACESFilmic</code>, your existing materials may look darker/less saturated. Don't fight it — the result is more cinematic and physically plausible. Adjust light intensities, not albedo values.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Chapter-7-—-Colour-management-pipeline"><a href="#Chapter-7-—-Colour-management-pipeline" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 7 — Colour management pipeline"></a>Chapter 7 — Colour management pipeline</h1>
<p>The complete modern pipeline (since r152):</p>
<pre><code class="language-text"><figure class="highlight text"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span><br><span class="line">6</span><br><span class="line">7</span><br><span class="line">8</span><br><span class="line">9</span><br><span class="line">10</span><br><span class="line">11</span><br><span class="line">12</span><br><span class="line">13</span><br><span class="line">14</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="text"><span class="line">Scene materials + textures (linear colour space, HDR-capable values)</span><br><span class="line">       │</span><br><span class="line">       ▼ renderer.render()</span><br><span class="line">HDR scene render target (linear)</span><br><span class="line">       │</span><br><span class="line">       ▼ post-process passes (all work in linear)</span><br><span class="line">HDR composite (linear)</span><br><span class="line">       │</span><br><span class="line">       ▼ tone mapping (ACES / Filmic / Neutral)</span><br><span class="line">LDR colour (0..1, perceptually mapped)</span><br><span class="line">       │</span><br><span class="line">       ▼ sRGB output conversion (gamma curve)</span><br><span class="line">Monitor pixel value</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Two things to set up:</p>
<pre><code class="language-js"><figure class="highlight js"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="js"><span class="line">renderer.<span class="hljs-property">toneMapping</span> = <span class="hljs-variable constant_">THREE</span>.<span class="hljs-property">ACESFilmicToneMapping</span>;</span><br><span class="line">renderer.<span class="hljs-property">outputColorSpace</span> = <span class="hljs-variable constant_">THREE</span>.<span class="hljs-property">SRGBColorSpace</span>;   <span class="hljs-comment">// default since r152</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>When using EffectComposer, the <code>OutputPass</code> performs both steps; otherwise the renderer does them automatically.</p>
<p>For textures (Post 04):</p>
<ul>
<li>Colour textures (albedo, emissive): <code>tex.colorSpace = THREE.SRGBColorSpace</code>.</li>
<li>Data textures (normal, roughness): <code>tex.colorSpace = THREE.NoColorSpace</code>.</li>
</ul>
<p>Get any of this wrong and the scene looks washed out, oversaturated, or weirdly dark.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-8-—-LUT-colour-grading"><a href="#Chapter-8-—-LUT-colour-grading" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 8 — LUT colour grading"></a>Chapter 8 — LUT colour grading</h1>

<h1 id="Practice-Exercises"><a href="#Practice-Exercises" class="headerlink" title="Practice Exercises"></a>Practice Exercises</h1>
<h2 id="Exercise-1-—-Bloom-on-emissives"><a href="#Exercise-1-—-Bloom-on-emissives" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 1 — Bloom on emissives"></a>Exercise 1 — Bloom on emissives</h2>
<p>A scene with one matte sphere + one emissive sphere (<code>emissive: 0xff8844</code>, <code>emissiveIntensity: 5</code>). Add UnrealBloomPass. Confirm the emissive glows; matte doesn't.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-2-—-Outline-on-hover"><a href="#Exercise-2-—-Outline-on-hover" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 2 — Outline on hover"></a>Exercise 2 — Outline on hover</h2>
<p>OrbitControls scene with 5 objects. Raycast on hover → <code>outline.selectedObjects = [hit.object]</code>. Outline appears.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-3-—-Tone-mapping-comparison"><a href="#Exercise-3-—-Tone-mapping-comparison" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 3 — Tone-mapping comparison"></a>Exercise 3 — Tone-mapping comparison</h2>
<p>Same scene 4× side by side: NoToneMapping, Reinhard, ACESFilmic, Neutral. Note the colour and contrast differences.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-4-—-pmndrspostprocessing-port"><a href="#Exercise-4-—-pmndrspostprocessing-port" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 4 — pmndrs/postprocessing port"></a>Exercise 4 — pmndrs/postprocessing port</h2>
<p>Convert your bloom + FXAA pipeline from <code>three/addons/postprocessing</code> to <code>pmndrs/postprocessing</code> in a single <code>EffectPass</code>. Measure framerate before/after.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-5-—-Colour-grading"><a href="#Exercise-5-—-Colour-grading" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 5 — Colour grading"></a>Exercise 5 — Colour grading</h2>
<p>Find a free <code>.cube</code> LUT online; load + apply via <code>LUTPass</code>. Try several LUTs to see the mood shifts.</p>
<h2 id="Exercise-6-—-Mobile-strip-down"><a href="#Exercise-6-—-Mobile-strip-down" class="headerlink" title="Exercise 6 — Mobile-strip-down"></a>Exercise 6 — Mobile-strip-down</h2>
<p>Take any of the above; create a mobile fallback path with no postprocess at all (just <code>renderer.render</code>). Switch automatically via <code>navigator.userAgent</code>.</p>
