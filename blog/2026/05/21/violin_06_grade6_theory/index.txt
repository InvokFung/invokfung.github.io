11:T59f0,<p>Grade 6 ABRSM violin is where the instrument <strong>doubles itself</strong>. Double stops — two strings sounded together — become a syllabus requirement. The right hand learns flying staccato, the left hand uses all positions through 5th regularly, and chromatic scales add 12-note fluency. The repertoire is now full Baroque sonatas, mid-Romantic showpieces, and the first complete concerto movements expected at concert level.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Series map: 00 Beginner → 01 Grade 1 → … → 05 Grade 5 → <strong>06 Grade 6</strong> (this post) → 07 Grade 7 → 08 Grade 8.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Play <strong>3rds and 6ths double stops</strong> through one octave on selected keys.</li>
<li>Play <strong>chromatic scale</strong> (one or two octaves) starting on G or A.</li>
<li>Use 1st–5th positions fluently in a single piece.</li>
<li>Play three-octave scales in G, A, B♭ major (and some minor).</li>
<li>Use <strong>staccato volante</strong> (&quot;flying staccato&quot;) in showpieces.</li>
<li>Identify ornaments + execute trills, mordents, turns idiomatically.</li>
<li>Perform a complete sonata movement + a Romantic showpiece.</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-1-—-Double-stops-the-violin-singing-in-two-voices"><a href="#Chapter-1-—-Double-stops-the-violin-singing-in-two-voices" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 1 — Double stops: the violin singing in two voices"></a>Chapter 1 — Double stops: the violin singing in two voices</h1>
<p>A double stop is two strings bowed simultaneously, each producing a different pitch. The bow must contact <strong>both strings at once</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="Bow-angle-for-double-stops"><a href="#Bow-angle-for-double-stops" class="headerlink" title="Bow angle for double stops"></a>Bow angle for double stops</h2>
<p>The bow normally tilts to one side to contact one string. For double stops, the bow lies <strong>flat on both strings</strong> at the same angle. Bow arm height adjusts to suit.</p>
<h2 id="Easy-double-stops-open-string-stopped-string"><a href="#Easy-double-stops-open-string-stopped-string" class="headerlink" title="Easy double stops: open string + stopped string"></a>Easy double stops: open string + stopped string</h2>
<p>Examples:</p>
<ul>
<li>Open D + 3rd finger A on D = A4 + D5 (a 4th)</li>
<li>Open A + 1st finger B on E = B5 + open A (depends; usually combined notes)</li>
</ul>
<p>These are the entry-level double stops; you've been hearing them in pieces since Grade 3 (as the <em>drone</em> effect).</p>
<h2 id="Stopped-double-stops-3rds"><a href="#Stopped-double-stops-3rds" class="headerlink" title="Stopped double stops: 3rds"></a>Stopped double stops: 3rds</h2>
<p>Two fingers on adjacent strings, both stopped. For a 3rd in 1st position:</p>

<p>To get a <strong>3rd</strong>, you need adjacent fingers giving notes 3 letters apart. In 3rd position on A + E:</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-attribute">A</span> string: <span class="hljs-number">1</span>=D</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-attribute">E</span> string: <span class="hljs-number">1</span>=F♯</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-attribute">sounding</span>: D5 + F♯<span class="hljs-number">5</span> = a major <span class="hljs-number">3</span>rd</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The Grade 6 syllabus typically asks for a one-octave scale <strong>in 3rds</strong> in keys like G major.</p>
<h2 id="6ths"><a href="#6ths" class="headerlink" title="6ths"></a>6ths</h2>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-attribute">A</span> string: <span class="hljs-number">1</span>=B</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-attribute">E</span> string: <span class="hljs-number">2</span>=G♯</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-attribute">sounding</span>: B4 + G♯<span class="hljs-number">5</span> = a <span class="hljs-number">6</span>th</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Two-finger spread; the two fingers are usually on different strings, one slightly behind the other.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Where I got stuck:</em> my fingers couldn't independently stop adjacent strings at different positions. Fix: very slow practice with a tuner on each note separately, then together. 5 minutes per day, 6 weeks, before any scale in 3rds was reliable.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Chapter-2-—-Chromatic-scale"><a href="#Chapter-2-—-Chromatic-scale" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 2 — Chromatic scale"></a>Chapter 2 — Chromatic scale</h1>
<p>12 semitones in one octave, played either with traditional fingering or <strong>sliding-finger chromatic</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="Traditional-fingering-G-chromatic-ascending-one-octave-on-G-D-string"><a href="#Traditional-fingering-G-chromatic-ascending-one-octave-on-G-D-string" class="headerlink" title="Traditional fingering (G chromatic ascending, one octave on G + D string)"></a>Traditional fingering (G chromatic ascending, one octave on G + D string)</h2>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line">Open G → <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=G♯ → <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=A → <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=A♯ → <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=B → <span class="hljs-attribute">3</span>=C → <span class="hljs-attribute">4</span>=C♯ → open D <span class="hljs-built_in">..</span>.</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The same finger plays two consecutive semitones by sliding (1 plays G♯ then A; 2 plays A♯ then B). The finger slides up about ½ a finger-width.</p>
<h2 id="Galamians-chromatic-fingering"><a href="#Galamians-chromatic-fingering" class="headerlink" title="Galamian's chromatic fingering"></a>Galamian's chromatic fingering</h2>
<p>Uses a different finger pattern that avoids slides. Faster but harder to learn.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Trick:</em> the slide-finger chromatic is more idiomatic and what most pieces expect. Practise it very slowly with metronome. The &quot;click&quot; of the finger sliding between two semitones should be inaudible — the bow disguises it.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-Flying-staccato-staccato-volante"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-Flying-staccato-staccato-volante" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — Flying staccato (staccato volante)"></a>Chapter 3 — Flying staccato (<em>staccato volante</em>)</h1>
<p>A series of <strong>bouncing</strong> staccato notes all on <strong>one bow direction</strong>, the bow lifting between each.</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line">   .  .  .  .  .  .</span><br><span class="line">↓  ♪  ♪  ♪  ♪  ♪  ♪    = 6 staccato <span class="hljs-keyword">notes</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">on</span> a single down-bow</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Distinct from slurred staccato (which stays on the string). Flying staccato lifts.</p>
<h2 id="How-to-learn-it"><a href="#How-to-learn-it" class="headerlink" title="How to learn it"></a>How to learn it</h2>
<ol>
<li>Solid up-bow staccato first (easier direction). 4 notes per bow at slow tempo.</li>
<li>Each note: bow stops <em>and lifts a millimetre</em>, then drops for the next note.</li>
<li>Build to 8 notes per bow.</li>
<li>Reverse to down-bow staccato.</li>
</ol>
<p>Used famously in: Sarasate <em>Zigeunerweisen</em>, Wieniawski concertos, encore showpieces.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Trick:</em> the wrist + fingers produce the &quot;stop+lift&quot; motion; the arm stays steady. If your arm is jerking, you're forcing it. Relax, slow down.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-4-—-Higher-positions-6th-7th-half-position"><a href="#Chapter-4-—-Higher-positions-6th-7th-half-position" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 4 — Higher positions: 6th, 7th, half position"></a>Chapter 4 — Higher positions: 6th, 7th, half position</h1>
<h2 id="Half-position"><a href="#Half-position" class="headerlink" title="Half position"></a>Half position</h2>
<p>The hand sits <strong>below</strong> 1st position — 1st finger plays where the open string normally sounds (or a semitone above). Used for low chromatic passages.</p>
<h2 id="6th-and-7th-positions"><a href="#6th-and-7th-positions" class="headerlink" title="6th and 7th positions"></a>6th and 7th positions</h2>
<p>6th pos on A: 1=G♯5.<br>
7th pos on A: 1=A5 (an octave above open A).</p>
<p>In 7th position, the hand sits over the <strong>body</strong> of the violin. Thumb adjusts to a different position; left hand has limited mobility. Used mostly for very high passages in concertos and showpieces.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Where I got stuck:</em> my left wrist collapsed in 7th position — the angle felt wrong. Fix: explicitly lift the left elbow forward (more underneath the violin) when shifting above 5th. The hand pivots more from underneath.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-5-—-Ornaments-executed-idiomatically"><a href="#Chapter-5-—-Ornaments-executed-idiomatically" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 5 — Ornaments executed idiomatically"></a>Chapter 5 — Ornaments executed idiomatically</h1>
<p>Grade 6 demands <strong>playing</strong>, not just identifying, ornaments.</p>
<h2 id="Trill-tr-or-⟵⟶"><a href="#Trill-tr-or-⟵⟶" class="headerlink" title="Trill (tr or ⟵⟶)"></a>Trill (tr or ⟵⟶)</h2>
<p>Rapid alternation between the written note and the note <strong>a step above</strong>. In Baroque: starts on the upper note. In Classical/Romantic: starts on the written note.</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-selector-tag">Trill</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">on</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">A4</span>: <span class="hljs-selector-tag">A</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">B</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">A</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">B</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">A</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">B</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">A</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">B</span> ... (very fast)</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The 2nd finger trills (lift-drop) while bow sustains. Trill speed: 6–10 oscillations per second. Practise slowly first, build speed.</p>
<h2 id="Mordent-𝆚"><a href="#Mordent-𝆚" class="headerlink" title="Mordent (𝆚)"></a>Mordent (𝆚)</h2>
<p>Quick three-note ornament: written note → step below (or above) → written note.</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-selector-tag">Lower</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">mordent</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">on</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">A</span>: <span class="hljs-selector-tag">A</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">G</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">A</span>   (very fast, takes maybe <span class="hljs-number">1</span>/<span class="hljs-number">8</span> of a beat)</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<h2 id="Turn-∾"><a href="#Turn-∾" class="headerlink" title="Turn (∾)"></a>Turn (∾)</h2>
<p>Four-note ornament: above-note → written → below-note → written.</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-selector-tag">Turn</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">on</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">A</span>: <span class="hljs-selector-tag">B</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">A</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">G</span> <span class="hljs-selector-tag">A</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<h2 id="Appoggiatura-small-note-before-main-note"><a href="#Appoggiatura-small-note-before-main-note" class="headerlink" title="Appoggiatura (small note before main note)"></a>Appoggiatura (small note before main note)</h2>
<p>Takes <strong>half the value</strong> of the following note in Classical music.</p>
<h2 id="Acciaccatura-small-note-with-slash-through-stem"><a href="#Acciaccatura-small-note-with-slash-through-stem" class="headerlink" title="Acciaccatura (small note with slash through stem)"></a>Acciaccatura (small note with slash through stem)</h2>
<p>A &quot;crush&quot; note — played as fast as possible, then immediately to the main note.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-7-—-Aural-Listening-Skills"><a href="#Chapter-7-—-Aural-Listening-Skills" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 7 — Aural / Listening Skills"></a>Chapter 7 — Aural / Listening Skills</h1>
<p>Grade 6 aural expands:</p>
<ul>
<li>Sing/play back 4-bar phrases with modulations.</li>
<li>Identify modulations to dominant, subdominant, relative minor.</li>
<li>Identify chord progressions including II-V-I, secondary dominants.</li>
<li>Identify formal sections (binary, ternary, rondo).</li>
<li>Sight-sing 8-bar phrase with chromatic notes.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="Modulation-identification"><a href="#Modulation-identification" class="headerlink" title="Modulation identification"></a>Modulation identification</h2>
<p>Listen for the <strong>new key's tonic</strong> as the resolution point. The dominant of the new key (V/V if modulating to dominant) often precedes it.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Trick:</em> practise modulation identification using piano hymnals or simple chorales. Sing the new tonic when it arrives. Build the habit of locating &quot;the new home&quot;.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Chapter-8-—-Common-Grade-6-Pitfalls"><a href="#Chapter-8-—-Common-Grade-6-Pitfalls" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 8 — Common Grade 6 Pitfalls"></a>Chapter 8 — Common Grade 6 Pitfalls</h1>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Pitfall</th>
<th>Fix</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Double stop sounds scratchy</td>
<td>Bow weight balanced between strings; even contact</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Double stop intonation wrong</td>
<td>Tune each note separately first; combine slowly</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Chromatic scale uneven rhythm</td>
<td>Metronome with subdivisions; slow practice</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Flying staccato becomes legato</td>
<td>Visible lift between notes — film yourself</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Trill speed inconsistent</td>
<td>Slow trill drill: 60 bpm @ 4 notes per beat → 8 → 12</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>7th position cramped</td>
<td>Lift left elbow more; pivot wrist position</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Ornaments start wrong (Baroque vs Classical)</td>
<td>Mark the start note above the trill in pencil</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Sonata movement memory failure mid-performance</td>
<td>Memorise in chunks (4 bars per session)</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
