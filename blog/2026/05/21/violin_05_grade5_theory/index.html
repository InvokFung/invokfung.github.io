Violin Notes — 05 · Grade 5 Vibrato, Shifting Across Positions, Three-Octave Scales

Created2026-05-21|Updated2026-05-21|Music
|Post View:

Grade 5 ABRSM violin is the milestone exam. Pass it, and you've earned the right to attempt Grades 6–8 (which require Grade 5 theory as a prerequisite — read your local rules). Technically, Grade 5 demands vibrato, shifts to 4th and 5th position, the first three-octave scales for some keys, and a far wider repertoire including full sonata movements.

Series map: 00 Beginner → 01 Grade 1 → … → 04 Grade 4 → 05 Grade 5 (this post) → 06 Grade 6 → 07 Grade 7 → 08 Grade 8.

Learning Outcome

  • Apply continuous vibrato to sustained notes.
  • Shift fluently between 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, and 5th positions.
  • Play three-octave G and A major scales (some syllabi).
  • Play two-octave scales in all major keys + most minor keys (harmonic + melodic).
  • Use sul G (play on G string only) and sul ponticello / sul tasto colour techniques.
  • Identify all triads (major, minor, diminished, augmented) at sight.
  • Play a complete Baroque sonata movement + one concerto movement.

Chapter 1 — Vibrato: the expression

Vibrato is the small, rapid pitch oscillation that adds warmth and life to sustained notes. Without it, classical violin tone sounds naked.

Three vibrato types

Type Source Speed When
Wrist vibrato Wrist rocks back and forth Medium Most repertoire
Arm vibrato Whole forearm rocks Slow + wide Romantic, big tone
Finger vibrato Finger joint flexes Fast + narrow Rare; modern works

Wrist is the standard first vibrato taught at Grade 5.

Learning wrist vibrato — step by step

  1. Air vibrato — without violin. Hold left arm up as if holding violin. Wave hand at the wrist, palm-up to palm-down motion, for 30 sec. This is the motion.
  2. Vibrato on knee. Sit. Place finger on knee. Rock wrist gently, finger stays in contact, pad rolls. Build motor pattern.
  3. Vibrato on violin neck (no string). Finger on the neck, not string. Rock wrist.
  4. Vibrato on string with finger anchored. 3rd finger on A (= D5). Bow long open D. Rock wrist; finger should roll (not slide). Pitch should wobble below the pitch and back to it.
  5. Slow metronome vibrato. 4 wobbles per beat at 60 bpm = 4 Hz. Build to 6 Hz over 6 months.
  6. Apply to scales. Vibrate every sustained note in a slow scale.

Where I got stuck: my wrist locked. Vibrato felt impossible for 3 months. Fix: spend 5 minutes per day on the "knee drill" — no violin, no string, just the wrist motion. The brain learns motion separately from instrument. Once the wrist could rock freely, transferring to the violin took one week.

Pitch direction

Vibrato should wobble below the in-tune pitch back up to it. Never above. The brain hears the upper limit of the oscillation as "the note".

Where to start using it

Don't vibrate every note. Start with:

  • Long notes (half-notes, whole-notes).
  • Phrase peak notes.
  • Final note of a phrase.

By Grade 6, you'll vibrate almost every note that's longer than a quaver. At Grade 5, just the obvious ones.

Chapter 2 — Shifting to 4th and 5th positions

4th position

On the A string: 1=E5, 2=F♯5, 3=G♯5, 4=A5.

Note: 4 in 4th position = A5 = open E next door. Useful for staying on one string.

5th position

1=F♯5, 2=G♯5, 3=A5, 4=B5.

5th position is where the violin's neck meets the body. The thumb position changes — you'll start using the "shoulder" of the violin as a reference.

The shift map (3-octave G major)

To play G major across 3 octaves, you traverse:

  • G string: 1st position G-A-B-C
  • D string: 1st pos D-E-F♯-G
  • A string: 1st pos A-B-C♯-D
  • E string: 1st pos E-F♯-G
  • Shift to 3rd pos on E: A-B-C
  • Shift to 5th pos on E: D-E
  • (or shift higher: 7th position D-E-F♯-G — depends on edition)

Trick: learn the shift route as a fixed sequence. Mark "III" or "V" above the music. Practise just the route (no rhythm) until the shifts are automatic; then add timing.

Silent shift drill (essential at Grade 5+)

  1. Bow open A.
  2. Slide 1st finger up to D5 (3rd pos).
  3. Continue sliding to F♯5 (5th pos).
  4. Back down to D5, then to 1st position B.
  5. Repeat 10×, very slowly. No glissando audible.

Daily. The shifts smoothen over weeks.

Recommended fingering source

  • Carl Flesch, Scale System — the bible of violin scales. Used by every conservatory student.
  • Galamian Contemporary Violin Technique — slightly different fingerings.
  • Whichever your teacher prefers.

Chapter 4 — Triads: all four qualities

Grade 5 theory requires identification of:

Triad Intervals (from root) Sound
Major Maj 3rd + Min 3rd Bright
Minor Min 3rd + Maj 3rd Sad
Diminished Min 3rd + Min 3rd Tense, unstable
Augmented Maj 3rd + Maj 3rd Suspenseful, dreamy

Building each from C

  • C major: C–E–G (M3 + m3)
  • C minor: C–E♭–G (m3 + M3)
  • C diminished: C–E♭–G♭ (m3 + m3)
  • C augmented: C–E–G♯ (M3 + M3)

Inversions

A triad has 3 notes; any of them can be in the bass:

Position Bass note Example (C major)
Root Root C–E–G
First inversion 3rd E–G–C
Second inversion 5th G–C–E

Notation: figured bass uses ⁶ (1st inv) and ⁶⁄₄ (2nd inv). At Grade 5 you must identify these from a score.

Chapter 5 — Tone colours

Grade 5 introduces colour markings in scores. Common ones:

Marking Italian Effect
sul G "on the G string" Forces phrase onto G string for rich, dark tone
sul D / A / E "on the D/A/E string" Same idea, different colour
sul ponticello "near the bridge" Glassy, eerie, metallic
sul tasto "over the fingerboard" Soft, flute-like, dreamy
pizzicato "plucked" Plucked string
col legno "with the wood" Tap with the bow stick

Practise: D major scale sul ponticello, then sul tasto. The difference is dramatic. Composers exploit this.

Chapter 7 — Aural / Listening Skills

Grade 5 aural is a separate exam paper in some syllabi or a section of the practical. It includes:

  • Sing or play back a 4–8 bar phrase after one hearing.
  • Identify modulations to dominant or relative minor.
  • Identify cadences in context.
  • Identify chord progressions (basic I-IV-V-I).
  • Sight-sing a longer phrase with leaps.

Trick: daily aural drill. 5 minutes, every day. Apps: Tonal Energy, Functional Ear Trainer, Teoria. Random intervals → cadences → progressions → modulations. Build the ear like a muscle.

Chapter 8 — The Grade 5 Theory prerequisite

In the ABRSM system, you cannot enter the Grade 6, 7, or 8 practical exam without first passing Grade 5 Theory. This is the bottleneck.

Grade 5 theory paper covers

  • All intervals (size + quality, simple + compound).
  • All triads (4 qualities + inversions).
  • All cadences.
  • Time signatures including 5/4, 7/8 (irregular).
  • Transposition (writing music in a new key).
  • Score reading (alto + tenor clefs introduction; full score interpretation).
  • Composition: 8-bar melody using given opening.
  • Ornament identification (trill, mordent, turn, appoggiatura, acciaccatura).

Preparation strategy

  1. Buy ABRSM Music Theory in Practice — Grade 5 workbook.
  2. Work through 1 chapter per week.
  3. Take 2 past papers per month for the last 3 months.
  4. Aim for 90% on practice papers before booking the exam.

Average study time: 6 months alongside violin practice.

Chapter 9 — Common Grade 5 Pitfalls

Pitfall Fix
Vibrato sounds forced Slower metronome, focus on relaxation
Shift to 5th pos misses by a semitone Slow silent shifts; visualise the position landmarks
3-octave scale fingers tangle Stick to ONE fingering; don't switch mid-week
Triad confusion (major vs minor) Sing them; bottom 3rd = the giveaway
Theory paper rushed Take past papers under timed conditions early
Vibrato applied too aggressively to fast notes Apply only to long notes at first; speed will follow
sul G phrase scratches at low end Bow weight + slow speed; G string demands patience

Useful Shortcut

Cheat-sheet

What How
Vibrato motion Wrist rocks; finger rolls below pitch back to it
Vibrato start Knee drill → no-string → string anchored → bowed
4th position 1st finger E (A string)
5th position 1st finger F♯ (A string)
3-octave G major shifts Around E string upper octave
Triad qualities Maj=M3+m3, Min=m3+M3, Dim=m3+m3, Aug=M3+M3
sul G Stay on G string for tone
Grade 5 theory needed Required for Grade 6+ practical entry

Daily routine (75 min)

Block Time Content
Tune 2 min Tuner + 5ths
Bow warm-up 8 min Long bows + spiccato + colour drills
Vibrato drill 5 min Slow on long notes
Scales 15 min 1 three-octave + 2 two-octave (major + minor)
Shifting drill 5 min Silent shifts up to 5th position
Étude 10 min Wohlfahrt / Mazas
Sight-read 5 min New piece
Repertoire 20 min Concerto + sonata movement
Cool-down 5 min Slow vibrato on open string-derived pitch
