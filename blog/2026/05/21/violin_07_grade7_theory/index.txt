11:T55e7,<p>Grade 7 ABRSM violin is <strong>conservatory-entry territory</strong>. Every position from half to 8th is required, three-octave scales in all major and minor keys, <strong>harmonics</strong> (natural and artificial), more double-stop intervals (4ths, octaves), and the bow technique vocabulary expands to <em>martelé</em>, <em>ricochet</em>, <em>sautillé</em>. Repertoire is full Romantic concertos and Bach solo suite movements.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Series map: 00 Beginner → 01 Grade 1 → … → 06 Grade 6 → <strong>07 Grade 7</strong> (this post) → 08 Grade 8.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Play <strong>all major and minor scales</strong> (three-octave + harmonic &amp; melodic minor).</li>
<li>Use <strong>all positions</strong> half through 8th in repertoire.</li>
<li>Play <strong>natural harmonics</strong> and introduce <strong>artificial harmonics</strong>.</li>
<li>Use <em>martelé</em> (&quot;hammered&quot;), <em>ricochet</em> (&quot;thrown bow&quot;), <em>sautillé</em> (fast spiccato).</li>
<li>Play <strong>octaves</strong> as double stops in selected keys.</li>
<li>Identify modulations to remote keys; understand modal music.</li>
<li>Perform a complete Romantic concerto movement.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-1-—-Harmonics-the-bell-like-tones"><a href="#Chapter-1-—-Harmonics-the-bell-like-tones" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 1 — Harmonics: the bell-like tones"></a>Chapter 1 — Harmonics: the bell-like tones</h1>
<p>A harmonic is a high, pure, bell-like tone produced by <strong>lightly touching</strong> the string at specific nodes (rather than pressing down).</p>
<h2 id="Natural-harmonics"><a href="#Natural-harmonics" class="headerlink" title="Natural harmonics"></a>Natural harmonics</h2>
<p>Found at division points of the open string:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>

<th>Resulting harmonic</th>
<th>Pitch</th>
</tr>
</thead>

<td>1st overtone</td>
<td>Open string + octave</td>
</tr>

<td>2nd overtone</td>
<td>Octave + 5th</td>
</tr>

<td>3rd overtone</td>
<td>Two octaves above open</td>

<p>The 1/2 node on the A string is found by <strong>lightly touching</strong> with your 4th finger at the position where the 4th finger would normally play A5 (one octave above open A). The sound: a soft, ringing high A — an octave higher than expected.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Trick:</em> harmonics need <strong>almost no finger pressure</strong> — just a feather touch. If you press, you get a muted scratchy note. If you brush, you get the pure overtone. Practise the touch on open A: bow long stroke, feather-touch where the 4th finger sits in 1st position. The ringing high A appears almost magically.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="Artificial-harmonics"><a href="#Artificial-harmonics" class="headerlink" title="Artificial harmonics"></a>Artificial harmonics</h2>
<p>Press one finger firmly (the &quot;stopping finger&quot;) and touch with another finger 1/4 above (the &quot;harmonic finger&quot;). The pitch produced is <strong>two octaves above the stopped pitch</strong>.</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-number">1</span>st finger: presses A <span class="hljs-keyword">on</span> G <span class="hljs-built_in">string</span> (= A4)</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-number">4</span>th finger: lightly touches D <span class="hljs-keyword">on</span> G <span class="hljs-built_in">string</span> (= <span class="hljs-number">1</span>/<span class="hljs-number">4</span> <span class="hljs-keyword">above</span>)</span><br><span class="line">sounding: A6 (two octaves <span class="hljs-keyword">above</span> A4)</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Notation: diamond-shape note head for the touched note, plus regular note for the stopped note.</p>
<p>Used famously in: Tchaikovsky Violin Concerto, Paganini Caprices, Ravel <em>Tzigane</em>.</p>

<h1 id="Chapter-2-—-All-positions-fluency-target"><a href="#Chapter-2-—-All-positions-fluency-target" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 2 — All positions: fluency target"></a>Chapter 2 — All positions: fluency target</h1>
<p>By Grade 7, you've learned half, 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th, 6th, 7th positions. Now: 8th position and <strong>fluency across all of them</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="8th-position"><a href="#8th-position" class="headerlink" title="8th position"></a>8th position</h2>
<p>On the A string: 1=B5, 2=C♯6, 3=D6, 4=E6 (right at the top of the violin's standard range for most pieces).</p>
<p>The left hand pivots: thumb shifts to a different angle, hand approaches the bridge.</p>
<h2 id="The-fluency-goal"><a href="#The-fluency-goal" class="headerlink" title="The fluency goal"></a>The fluency goal</h2>
<p>You should be able to:</p>
<ol>
<li>Shift to any position <strong>without looking</strong>.</li>
<li>Find the correct first finger pitch within a quarter-tone, on the first try.</li>
<li>Choose appropriate position for <em>expression</em> (tone colour) — not just to reach a note.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Trick:</em> practise a <strong>single piece</strong> entirely in 1st position, then again entirely in 3rd, then entirely in 5th (skipping notes you can't reach). The same melody in different positions sounds <strong>different</strong> — different string vibrations, different timbre. Pick the position that <em>suits the music</em>.</p>
</blockquote>

<td>Hand over the body curve</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>8th+</td>
<td>Thumb leaves the neck, sits on the shoulder of the violin</td>
</tr>

<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-Advanced-bowings"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-Advanced-bowings" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — Advanced bowings"></a>Chapter 3 — Advanced bowings</h1>
<h2 id="Martelé-hammered"><a href="#Martelé-hammered" class="headerlink" title="Martelé (&quot;hammered&quot;)"></a><em>Martelé</em> (&quot;hammered&quot;)</h2>
<p>A short, <strong>percussive</strong> stroke with a strong attack and a dead stop. Bow pressure increased <em>before</em> the stroke begins, released through the stroke, stops abruptly.</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-selector-attr">[silent pressure build]</span> → quick <span class="hljs-attribute">stroke</span> → <span class="hljs-selector-tag">STOP</span> → <span class="hljs-selector-attr">[silent pressure build]</span> → next <span class="hljs-attribute">stroke</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Used for: marching feel, accented passages, opening of dramatic phrases.</p>
<h2 id="Ricochet-rebound-thrown"><a href="#Ricochet-rebound-thrown" class="headerlink" title="Ricochet (&quot;rebound, thrown&quot;)"></a><em>Ricochet</em> (&quot;rebound, thrown&quot;)</h2>
<p>Multiple notes from a <strong>single thrown bow</strong>. The bow is dropped from above, bounces 2–4 times naturally, lifted before it stops.</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line">↓ drop → <span class="hljs-keyword">bounce, </span><span class="hljs-keyword">bounce, </span><span class="hljs-keyword">bounce </span>→ lift</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Used in: Mendelssohn Concerto last movement, Paganini Caprices, Sarasate showpieces.</p>
<h2 id="Sautillé-springing"><a href="#Sautillé-springing" class="headerlink" title="Sautillé (&quot;springing&quot;)"></a><em>Sautillé</em> (&quot;springing&quot;)</h2>
<p>Very fast spiccato — so fast that the bow no longer fully lifts off; the wood &quot;springs&quot; rapidly. Played in the middle of the bow with a loose grip.</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line">∾∾∾∾∾∾ <span class="hljs-keyword">at</span> metronome <span class="hljs-number">144</span>+ per quaver</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Used in: Vivaldi <em>Four Seasons</em> (Summer storm), Beethoven Concerto 1st mvt, Mendelssohn Concerto 1st mvt.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Where I got stuck:</em> I couldn't transition from spiccato to sautillé. The hand wanted to &quot;control&quot; each bounce. Fix: extreme tempo experiment. Start a tremolo (very fast back-and-forth on one note). Gradually add finger changes. The wrist learns the bouncing speed first, then the fingers add pitches.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-4-—-Octave-double-stops"><a href="#Chapter-4-—-Octave-double-stops" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 4 — Octave double stops"></a>Chapter 4 — Octave double stops</h1>
<p>Two notes an octave apart, played simultaneously. Fingering: 1st finger on lower string + 4th finger on upper string, 3 strings apart (e.g., 1 on G + 4 on D for an octave).</p>
<p>Octaves require:</p>
<ul>
<li>1st and 4th fingers to be <strong>exactly in tune</strong> with each other.</li>
<li>3rd and 4th fingers stretched apart maximally.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="Practice"><a href="#Practice" class="headerlink" title="Practice"></a>Practice</h2>
<p>Slow tuner work. Tune 1st finger first; freeze it. Add 4th finger; adjust until the octave rings (no beats). Then bow the double stop.</p>
<p>Practise on every string pair: G+D, D+A, A+E.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Trick:</em> octaves are the <strong>second-hardest interval to tune</strong> (after major 7ths). Beats are very audible; an octave that's even slightly off sounds painful. Practise daily for 5 minutes. Octave fluency takes 6 months minimum.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Chapter-7-—-Memorisation-strategy"><a href="#Chapter-7-—-Memorisation-strategy" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 7 — Memorisation strategy"></a>Chapter 7 — Memorisation strategy</h1>
<p>By Grade 7, you should perform from memory. Strategy:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Analyse the structure first.</strong> Break the piece into sections (A-B-A, exposition-development-recap, theme + variations, etc.). Label each section.</li>
<li><strong>Memorise section by section</strong> — not start-to-finish.</li>
<li><strong>Memorise the <em>hardest</em> section first.</strong> Mental energy is highest at the start of practice; spend it on the toughest bars.</li>
<li><strong>Practise from any starting point.</strong> Open the score randomly; you should be able to start there from memory. This builds resilience for performance anxiety.</li>
<li><strong>Mental practice.</strong> Without the violin, mentally walk through the piece — every shift, every bow direction, every fingering. 10 minutes a day. Increases memory reliability dramatically.</li>
<li><strong>Slow performance practice.</strong> Once memorised, play the whole piece at 60% tempo from memory. Catches weak spots.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Where I got stuck:</em> I memorised by repetition only — fingers learned the piece, but my brain didn't. Under stress (an audition), my fingers blanked. Fix: 10 minutes of mental practice daily. It feels useless at first, but after a month, memory failure rate dropped 80%.</p>
</blockquote>

<h1 id="Chapter-8-—-Aural-Listening-Skills"><a href="#Chapter-8-—-Aural-Listening-Skills" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 8 — Aural / Listening Skills"></a>Chapter 8 — Aural / Listening Skills</h1>
<p>Grade 7 aural:</p>
<ul>
<li>Sing/play 8-bar phrases with modulations after 2 hearings.</li>
<li>Identify modes (Dorian, Phrygian, etc.).</li>
<li>Identify chord progressions with non-diatonic chords.</li>
<li>Sight-sing 8-bar with modulation.</li>
<li>Identify period (Baroque / Classical / Romantic / 20th-c) by style.</li>
</ul>
