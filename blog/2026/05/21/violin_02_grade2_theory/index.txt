11:T636e,<p>Grade 2 ABRSM violin doubles your reach: two-octave scales, the first minor key, dotted rhythms, and longer slurs that demand real bow planning. The hand shape from Grade 1 stays, but it now travels — string crossings within a scale become the central skill.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Series map: 00 Beginner → 01 Grade 1 → <strong>02 Grade 2</strong> (this post) → 03 Grade 3 → … → 08 Grade 8.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Learning-Outcome"><a href="#Learning-Outcome" class="headerlink" title="Learning Outcome"></a>Learning Outcome</h1>
<ul>
<li>Play <strong>two-octave</strong> scales of G and D major, one-octave C and F major.</li>
<li>Play A natural minor (one octave) and recognise the relative-minor concept.</li>
<li>Use <strong>dotted-quaver + semiquaver</strong> rhythms accurately.</li>
<li>Slur 4–8 notes per bow with even tone.</li>
<li>Read fluently up to top D / E above the staff (E string, 4th finger).</li>
<li>Play 4–6 Grade 2 pieces with hands together, musically shaped.</li>
</ul>

<h1 id="Chapter-1-—-Two-octave-scales-arrive"><a href="#Chapter-1-—-Two-octave-scales-arrive" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 1 — Two-octave scales arrive"></a>Chapter 1 — Two-octave scales arrive</h1>
<p>A two-octave scale spans two strings (Grade 1 used two strings for one octave); now you traverse three strings.</p>
<h2 id="G-major-two-octaves"><a href="#G-major-two-octaves" class="headerlink" title="G major (two octaves)"></a>G major (two octaves)</h2>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span><br><span class="line">5</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line">G string:  G   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=A   <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=B   <span class="hljs-attribute">3</span>=C</span><br><span class="line">D string:  D   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=E   <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=F♯  <span class="hljs-attribute">3</span>=G</span><br><span class="line">A string:  A   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=B   <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=C♯  <span class="hljs-attribute">3</span>=D</span><br><span class="line">E string:  E   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=F♯  <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=G   (<span class="hljs-literal">no</span> 3 needed; top<span class="hljs-built_in"> note </span>is G5 <span class="hljs-keyword">in</span> many syllabi, <span class="hljs-keyword">or</span> continue <span class="hljs-keyword">to</span> A5 with 3rd finger)</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line">D string:  D   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=E   <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=F♯  <span class="hljs-attribute">3</span>=G</span><br><span class="line">A string:  A   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=B   <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=C♯  <span class="hljs-attribute">3</span>=D</span><br><span class="line">E string:  E   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=F♯  <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=G♯  <span class="hljs-attribute">3</span>=A</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>

<h2 id="C-major-one-octave-—-first-piece-with-no-sharps"><a href="#C-major-one-octave-—-first-piece-with-no-sharps" class="headerlink" title="C major (one octave) — first piece with no sharps"></a>C major (one octave) — first piece with no sharps</h2>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line">G string:  <span class="hljs-attribute">3</span>=C</span><br><span class="line">D string:  D   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=E   <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=F   <span class="hljs-attribute">3</span>=G  ← half-step between 1 <span class="hljs-keyword">and</span> 2 here!</span><br><span class="line">A string:  A   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=B   <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=C</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Trick:</em> C major is the <strong>shock key</strong> for violin beginners. Every other scale you've played has the half-step between 2 and 3. C major has it between <strong>1 and 2</strong> on the D string. Mark the music with an arrow over the F natural for the first 2 weeks.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="F-major-one-octave"><a href="#F-major-one-octave" class="headerlink" title="F major (one octave)"></a>F major (one octave)</h2>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span><br><span class="line">4</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line">D string:  <span class="hljs-attribute">3</span>=F   (<span class="hljs-keyword">or</span> open F substitute on some editions)</span><br><span class="line">A string:  A   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=B♭   <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=C   <span class="hljs-attribute">3</span>=D</span><br><span class="line">E string:  E   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=F   <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=G</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Half-step lands between <strong>finger 1 and 2</strong> on the A string (B♭ → C). The B♭ requires placing finger 1 <em>low</em> — closer to the nut than normal.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Where I got stuck:</em> &quot;low 1&quot; felt unnatural for weeks. The finger wants to land where it always does. Fix: physically mark a tape line for &quot;low 1&quot; position on the A string for one practice session. Once your finger learns the distance, peel the tape off.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-2-—-The-first-minor-scale-A-natural-minor"><a href="#Chapter-2-—-The-first-minor-scale-A-natural-minor" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 2 — The first minor scale: A natural minor"></a>Chapter 2 — The first minor scale: A natural minor</h1>
<p>A natural minor uses the <em>same notes as C major</em> but starts on A:</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-selector-tag">A</span>   <span class="hljs-selector-tag">B</span>   C   D   E   F   <span class="hljs-selector-tag">G</span>   <span class="hljs-selector-tag">A</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Fingering (one octave starting from open A):</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line">A string:  A   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=B   <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=C   <span class="hljs-attribute">3</span>=D</span><br><span class="line">E string:  E   <span class="hljs-attribute">1</span>=F   <span class="hljs-attribute">2</span>=G   (3 <span class="hljs-keyword">not</span> needed <span class="hljs-keyword">for</span> one octave <span class="hljs-keyword">to</span> A — use harmonic A)</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Or starting on the G string for low-octave practice: 1=A (low) ... but Grade 2 starts on open A.</p>
<h2 id="What-natural-minor-means"><a href="#What-natural-minor-means" class="headerlink" title="What &quot;natural minor&quot; means"></a>What &quot;natural minor&quot; means</h2>
<p>Three forms of minor exist:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Form</th>
<th>Pattern (intervals)</th>
<th>Where used</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Natural</td>
<td>W-H-W-W-H-W-W</td>
<td>Grade 2 introduction</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Harmonic</td>
<td>Raises 7th by half-step</td>
<td>Grade 3+</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Melodic</td>
<td>Raises 6th + 7th going up, naturals coming down</td>
<td>Grade 4+</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Grade 2 only requires natural minor. Memorise it now; the others build on it.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Trick:</em> A natural minor = the white notes from A to A. C major = the white notes from C to C. They share <strong>all</strong> notes — they're <strong>relative</strong>. Every major key has a relative minor a minor 3rd (3 semitones) below. Memorise this pairing now: it saves hours later.</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-3-—-Dotted-rhythms"><a href="#Chapter-3-—-Dotted-rhythms" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 3 — Dotted rhythms"></a>Chapter 3 — Dotted rhythms</h1>
<p>A dot after a note adds <strong>half its value</strong>:</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line">♩.  = ♩ + ♪  <span class="hljs-params">(dotted <span class="hljs-attr">crotchet</span> = crotchet + <span class="hljs-attr">quaver</span> = 1.5 beats)</span></span><br><span class="line">♪.  = ♪ + 𝅘𝅥𝅯  <span class="hljs-params">(dotted <span class="hljs-attr">quaver</span> = quaver + <span class="hljs-attr">semiquaver</span> = 0.75 beats)</span></span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>The Grade 2 staple is the <strong>dotted-quaver + semiquaver</strong> pair:</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line">♪. 𝅘𝅥𝅯  =  3/4 beat + 1/4 beat   (<span class="hljs-keyword">one</span> beat <span class="hljs-keyword">total</span>)</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>Count subdivisions:</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-number">1</span>-e-&amp;-<span class="hljs-selector-tag">a</span>       (<span class="hljs-number">4</span> semiquavers = <span class="hljs-number">1</span> beat)</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-selector-attr">[long  ]</span><span class="hljs-selector-attr">[s]</span>   (dotted-<span class="hljs-selector-tag">q</span> on <span class="hljs-string">&quot;1-e-&amp;&quot;</span>, semiq on <span class="hljs-string">&quot;a&quot;</span>)</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Trick:</em> I subdivided in semiquavers aloud for every dotted pair for 2 weeks: &quot;ONE-e-and-AH, ONE-e-and-AH&quot;. Sounds ridiculous, locks the ratio in. Once it's automatic, drop the speaking.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="Bow-distribution-for-dotted-rhythms"><a href="#Bow-distribution-for-dotted-rhythms" class="headerlink" title="Bow distribution for dotted rhythms"></a>Bow distribution for dotted rhythms</h2>
<p>The long note gets <strong>more bow</strong>, the short note gets <strong>less</strong>. Roughly 3:1 ratio matching the duration:</p>
<pre><code><figure class="highlight plaintext"><table><tr><td class="gutter"><pre><span class="line">1</span><br><span class="line">2</span><br><span class="line">3</span></pre></td><td class="code"><pre><code class="plaintext"><span class="line"><span class="hljs-attribute">dotted</span>-quaver: <span class="hljs-number">3</span> inches of bow</span><br><span class="line"><span class="hljs-attribute">semiquaver</span>:    <span class="hljs-number">1</span> inch of bow</span><br><span class="line"> </span></code></pre></td></tr></table></figure></code></pre>
<p>If you give them equal bow, the short note sounds <em>louder</em> (more bow speed in less time). Wrong feel.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-4-—-Longer-slurs-and-bow-planning"><a href="#Chapter-4-—-Longer-slurs-and-bow-planning" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 4 — Longer slurs and bow planning"></a>Chapter 4 — Longer slurs and bow planning</h1>
<p>Grade 1 slurs were 2–4 notes. Grade 2 introduces 6, 8, even a full scale (8 notes) on one bow.</p>
<h2 id="The-bow-budget-rule"><a href="#The-bow-budget-rule" class="headerlink" title="The bow-budget rule"></a>The bow-budget rule</h2>
<p>Total bow length = ~25 inches (varies by player + bow).<br>
For 8 notes in one bow: 25 ÷ 8 ≈ 3 inches per note.</p>
<p>You <strong>cannot</strong> play loud + slow + long-slurred. Pick two. Grade 2 long slurs are usually <em>mezzo-piano</em> and steady — the bow conserves itself.</p>
<h2 id="Slur-with-string-crossing"><a href="#Slur-with-string-crossing" class="headerlink" title="Slur with string crossing"></a>Slur with string crossing</h2>
<p>Example: slur &quot;open A → 1st finger B → open E&quot; in one bow. Two string changes inside one stroke. The right wrist + elbow tilt to roll between strings without breaking the sound.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Where I got stuck:</em> every slur with a string crossing scratched on the crossing point. Fix: slow practice with the metronome at 40 bpm, paying attention to the <em>angle of the bow hair</em> at the crossing. The bow should tilt gradually, not snap. Drill: 5 minutes a day for 2 weeks → solved.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="Practice-progression"><a href="#Practice-progression" class="headerlink" title="Practice progression"></a>Practice progression</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>2-note slur</strong>, same string.</li>
<li><strong>2-note slur, crossing strings</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>4-note slur</strong>, same string.</li>
<li><strong>4-note slur, crossing strings</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>8-note slur</strong> = full scale.</li>
</ol>
<p>Don't jump steps. Each stage takes about a week.</p>

<h2 id="Listening-assignment"><a href="#Listening-assignment" class="headerlink" title="Listening assignment"></a>Listening assignment</h2>
<p>Watch a recording of Itzhak Perlman or Hilary Hahn playing <em>Bach Partita No. 2 — Allemande</em> (don't try to play it; just listen). Notice how each note has identical bow weight despite enormous variety in speed. That's the Grade 2 goal: even tone across changes.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-7-—-Intonation-upgrade-cross-string-checking"><a href="#Chapter-7-—-Intonation-upgrade-cross-string-checking" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 7 — Intonation upgrade: cross-string checking"></a>Chapter 7 — Intonation upgrade: cross-string checking</h1>
<p>Grade 1 used drones on open strings. Grade 2 adds <strong>two-string checks</strong> at the same pitch:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Pitch</th>
<th>Way 1</th>
<th>Way 2</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>A4</td>
<td>Open A</td>
<td>3rd finger on D</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>E5</td>
<td>Open E</td>
<td>3rd finger on A</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>D5</td>
<td>3rd finger on A</td>
<td>(also = open string interval check)</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>If the two ways give the <strong>same pitch</strong>, you're in tune. If they fight, one finger is off.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Trick:</em> end every scale with a check-note. Play G major scale up to G5, then <em>immediately</em> play open G and the G5 together as a double-stop. If the octave rings, your top note is true. If it beats, adjust.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="Tape-removal"><a href="#Tape-removal" class="headerlink" title="Tape removal"></a>Tape removal</h2>
<p>If you used tape on the fingerboard in Grade 1, remove it by Grade 2 exam. The eyes-on-fingerboard habit costs you sight-reading speed.</p>
<hr>
<h1 id="Chapter-8-—-Aural-Listening-Skills"><a href="#Chapter-8-—-Aural-Listening-Skills" class="headerlink" title="Chapter 8 — Aural / Listening Skills"></a>Chapter 8 — Aural / Listening Skills</h1>
<p>ABRSM Grade 2 aural adds:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Clap back</strong> rhythm in 6/8 (new at Grade 2).</li>
<li><strong>Sing back</strong> a 4-bar phrase.</li>
<li><strong>Identify</strong> if a melody is in major or minor.</li>
<li><strong>Identify</strong> the time signature (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, <strong>or 6/8</strong>).</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="Major-vs-minor-identification"><a href="#Major-vs-minor-identification" class="headerlink" title="Major vs minor identification"></a>Major vs minor identification</h2>
<p>Major = &quot;bright, happy&quot;. Minor = &quot;sad, darker&quot;. The most reliable test: the <strong>third</strong>. Major 3rd (C–E) = 4 semitones. Minor 3rd (C–E♭) = 3 semitones.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><em>Trick:</em> sing &quot;Happy Birthday&quot; (major) immediately, then &quot;Hey Jude&quot; verse (also major) — internalise the <em>colour</em>. Then sing &quot;Greensleeves&quot; (Dorian, sounds minor) or &quot;House of the Rising Sun&quot; (minor) — feel the <em>gloom</em>. After a week of A/B comparisons, you'll identify them in 2 seconds.</p>
</blockquote>
