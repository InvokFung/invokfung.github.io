Ink

My name, printed in characters on a fluid. Stir it with the cursor and it turns to smoke. Leave it alone and it settles back into the letters.

Where it came from

  • The cursor fluid people call a "splash cursor" comes from Pavel Dobryakov's WebGL Fluid Simulation (about 17k stars, MIT).
  • Drawing it in characters follows the 2026 wave of ASCII renderers: Pretext's fluid smoke demo, play.core, and Alex Harri's "ASCII characters are not pixels".

How it works

  • Jos Stam's stable fluids on the CPU, one grid cell per character: advection, a 16-step pressure solve, and vorticity confinement for the curls.
  • The name is rasterised at the grid's size; every frame the ink relaxes a little toward it, so it heals.
  • One fragment shader draws every character from a glyph atlas: denser ink picks a heavier glyph, faster ink turns amber.
