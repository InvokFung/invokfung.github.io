round 4 · whole site

One continuous move through a paper landscape, drawn on one canvas so it stays light. 2026 is a camp, not the top: the trail goes on into cloud, to a higher summit that clears at the end.

  • Hello. The name between ridges at first light. The ridges tear at their valleys and part like a curtain.
  • How I got here. A flag a year up the trail to the camp, each with a line on what changed.
  • Selected work. The camera flies into the cloud by the camp and comes out at the work: seven live panels, the front four with two real numbers each.
  • Craft. A crane along the camp at golden hour. The panels lie down in a wave and six live specimens stand up.
  • Contact. Clouds rise and part on the summit at last light, with the trail up it and "Say hello."

Candidate for the live site. Replaces round 3. The browser's own scroll, eased, with no library. It drops its own quality on slow machines and works without WebGL. It replaces nothing until Alan says so.

Flow

round 2 · whole site

Who I am, the work running live, how I got here, what the page is made of, and how to reach me, in one scroll.

  • Hello: Badge + Reflow. The badge hangs in front of the copy, and the copy re-flows around it every frame. Drag the card in and the text parts.
  • Work: the live figures + Assemble. One stage runs each project's figure; between projects the figure breaks into points and rebuilds as the next one. FDE work first.
  • How I got here: Morph. Titles turn into each other as you scroll, and the letters they share light up as they carry over.
  • Craft: Pressure. "Hand-built, down to the type" leans toward your cursor, over a paragraph that names every effect and links here.
  • Contact: Badge again. The same card comes back, turned over to the email and GitHub.
  • Sign-off: Ink. The name as a fluid you can stir. It comes back.

Candidate for the live site. It replaces nothing until Alan says so. Content comes from assets/portfolio/data.js, the same file the live pages read.

Reflow

1 · hero

The work, as a wall of text that parts around two giant initials. Drag a letter through it and the lines re-flow every frame.

Went viral as
Pretext by Cheng Lou, March 2026: 49.6k stars by October, and about 19M views on X in its first two days (VentureBeat).
Built with
Pretext 1.0 (MIT, vendored) and 300 lines of mine. About 1 ms per frame for 42 lines, with no DOM measuring.
Would go
The intro's first screen.

Ship it. It is fresh, few portfolios use it yet, and the motion is made of real content. Keep the copy short, because lines that run across a letter read in an unusual order.

Badge

2 · contact

An ID card on a lanyard. Grab it, throw it, and tap it to flip over to the email and GitHub.

Went viral as
Lanyard from React Bits (48.8k stars, the #2 rising JS project of 2025), with foil after pokemon-cards-css.
Built with
No libraries: a verlet chain for the strap and card, CSS 3D for the tilt. The card stays real, selectable HTML.
Would go
The end of the intro, replacing the contact() ring.

Ship it. The most fun per line of code, and it still reads professional. The original is a known component, so the foil in the seven project colours and the card copy are what make it Alan's.

Morph

3 · career line

Each job title turns into the next while the year rolls. Three styles to compare: glide, decode and liquid.

Went viral as
Split and scramble text, the biggest category in React Bits. GSAP made SplitText and ScrambleText free in April 2025. Liquid is Magic UI's Morphing Text, and the year rolls like NumberFlow (7.7k stars).
Built with
No libraries, about 200 lines. Glide matches letters the two titles share, so "Engineer" never leaves the screen.
Would go
The career line, 2020 to 2026.

Ship glide. The quietest of the six and the easiest to ship. On its own it won't shock anyone, but as the career line it tells the whole path in one glance.

Assemble

4 · photo hero

42,000 particles that form the name, then a Layerline gear, a line of copy and the Tracewise map. Drop a photo on the page and they become it.

Went viral as
Image to particles, best known from Bruno Imbrizi's Interactive Particles. Igloo Inc, Awwwards Site of the Year 2024, morphs one particle cloud per link.
Built with
Plain WebGL, no three.js. Every particle flies in the vertex shader, so it is one draw call.
Would go
A hero that becomes Alan's photo, or the move between projects.

Needs a photo. The biggest wow on a good GPU and the effect asked for by name. Save a headshot as lab/assemble/portrait.jpg to see it. Dashboards turn to mush as dots, so keep it to portraits and objects.

Pressure

5 · type hero

The name set wall to wall in a variable font. Letters near the cursor swell wide and heavy; left alone, a slow wave runs through them.

Went viral as
TextPressure and VariableProximity from React Bits, cloned across shadcn, Framer and 21st.dev.
Built with
No libraries. Roboto Flex's width, weight and optical size axes, set per letter each frame.
Would go
A typographic hero, or the name on /explore/.

Keep in reserve. Instantly striking and cheap, but common now, so it reads as taste rather than invention.

Ink

6 · 404 or footer

The name printed in characters on a fluid. Stir it into smoke and it settles back into the letters.

Went viral as
WebGL Fluid Simulation by Pavel Dobryakov (16.6k stars), plus the 2026 ASCII wave: Pretext's smoke demo and Alex Harri's "ASCII characters are not pixels" (1,353 points on Hacker News).
Built with
Stable fluids on the CPU, one cell per character, and one shader that draws all 10,920 characters.
Would go
A 404 page, or behind the footer.

Not for the hero. The most original mix here, but it sits close to the dot-matrix look the site already has, and the name only reads when it is still.

Looked at, not built

Each of these went viral too. Here is why none of them made the six.

  • A desktop OS as the portfolioMitch Ivin's Windows XP site got 1,032 points on Hacker News in 2025; ryOS and Henry Heffernan's 3D desk did well too. It is a whole-site concept rather than an effect, and it has been cloned many times over.
  • Liquid glassrdev/liquid-glass-react reached 6.4k stars from the day after WWDC 2025. The refraction only works in Chromium; Safari and Firefox show a flat panel.
  • Shader gradients and aurora backgroundsReact Bits' fastest-growing category. They look good and say nothing about the work.
  • Live state shared between visitorsJosh Comeau's rainbow and Bruno Simon's whispers. They need a server, and GitHub Pages can't run one.
  • Dithering shadersFour Codrops tutorials in 2025 and 2026. Too close to the dot-matrix figures already on the intro.
  • A drivable 3D worldBruno Simon's 2025 relaunch was Awwwards Site of the Month in January 2026. The car and the walkable city were already ruled out.

How this lab is kept

  • Every experiment has its own folder under /lab/: the page, the component it is built from, and its notes (press N on the page).
  • lab/README.md logs each one with its date, source, license, files and verdict, so nothing tried gets lost.
  • Nothing on the live site links here, and every page carries noindex.
  • The only third-party code is Pretext (MIT), vendored unmodified in lab/vendor/ with its license. Everything else is written from scratch.
